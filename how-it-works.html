What Prismal Budget Is

Prismal Budget (prismalbudget.org) is a web budgeting app built around a four-week calendar. Every day on the calendar lists that day's entries (costs and gains), and the budget adds them up into In Bank: how much money will be in your budget's bank account at the end of each day. Recurring entries like paychecks and bills fill themselves in, and so do entries from spreadsheets you connect (like a list of bills you already keep in Google Sheets or Excel), so you can see days where your balance would go negative before they happen, and the lowest your balance gets in the 28 days after the calendar. Prismal Budget is for people 18 and older.

This page explains every page, feature, rule, and data format in detail, in plain words, with exact formats and examples, as the website works now: what each page shows, top to bottom; what every button does; the limits; what each feature needs; and how the budget is worked out and stored. It's written so a person, a search engine, or an AI assistant can answer a question about Prismal Budget from this page alone. Each page, top to bottom, Limits at a glance, What each feature needs, and Common questions are good places to start.

Things Every Page Shares

  • Header: the logo and the page's name (on Home and its tabs, "Prismal Budget" with its tagline, Track • Plan • Learn) at the left, and the page's buttons at the right, each an icon over its name on a glass tile (Back, Home, Menu, Settings, Logout, depending on the page; Logout only shows while signed in, so the public pages don't offer it to visitors). Home, Recurring Entries, History, and Other Accounts have Settings, Menu, and Logout; the menu's pages (Spreadsheets, Tracker, Paycheck Calculator, Change Logs) have Back, which goes back to the page that opened them (the menu, unless another page did); other pages, like Settings, How It Works, and the Privacy Policy, have Back, which goes back to the page before. Home's button in a header is a green house and Back's a red arrow, each with a gradient like Menu's (the tabs' icons and Home's Undo keep their own).
  • The budget's four pages as tabs: Home, Recurring Entries, History, and Other Accounts share one layout: under the header, the four pages as tabs at the left (each an icon and its name; the one showing glows, with a small neon dot at its left, and tapping it does nothing), beside a glass card with the page's own buttons (on the other three, the page's icon and title over them), then the page itself in a glass card. On the narrowest phones (under 360 pixels wide), the tabs sit two by two above that card instead, and under 340 pixels the header leaves out the tagline. Going from one of these pages to another by its tab, the header and the tabs stay put (a page scrolled down glides back to its top first) while the rest of the page slides down and away, and the next page's slides up into place under them. Meanwhile the tab's bright edge (and its dot) slides up or down from the page that was showing to the tapped tab, and the light inside the tab fades from one into the other in the same time, so the tapped tab already looks just like it will on the next page.
  • The look: glass cards, tabs, and buttons (dark navy, see-through enough for the starfield, with a faint blue edge) over the starfield, light text, and neon blue and violet accents. Things that glow (the page that's showing, today on the calendar, Quick Entry) have a blue-violet radial gradient from their center, a bright edge, and a glow around them.
  • Buttons, the same everywhere (Home's are the model): a button that can be tapped is bright glass with a light edge, and under the pointer it grows a little while its edge brightens and glows blue-violet. The recommended one in a form or card (Save, Add Entry, Add Account, Set It Up, Sign In, Download My Data) glows like Home's Quick Entry button: a blue-violet radial gradient from its center over a blue-to-violet fill, with a bright edge. One that's not recommended (Delete, Sign Out Everywhere, Delete Account) is darker, with muted red words, and one that can't be used right now (like Undo with nothing to undo) is darker and dimmed. Only a chosen one is red-orange: the picked refraction type, account, Cost or Gain, $ or %, Set to or Add to it, an open ? and an open date picker's button get a red-orange diagonal gradient border, a red glow, and a red radial gradient from their center.
  • Boxes to type in have a thin edge that brightens a little under the pointer; the one being typed in gets a bright blue-violet edge. Nothing about them grows or glows.
  • Colors of entries: wherever an entry shows (a day, Upcoming, an account's Entries), a cost's words are red, a gain's green, and a $0.00 entry's a darker grey; a placeholder's are yellow whatever its amount. The lines the budget writes itself (In Bank, Gains, Costs, and the 28-day lines) stay the day's light grey: a day whose In Bank is below $0 turns red itself, and its In Bank line has the red ⛔️ triangle.
  • Pill switches: on/off choices (agreeing to the Privacy Policy & Terms of Use, Turn on passkey sign-in, a tracker row's Count ⭕️ Transfers, an Other Account's Add interest each month, each Search title's Exact, each connected spreadsheet's On/Off on the Spreadsheets page, Settings' Show balances on Quick Entry icons, and an entry's Placeholder, whose word is yellow) are pill-shaped switches: off is a dark pill with its knob at the left, on fills with the red-orange gradient with its knob at the right. Tapping the words flips them too. Checkboxes that pick rows from a list (search results, and the rows to delete in Edit) stay checkboxes.
  • Animated starfield background and page transitions: pages slide in and out instead of flashing, as if they sat side by side: a page slides out one way, and the next one slides in the same way. Going to the Menu or one of its pages slides left, and going Home or back from them slides right. Quick Entry and the Privacy Policy slide right to open and left to leave. Settings slides down into view, and back up to leave. Signing in slides left, and signing out (any way: Logout, Sign Out Everywhere, or after 15 idle minutes) slides right. Between the budget's four pages, only the part under the tabs slides (down and away, and the next page's up into place). Anything else, like How It Works, slides down and away, and the next page up into view, and so does a page opened from somewhere else or reloaded. A page the browser brings back just as it was left (with Back or Forward) slides back the way it went. While a page slides out, the next one starts loading, and it opens as soon as the last one is out of sight, so the pause between them is short. While a page slides in, it stays at its top, so it never lands scrolled down (a link to a spot on a page, like #search, scrolls there once it's in place), and a page sliding up or down moves only what's on the screen, so the rest of a long page never runs past. The starfield's stars carry over from page to page, and react to the pointer, taps, and (secretly) the keyboard: see The starfield background.
  • Sounds: soft crystal sounds, made for the site. A single twinkling ding for anything that says yes (like Save, Add, Sign In, or a switch turned on), a quick crystal shatter for anything that says no (like Delete, Cancel, ×, Sign Out, or a switch turned off), a smaller, gentler twinkle for any other button, a soft shimmer that's easy to miss for anything else that's tapped (a day, a box, an entry), and a twinkling crystal rolling across other crystals, starting and ending softly, as a page slides out. On the signed-in pages there's music too: a quiet bed of soft, far-off twinkles that runs the whole time (one every 1.4 to 3.2 seconds, each about 2.6 seconds long, so they blend, played lower and softer), and now and then (every 7 to 18 seconds) a clearer few. Browsers only let a page play sound after a tap, so nothing plays before the first one. On an iPhone or iPad the sounds mix with your own audio instead of stopping it (and the silent switch silences them). Something that takes the speaker (like a call) pauses them, and the next tap plays them again. Settings turns music and sound effects on or off for each device.
  • Footer: trademark note, contact, Privacy Policy & Terms of Use, and How Prismal Budget Works.
  • Help button (the word Help, in a small pill) at the bottom of the page, just under the footer's line, on Home, Other Accounts, Recurring Entries, Tracker, History, Paycheck Calculator, Spreadsheets, Settings (its Help covers the account's email and birth date), and Quick Entry (adding it to the home screen, opening it from the Lock Screen, and In Bank on a watch, with steps for each kind of phone). It opens that page's help in a box that floats over a dimmed page; ×, Escape, or tapping outside closes it.
  • Floating boxes: boxes like New Entry, an entry's options, and the panels for recurring entries, tracker rows, and accounts float over the page, so nothing under them moves. So do Search (with its results under its form) and Help. Each one zooms out of the button (or entry) that opened it and back into it when it closes, and has a deep violet to navy glass background. One can open over another (a connected spreadsheet's ➕ boxes open over its panel, and an entry's options over an account's Entries): the one under it waits, dimmed, and comes back when the top one closes; Escape closes only the top one. Leaving the page closes them right away (and zooms a zoomed day back out), so the browser's Back button never brings a page back with one still open. A box placed by what opened it (an entry's options, a goal) is placed again whenever its size changes, so it never runs past the bottom of the screen.
  • Floating boxes are part of the page: a floating box (and the date picker, Search, and Help) opens on the part of the page that's on the screen, at its normal size, even when the page is pinch-zoomed in. From then on it stays put on the page: scroll the page and it scrolls with it, and pinch-zoom the page and it zooms with it, so you can zoom in on a box, or scroll to the rest of a tall one, the same as anything else on the page. A zoomed day works the same way. Once every box is closed, the next one opens wherever the screen is then.
  • Notes stay in sight: notes (like "Undone: …"), the countdown before an idle sign-out, and the version number in the bottom left corner sit on a layer that always follows the screen, at their own size, over everything else, however the page is scrolled or zoomed.
  • Signed out, a page that needs signing in goes to the login page. The site's own address shows the front page instead.
  • Without JavaScript, pages other than this one and the front page only ask you to turn it on, since nothing works without it. The same goes for a browser that blocks site data (cookies and site data turned off): signing in is kept there, so the login and budget pages say how to allow it, while the front page, this page, the Privacy Policy, Quick Entry, and Not Found still work.
  • Less motion: when the device asks for reduced motion, pages appear without sliding, links go right away, and the starfield doesn't run (see Reduced motion).
  • Notes (like "Undone: …") show at the bottom of the screen for a few seconds; two at once stack instead of covering each other.
  • Missing images show their words instead of a broken-image icon, so a button still says what it does.
  • Sprites: the special emoji show as the site's own small images on every page, the same on every device: the entry types as circles (❗️ Regular red, ✖️ Hidden purple, ⭕️ Transfer green), ✔️ Recurring an orange check and ➕ (a connected spreadsheet's) a yellow plus, and the lines the budget writes itself as triangles (✅ In Bank green and ❇️ Gains blue, pointing up; ⛔️ In Bank below $0 red and ✴️ Costs orange, pointing down; see Sprites). The text underneath keeps the emoji.
  • Other languages: the site is written in English, and the browser's own translation (in Chrome, Edge, Safari, or Firefox) shows it in another language. The person's entries, names, amounts, and dates are marked to stay just as they were typed (translate="no"), and the site's own words translate. Settings says how to turn it on.
  • Dates show, and are typed, in the device's date format (like 10/05/2026, 05/10/2026, or 2026-10-05), or the one picked in Settings. They're always stored the same way (see Dates, time zones, and formats).
  • Picking a date: every date box (New Entry, on Home and the Quick Entry page; an entry's Move to; Recurring Entries' Start and End Dates; Search's From and To; and the birth date on Create Account and in Settings) has a calendar button beside it (a glass button with a small calendar drawn in thin light grey lines, like the site's other small marks), instead of the device's own date picker. It opens a small calendar under the box, in the look of Home's calendar: a square glass day for each date with one big number, the weekdays over them, and at the top the month and the year side by side, each a box to type in (a list of months, or of years, shows under it while it's picked, narrowing as it's typed; it scrolls on its own with a finger or the mouse wheel, never moving the page, and Enter or a tap on one goes there while the calendar stays open), with ‹ and › for the month before and after. Picked with a finger, the box lets go, so a phone's keyboard goes away. Today glows, the date that's typed has the red-orange edge, the days of the months before and after are faint, and days that can't be picked (like a birth date in the future, or a day outside History when moving a History entry) are dimmed. Today (when today can be picked) and Close are at the bottom. A tap on a day fills in the box and closes it; Escape or a tap outside closes it too. On a keyboard, the arrows move a day or a week, Page Up and Page Down a month, Home and End to the week's ends, and Enter or Space picks.
  • Links within the site slide the page out first. Links to a spot on the same page (like this page's Contents) just scroll there, and links opened in a new tab (Ctrl or ⌘ click, or the middle button) open there as usual.
  • Idle sign-out warning: on signed-in pages, the last minute before the 15-minute idle sign-out shows a notice counting down; any tap, key, or scroll keeps you signed in. A page left in the background just signs out when it's opened again, and the login page then says why.
  • Saving safely: every change saves right away. If the server can't be reached, the change is kept on the page and sent again when the connection comes back (and every 30 seconds until then), and leaving the page before then asks first. If the budget was saved on another device or tab since the page loaded, the page reloads with the newest budget instead of saving over it (see How the website is built).
  • Live updates: two devices (or two people sharing a budget) with it open see each other's changes within about 5 seconds, with no reload: a new entry, a changed recurring entry, a tracker row, an account, a quick entry added from a Quick Entry icon, anything. While a page is on the screen, it asks the server every 5 seconds whether the budget changed (and whether quick entries were added, changed, or taken back), and when one did, it loads the budget again in place and redraws (a zoomed day stays zoomed, and open Search results are found again). It waits while something here isn't saved yet, while a box (panel) is open, while a page is in Edit, while a row is being dragged, or while a box the page would draw again is being typed in (like the Paycheck Calculator's, until it's calculated), then catches up. A page off the screen doesn't ask; it checks the moment it's back.

Login

  • Create an account: a username (5 to 30 characters, and any characters you can see work: letters in any language, numbers, symbols, emoji, and spaces), an email address, your birth date (typed, or picked with the calendar button; Prismal Budget is for people 18 and older: under 18, no account is made, and the server refuses it too), a password of at least 8 characters (any characters, typed twice), and agreeing to the Privacy Policy & Terms of Use. Usernames and passwords follow the rules in Accounts, sign-in, and security; in short, "Sam" and "sam" are the same username, and a password known from data breaches is refused.
  • Signing in: the username (in any capitals) or the account's email (or one of its confirmed Other Emails, see Settings), and the password. After too many wrong passwords, signing in pauses for a while.
  • Verifying the email: a new account's email gets a link to verify it (it works for 24 hours), and "Account created!" says so. The link opens the login page, signed in or not, which checks it and says whether the email is verified, with Open Spreadsheets (signed in on that device) or Sign In next. Connecting spreadsheets and adding Other Emails need a verified email; nothing else does (see Accounts, sign-in, and security). The link that confirms one of the account's Other Emails (Settings, under Email) opens the login page the same way ("Confirm Your Email").
  • Forgot Password: enter the account's email (or one of its confirmed Other Emails) and a one-time reset link is emailed to that address (it works for an hour).
  • Reset form: the emailed link opens the login page to choose a new password, which signs out every device and turns passkey sign-in off.
  • Sign In With A Passkey: signs in with Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, a fingerprint, or the device's PIN, with no username or password to type (see Passkeys). Browsers with passkey autofill also list the site's passkeys under the username box.

2. The Starfield Background (And Its Hidden Controls)

Every page of Prismal Budget has a moving starfield behind it: up to 400 small stars (fewer on smaller screens) that drift, twinkle (each one fades out and comes back, now and then with a white spark), and are joined by faint lines, like constellations, when they're near each other. It's only decoration: it never touches the budget, and nothing about it leaves the device.

Moving The Stars With A Pointer Or A Finger

  • Moving the mouse, or dragging a finger, gives the stars near it energy: they're pulled toward it and pushed away from it (harder the faster it moves), so they swirl around it, and a ring follows it.
  • Tapping or clicking anywhere pokes the stars: the ones nearby burst away from that spot, and a ring spreads out from it.
  • A star that drifts off one edge comes back on the other, except right around a moving pointer, where the stars bounce off the edges instead.

Hidden Keyboard Controls

On a computer (or a tablet or phone with a keyboard), these keys move the stars, on any page. Holding a key down keeps it going. They work even while typing in a box (typing a title nudges the stars too), and they never change anything in the budget.

Q E Z XPush them diagonally: up and left, up and right, down and left, or down and right.
Y U I, H J K, B N MMagnets on a 3 × 3 grid of the screen, laid out like the keys: Y is the top left, J the middle, and M the bottom right. The stars are pulled toward that spot and orbit around it.

The Not Found Page's Starfield Controls

The Not Found (404) page, which shows at any address that doesn't exist, hides a control panel far below its first screen, so the page looks empty until it's scrolled down:

  • Controller: a box for typing the keys above (it empties itself as you go).
  • Readouts: live numbers for how long each frame takes to work out (Misc), the pointer's speed, and the ring and poke timers.
  • Attract (Strength, Radius, and Scale), Clamp, Repel (Strength, Radius, and Scale), and Poke: sliders, each with a number box and − and + buttons (hold one to keep stepping), for how the pointer moves the stars. Attract sets how hard and how far the pointer pulls them in, and Scale how sharply that fades with distance; Repel is the same for pushing them away; Clamp is the stars' top speed; and Poke is how strong a tap's burst is. The settings are saved on that device and used on every page.

Across Pages, And Out Of The Way

  • The same stars carry over from page to page: when a page is left, the stars, where the pointer was, and the slider settings are saved in the browser on that device (constellationStars and constellationMeta), and the next page picks them up, fitted to its screen. Clearing the browser's site data starts a new starfield.
  • It runs in a background thread (a Web Worker drawing on an OffscreenCanvas; a browser without one runs it on the page instead), pauses while the page is hidden or sliding to another page, and shortens its lines on a slow device to stay smooth.
  • It shows right away when a page opens: the background picture is part of the site's stylesheet, so it's there in the page's first frame, and the starfield starts loading (its engine and its star image) the moment a page starts, so the stars are drawn as soon as the page shows instead of blinking in a moment later.

3. Entries And Refraction Types

An entry is one line of text: an emoji (its "sprite"), an amount, and a title, separated by spaces.

❗️ -$6.50 Coffee
✔️ $1200.00 Paycheck
⭕️ -$100.00 Savings

Costs are negative and gains are positive. Amounts are written like -$1234.56 (no thousands commas). An amount box is typed like a cash register: the digits fill in from the cents, so the decimal point never needs typing (5 is $0.05, 1234 is $12.34, and each digit after moves it along), and phones show their number pad for it. Its Cost and Gain buttons (or Negative and Positive, for a balance) pick its sign, and typing a − or + flips them. A box whose amount would be $0.00 starts blank, with 0.00 as its hint, and a blank one is $0.00. Tapping into a box with an amount in it selects the amount, so typing replaces it. A pasted amount is read as it is: a $ and thousands commas are fine, and a comma before the last one or two digits is a decimal point, the way phones in many countries write it (4,50 is $4.50, and 1.234,50 is $1234.50). An amount can be up to $1,000,000,000, cost or gain.

Titles are compared without regard to capital letters, extra spaces, or punctuation, so "Coffee", "coffee", and "COFFEE!" are the same title. Titles can be in any language: letters and numbers in any script count, so "Кофе" and "Чай" are two titles, and full-width letters match their plain forms. A title with no letters or numbers at all (like ☕) keeps its symbols, so ☕ and 🍔 are different titles. New titles get each word's first letter capitalized, in any language ("école" becomes "École"). Other Accounts' names follow the same rules. Titles can be up to 100 characters, and account names up to 60.

For a Hidden or Transfer entry for an Other Account, a Transfer changes the account by the opposite of its amount and a Hidden entry by its own amount. In New Entry (on Home and the Quick Entry page) and the recurring entry panel, a small ? next to Refraction Type shows what each type does.

Recurring, Unique, And Temporary Entries

  • Recurring entries (✔️, ✔️✖️, ✔️⭕️) start with a checkmark. They come from the Recurring Entries page and are written onto today and every later day they land on.
  • Unique entries (❗️, ✖️, ⭕️) are ones you add or change yourself.
  • Connected spreadsheets' entries (➕❗️, ➕✖️, ➕⭕️) start with a yellow plus. They come from spreadsheets connected on the Spreadsheets page and are written onto today and every later day of the calendar they're dated on (see Connected spreadsheets). Their temps (❗️➕, ✖️➕, ⭕️➕, the plus second) work just like recurring entries' temps.
  • Temporary recurring entries ("temps": ❗️✔️, ✖️✔️, ⭕️✔️) are unique entries made from a recurring entry, with the checkmark second. A recurring entry becomes a temp when its day passes, or when it's changed on the calendar for that day only. A temp keeps the recurring entry from being written again on its day (for its refraction type). Moving or deleting a recurring entry's day leaves a $0.00 temp behind as a "blocker" for the same reason. Once the recurring entry can't land on a temp's day anymore (its End Date moved before it, or it was deleted), the temp from today on becomes a plain unique entry, and a blocker goes away.

One Entry Per Title And Type, Per Day

A day has at most one entry for each title, Other Account, and refraction type, so a title can appear at most three times on a day: once as Regular, once as Hidden, and once as Transfer. An Other Account after a title makes it a different title here, so "Payday → Savings" and "Payday → HSA" are two entries. Whether the amount is a % or dollars doesn't make another entry: a % entry and a dollar entry with the same title, account, and type on one day are one entry (one plain amount, what the two come to), on the calendar, in History, and in Upcoming alike. When New Entry's title, type (Auto matches any), Other Account, and day match an entry already there (a recurring or spreadsheet entry landing there too), the form shows that entry and asks: Add to it (the two amounts are added together; it's picked to start) or Set to this (the entry becomes the new amount). Either way, a recurring or spreadsheet entry becomes a temp for that day (like ❗️✔️), the same as changing it on the calendar. The Quick Entry icon always adds to it. Recurring entries and spreadsheets' entries landing on a day with a matching unique entry are added into it too (it becomes a temp). A spreadsheet's entry with the same title and type as a recurring entry landing that day is left out (the Spreadsheets page says so). If matching entries ever end up on the same day another way (like renaming an account to a title already on that day), they're combined the next time the budget refreshes (Upcoming's too).

Changing An Entry

Tapping an entry on a zoomed day (on Home or History, one of Upcoming's under the calendar, one of an Other Account's Entries, or a Search result) opens its options, a small box by the entry, which show its whole entry (however long its title), and offer, in this order: its Title, its refraction type and Other Account, Change the amount (Set to a new amount, where a blank box sets it to $0.00, or Add to it) with the amount box, Move to another day (it combines with a matching entry there), Placeholder (for a day from today on, see Placeholders), then Save, Delete, and Cancel. If the entry is for an Other Account, its options say so. Its title (its own, before any Other Account) can change on any entry on a day before today, History included, and from today on on any entry that doesn't land by itself: one from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet (or a temp of one, like ❗️✔️) keeps its title from today on, since it's changed on the Recurring Entries or Spreadsheets page, and its options say so. The lines the budget writes itself (In Bank, Gains, Costs) never have a title to change. A new title keeps the entry as it is (its type, mark, %, and placeholder), unless an entry with the new title and type is already on that day: then the two become one, their amounts added (a placeholder only if both were, and a plain amount). Its Change Log is "✏️ Rename Entry", and Undo takes it back. Its refraction type and Other Account can change too: it then comes off its day the way Delete takes it off (one that landed by itself leaves a $0.00 temp), and goes on as the new one, adding into an entry already on that day with the same title and type (a day has one of each). That's one step for Undo. On a History day, these change the entry without changing In Bank or any Other Account's balance (see Changing a History entry).

Tapping the day's In Bank on a zoomed day asks what the bank says for that day (Negative when the account is below zero). The difference goes on it as a ⭕️ Transfer titled Catchup, with no Other Account (added into a Catchup Transfer already there), so that day's In Bank matches and the days after it follow. Being a Transfer, it fixes In Bank without counting as a cost or gain: the day's Gains and Costs leave it out, and the tracker only counts it in its Transfers row (and rows set to count transfers).

An entry can go on any day the budget has, from History's oldest day on (for a new budget with no History yet, from the calendar's first day, this week's Sunday), and any later day goes to Upcoming. A day before that has nowhere to hold an entry, so New Entry, Move to, and the Quick Entry icon say so instead of adding it.

Placeholders

A placeholder stands in for an entry you expect but don't know yet, like an estimate for groceries or a bill whose amount isn't out yet. The Placeholder pill switch makes one: in New Entry (on Home and the Quick Entry page), an entry's options, the recurring entry panel (every day it lands on gets one), Quick Entry's prisms, and a connected spreadsheet's panel (every entry it adds). In New Entry and an entry's options, it shows only for a day from today on (the day picked, or Move to's), and in the recurring entry panel, only while the entry can still land (no End Date, or one from today on).

  • It counts like any entry of its refraction type: In Bank, the day's Gains and Costs, the 28 days after the calendar, Lowest in bank after today, Other Accounts' balances (and In 6 Months), the tracker, Search, Jeremy, reminders, and the Quick Entry icon's balances.
  • Yellow: a placeholder's words are yellow wherever it shows, a cost or a gain: on a day, in Upcoming, Search's results, the Recurring Entries table (its whole row), an account's entries, Quick Entry's prisms and Waiting list, and a connected spreadsheet's count and preview.
  • Deleted once its day passes: the first time the budget opens on a new day, the daily update deletes every placeholder on a day before today, and works In Bank out again from that day on without it, so a placeholder is never left on a day that passed, or in History. A recurring entry's placeholder lands on each of its days and goes once that day passes, and so does a spreadsheet's. A waiting quick entry that's a placeholder whose day passed before the budget opened is left out, and a note says so. The daily update's Change Log says how many it deleted ("Placeholders Removed").
  • Two entries that become one: when a placeholder and an entry with the same title and type end up on one day (New Entry adding to it, Move to, the Quick Entry icon, or a recurring or spreadsheet entry landing on it), the one entry they become is a placeholder only if both were. Real money is never deleted with a placeholder: a real $30 added into a $100 placeholder makes a real $130 entry.
  • Changing one: an entry's options start with its switch as the entry has it, and turning it on or off changes just that (Undo says "made Coffee on 10/05 a placeholder"). Moved to a day before today, it becomes an ordinary entry (the switch hides for a day that passed). Changing one day of a placeholder recurring or spreadsheet entry makes a temp with its own switch, and deleting one leaves a $0.00 blocker that's a placeholder too, so it goes with its day. Turning a recurring entry's (or a spreadsheet's) switch on or off changes the days it lands on from today on, never the days changed for it.
  • History's entries have no switch: their days have passed.

% Amounts

An entry for an Other Account can be a percent of that account's balance instead of an amount, like moving 10% of a credit card's balance into it every payday. When Hidden or Transfer is picked and an Other Account is chosen, the amount box gets a $ / % switch at its left (in New Entry on Home and the Quick Entry page, an entry's options, the recurring entry panel, Quick Entry's prisms, and a connected spreadsheet's typed amount). With %, the box takes the percent as it reads (3 or 12.5, up to 1000), and Cost or Gain picks its sign, like any amount.

  • What it comes to: that percent of what's in the account as its day starts (its balance at the end of the day before, before that day's entries and interest), to the cent. The day shows that amount with the percent after it: ⭕️ -$45.00 (10%) Card → Visa. A balance below $0 counts by its size (10% of a −$450.00 card is $45.00), and Cost or Gain says which way it goes.
  • Worked out again with every change, from today on (on the calendar, in Upcoming, and through six months ahead), one day after another, so each % entry counts in the balance the next one starts from, along with the account's other entries and interest. Everything that adds up amounts uses what it comes to: In Bank, Gains and Costs, the 28 days after the calendar, Other Accounts' balances and In 6 Months, the tracker, Search, reminders, Jeremy, and the Quick Entry icon's balances.
  • Once its day passes, it stays what it came to that day: the daily update makes it a plain amount, so History never has one. Days that joined the calendar while you were away work theirs out one day at a time as they're caught up.
  • Two entries that become one (the same title and type on a day: adding to it, Move to, or a recurring or spreadsheet entry landing on it) are one plain amount, what the two come to. Set to with % makes it that percent again.
  • It needs its account: changed to Regular or to no Other Account (or with its account deleted), a % entry keeps what it came to as a plain amount. It can't go on a day that already passed (type the amount there instead), and a Quick Entry icon's % entry for a day that passed before the budget opened is added as the amount the icon worked out.
  • A recurring entry's percent shows in the Recurring Entries table (like -10%), and each day it lands works out its own; its stored amount, which Monthly and Yearly count, is about what it comes to the first time it lands. A connected spreadsheet's amount cell can be a percent too (see Connected spreadsheets).

4. The Calendar And The Budget Math

The calendar always shows four weeks, Sunday through Saturday, starting with the current week. Each day's cell is text:

10/05
✅ $1528.50 In Bank
❇️ $45.00 Gains
✴️ -$6.50 Costs
❗️ -$6.50 Coffee
❗️ $45.00 Sold Lamp
  • The first line is the date, stored as MM/DD and shown in the device's date format (like 10/05 or 05/10).
  • In Bank (✅, or ⛔️ below $0): what will be in the budget's account at the end of that day.
  • Gains (❇️) and Costs (✴️): that day's gains and costs added up, Regular and Hidden (shown only when not $0). Transfers are left out.
  • Then the entries, grouped by kind (unique, temps, hidden, transfers, recurring, then connected spreadsheets') and in title order within each group.
  • Today is highlighted. A day turns red when its In Bank is below $0.
  • A cost's words are red, a gain's green, and a $0.00 entry's a darker grey (a placeholder's yellow, whatever its amount). In Bank, Gains, and Costs stay light grey.
  • A day that isn't zoomed shows each line only as wide as the day, cut short with "..." when it's longer.

How In Bank Is Worked Out

In Bank is a running total. It starts from the In Bank of the last day in History (the most recent Saturday), or $0 for a new budget. Each day: In Bank = the day before's In Bank + that day's Regular gains − Regular costs + transfers. Hidden entries show in the day's Gains and Costs, but never change In Bank (they happen outside the budget's account). It's worked out again after every change, so it's always up to date.

The 28 Days After The Calendar

The budget looks at the 28 days after the calendar too (recurring entries, connected spreadsheets' entries, and Upcoming entries, but not Hidden ones), and keeps what it finds as two lines in the calendar's last day, right under its In Bank:

  • ✅ $812.40 Lowest: 11/14: the lowest In Bank in those 28 days, and the day it happens.
  • ✅ $1650.00 Day 28 In Bank: In Bank on the 28th day.

Home shows them in the Lowest card instead of on the day ("Lowest is on 11/14: $812.40" and "Day 28 is: $1650.00", each amount red when it's below $0). If the lowest is below $0, its line has ⛔️, and the whole Lowest card turns red (it also does when Lowest in bank after today is below $0). The last day itself only turns red for its own In Bank.

Jeremy

Jeremy is the budget's helper. On Home, his card has his everyday message, worked out with every change: a greeting (it changes daily), then what needs attention, then the day's affirmation (each date has its own, the same one every year, with a leap year one on February 29 and a new year's one on December 31). Jeremy mentions:

  • days on the calendar from today on whose In Bank is below $0;
  • going below $0 in the 28 days after the calendar;
  • uncommon recurring payments (ones that land every 3 months or less often) on the calendar from today on;
  • how many Upcoming entries there are (not $0.00 temps, which only keep a recurring entry off a day; a $0.00 note, like "Add To Savings", counts).

If there's nothing to mention, Jeremy says everything looks fine. A budget with no recurring entries yet gets a welcome instead of a greeting. Tapping his card (it says "More from Jeremy") opens his page.

Jeremy's Page

Jeremy's page (/menu/jeremy.html: Menu, then Jeremy, or tap his card on Home) is where he works out everything heavier than his message, only while the page is open, so the rest of the budget never waits on it. It's a column of cards:

  • Jeremy's Highlights, at the top with his picture: a line from each card that's on (red-orange when it needs a look), and how many cards are off.
  • Data and Accounts are always on. Every other card has an on/off switch at its right and starts off (a budget that had goals before the page starts with Goals on). Which cards are on is kept with the budget, so every device, and anyone sharing the budget, sees the same page. A card that's off works nothing out. Turning one on or off is logged in Change Logs ("💡 Jeremy's Cards").
  • Every card has a ? in a circle after its title that says, in a line or two, what the card is for.
  • What a tap shows (a table or a list) opens in a floating window over the page.

Data lists what every card works from, each row with its figure and a note: when History starts (and whether it reaches back the 4 months Payday and Spending Pace need), In Bank today, Lowest in bank after today and its day, recurring entries each month (bills and gains, transfers left out, from how often each one lands), paydays (the next one: a payday is a Regular gain from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet, and paychecks fewer than 4 days apart, like two people's, are one payday), what was spent besides recurring bills in the last 7 days and in a usual week, what usually goes out between paychecks, Catchup entries in the last 30 days, big bills in the next 60 days, and purchases in the last 30 days (Regular costs added by hand or with the Quick Entry icon, not placeholders). A row with a › opens what's behind it: the recurring entries with each one's amount a month, each payday with what went out after it, each week of the last 4 months, or the entries themselves.

Accounts is a table of In Bank, each Other Account, and the Total (In Bank and every Other Account together): what each holds now (with its Max after it, when it has one), and what it will hold at the end of the day in the box at the top of the third column, counting everything planned until then (the calendar's days as they are, then Upcoming, recurring entries, and connected spreadsheets' entries, the way the daily update will fill those days in, and interest). The day can be tomorrow through 10 years ahead, typed or picked with its calendar button. It starts 6 months ahead, and the device remembers the one picked (prismal_jeremy_accounts_day, kept after signing out). When an account has interest, an Interest column shows what each one gets from today through that day (below $0 is interest paid, like a credit card's) with its yearly rate, and the Total's.

Goals

Jeremy's Goals card sets amounts for In Bank, the Total, or an Other Account to reach. New Goal asks what it's for (each with what it holds now; Savings is picked to start), its amount, and if you like, a title of its own and a day to reach it by. The amount can be a % of what it's for instead (the $ / % switch): that percent of what it holds the day the goal is set, which then stays the goal's amount (the goal shows the percent "when set" too). A credit card's goal can be $0.00 (paid off), or any amount above what it holds.

  • Each goal shows its title (or what it's for), what that holds now and the goal's amount, and its day ("Savings: $500.00 of $2000.00 by 12/31/2027"), with a thin bar for how far along it is. How far along counts from what it held when the goal was set, so a card climbing from -$2000.00 to $0.00 shows how much is paid off.
  • Reached once it holds the amount. With a day: On track when the budget as it stands gets it there by then; otherwise how short it is on that day, and what to set aside from each payday until then (for an Other Account; for one below $0, paying more toward it), or how much less to spend from each (for In Bank or the Total), and about how much that is a month. For an account with interest, Jeremy works the interest out again with the extra in it (a card paid down faster pays less interest), so the amount is the least that gets there, to the cent.
  • In danger when setting that aside would take In Bank below $0 on a day before then (the money comes out of In Bank), when spending that much less is more than usually goes out between paychecks besides bills (from Data), when no paycheck lands before its day, or when its day passed ("Missed").
  • Without a day: when the budget as it stands gets it there (looking up to 10 years ahead), or when it doesn't, what setting aside from each payday would get it there in a year.
  • Tapping one changes it or deletes it. A % goal keeps its amount unless its percent or what it's for changes. Up to 20 goals. A goal set before goals needed what they're for asks for one the next time it's changed.
  • Renaming an account keeps its goals with it, and a goal for an account that was deleted says so. Goals are logged in Change Logs ("🎯 New Goal", "🎯 Change Goal", "🎯 Delete Goal").

Needs, Wants, And Future

Each title can have a purpose: Needs (like rent, groceries, and bills), Wants (like eating out and fun), Future (like savings, or paying down a card), or None. Once Jeremy's Needs, Wants, And Future card is on, every form that adds or changes an entry asks at its bottom: New Entry, an entry's options (on a zoomed day on Home or in History, in Upcoming, in Search's results, and in an account's Entries), the Recurring Entries page's form, and the Quick Entry icon's form.

  • It's kept for the title (compared the way the tracker compares titles: "Groceries → Cash" is Groceries), so every entry with that title shares it, past and future. The form starts on the title's purpose and follows the title as it's typed, until one is tapped, and changing it on one entry changes it for the title. The Quick Entry icon can't see which titles have which, so its form starts with none picked, which leaves the title's as it is; one that's picked is given to the title when the entry joins the budget.
  • Turning the card off hides the question everywhere and keeps what each title has. Up to 2,000 titles can have one. A change to only a title's purpose is logged in Change Logs ("🏷️ Entry Purpose"), and a change to an entry that changes its title's purpose says so in its own row.
  • The card's pie chart (a ring) adds up what each purpose's titles cost between its From and To days (the last 30 days to start): History's days, the calendar's, and after the calendar the way the budget fills those days in (Upcoming, recurring entries, and connected spreadsheets' entries, up to two years ahead). A title counts by what its entries come to together, so a refund takes off what it cost, and a title whose entries come to a gain isn't in it. Placeholders count; $0.00 entries don't.
  • The total is in the middle, and pointing at a slice (or moving to it with the keyboard) shows its amount and share there. Beside the chart (under it on phones), each purpose's name, amount, and share. Tapping a slice or a name opens its window: the days (changing them there changes the card's too), what it came to and its share, and each of its titles, biggest first, with what it cost, its share of the purpose, and how many entries it has, in a box that scrolls.
  • What the costs with no purpose yet come to shows under the chart; tapping it opens those titles, biggest first, each with Needs, Wants, and Future buttons that give it one right there.
  • The colors are blue (Needs), orange (Wants), and green (Future), picked so every pair stays apart for every kind of color blindness, and each one is always named in words beside its amount.

Jeremy's Tips

Each of Jeremy's tips is a card of its own on his page, with its own switch, worked out each time the page draws:

  • Payday: on a day with a paycheck (a Regular gain from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet), "Hey, you just got paid! Keep in mind, besides your recurring bills, you tend to spend about $X in the next N days, so be careful what you spend it on!" N is the days until the next payday, at least 4 days away (14 if there isn't one). On any other day it says when the next payday is, and about how much usually goes out from one payday to the next (N is then the days from the next payday to the one after it). $X is the average of what went out of In Bank in the N days after each payday in the last 4 months, besides recurring bills (Regular costs that didn't come from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet). It needs History to reach back 4 months. See paydays opens each one.
  • Spending Pace: what went out in the last 7 days (today too) besides recurring bills, next to a usual week (the 4 months before them, averaged). When the last 7 days are at least $50 and 25% over, Jeremy says how much was spent and how much more than usual that is, and suggests taking it easy for a few days. It also needs 4 months of History.
  • Big Bills: the big uncommon bills in the next 60 days: a recurring cost of $200 or more that lands every 3 months or less often, a connected spreadsheet's cost of $200 or more with no other one like it (same title and type) within 90 days, or a one-time cost of $200 or more added for a later day, on the calendar or in Upcoming, never a transfer. A bill that a recurring entry lands more often than every 3 months, like rent every month, is never one, not even on a day it was changed for, or added by hand with that recurring entry's title and type. Jeremy names the soonest one (and how many more there are), says to make sure you're saving enough to afford it, and how much to set aside from each paycheck until then (the bill divided by the paychecks before it, today's too, rounded up to a dollar); if it lands before the next paycheck, to check that what's there now covers it.
  • Weekly Check: on Sundays, to compare today's In Bank with the real bank account, find the purchases that make up any difference and add them (on the day they happened, or just today), and for anything that can't be found, tap today's In Bank on Home and type what the bank says, "and I'll add a Catchup entry to make up the difference." On other days it says when the next check is.
  • Catchup Entries: what entries titled Catchup (or Catch Up), Regular or Transfer, cost in the last 30 days. At $100 or more, Jeremy says purchases are slipping by and suggests adding each one as it happens, with the Quick Entry icon.
  • Refunds: when Lowest in bank after today is below $0, "Hey, Lowest in bank after today goes to (that amount) on (that day). Is a refund possible?", with up to 5 purchases (Regular costs added by hand or with the Quick Entry icon, not placeholders) from the last 30 days, or still to come by that day, that would help: first any that would cover it by itself (the smallest of those first), then the biggest others.

Upcoming (Future Dates)

An entry dated after the calendar waits in Upcoming, in date order. When its day joins the calendar, it moves onto that day (combining with a matching entry there). The 28-day lines and Lowest in bank after today count Upcoming entries. Tapping one in Home's Upcoming list opens its options, like an entry on a zoomed day.

Dates, Time Zones, And Formats

  • Stored the same way everywhere: a day is stored as text, MM/DD/YYYY (MM/DD on a calendar day's first line, YYYY-MM-DD for the API), never as a moment in time, and read back as that same calendar day in whatever time zone the device is in. Changing the device's language, region, date format, or time zone never moves an entry to another day.
  • Shown and typed in the device's format: its own (from its language and region: MM/DD/YYYY in the US, DD/MM/YYYY in most of the world, YYYY-MM-DD in some places), or the one picked in Settings, Language And Dates (kept on that device). Typed dates are read the same way, with /, ., -, or spaces between the numbers, or as digits alone, since a phone's number pad has no / or - (10052026, 100526, or 1005 for 10/05; 20261005 where the year comes first). A year first (2026-10-05) always works, a missing year is the nearest one, a 2-digit year is in the 2000s (in a birth date, the latest year it can be, so 90 is 1990), and a day that doesn't exist (like 31/02) is refused instead of rolling over. Month names work too, in English, either way around and with or without a year or a weekday (Oct 5, 5 October 2026, Mon, Oct 5, 2026), and read the same in every browser. Change Logs are written in the format in use at the time.
  • Today is the device's date. Days are counted on the calendar, never in 24-hour steps, so daylight saving time can't skip or repeat one.
  • The budget's day never goes back: if the device's date is before the last day the budget was brought up to date (after flying west across midnight, with the clock set back, or after another device that's a day ahead opened it), the budget keeps using that later day until the device catches up. Otherwise the calendar could be laid out on the week before, with every day's entries on the wrong day.
  • A page left open past midnight, or on a device that moved to another time zone, reloads when it's next brought into view or used (unless it has a change it couldn't save yet), so the daily update runs first. Reminders follow the device's current time zone, which is sent with every reminder plan.

5. Recurring Entries

Each recurring entry has a title, a refraction type (shown as ✔️ Regular, ✔️✖️ Hidden, or ✔️⭕️ Transfer), an amount, a frequency, a Start Date, and an optional End Date. Its panel (New Entry or a tapped row, on the Recurring Entries page) asks for them in this order: Title, Refraction Type (Regular to start), Other Account (for Hidden or Transfer), Amount, Frequency, Start Date (today to start), End Date, and Placeholder, then Add Entry (or Save, Delete) and Cancel. Hidden and Transfer ones can be for an Other Account, like a transfer into Savings every payday, and then their amount can be a percent of the account's balance instead, worked out for each day they land (see % amounts).

Frequency: Every N Days, Weeks, Or Months

  • Frequency is a whole number from 1 to 999 and a unit: Days, Weeks, or Months. It's stored and shown like "Every Day", "Every 2 Weeks", "Every Month", or "Every 12 Months".
  • It counts from the Start Date. Every 2 weeks lands on the Start Date, then every 14 days. Every 6 days lands every 6th day.
  • Months land on the Start Date's day of the month, or the month's last day when the month is shorter: an entry starting on the 31st lands on the 30th in 30-day months and on February 28 (29 in leap years). Every 12 months is once a year.
  • It never lands before its Start Date or after its End Date. No End Date means it keeps going.
  • Older names still work: Weekly, Biweekly, Monthly, 3 Month, 6 Month, and Yearly are every 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months, and get rewritten that way. Semi-Monthly (the 1st and the 15th) has no every-N version, so entries that already have it keep landing on the 1st and 15th. For something new on the 1st and 15th, make two monthly entries with different titles, like "Paycheck 1st" and "Paycheck 15th".
  • Uncommon recurring entries are ones that land every 3 months or less often (every 3+ months, 13+ weeks, or 90+ days). Jeremy and the daily reminders point them out.

One Per Title, Type, And Other Account

Two recurring entries can have the same title only when their refraction type or Other Account is different. So Payday can be a Regular, a Hidden, and a Transfer recurring entry at once, and "Payday → Savings" and "Payday → HSA" are two different ones (as is a plain Payday Transfer next to them), but there can't be two Transfers called "Payday → Savings". This keeps it clear which recurring entry a day's entry came from.

  • Adding one, or changing one's title, type, or account, so it matches another in all three is refused with a message naming that one, so the existing one gets changed instead.
  • Renaming an Other Account renames its recurring entries too, so a rename that would make two match (like one still named for a deleted account with the new name) is refused, saying which.
  • If a budget has two that match anyway, the Recurring Entries page lists them under "Needs a look" until one is changed or deleted, and on a day both land on, only the first one in the list does.

On The Calendar

  • Recurring entries are written onto today and every later day of the calendar they land on. Their amount counts in In Bank like any entry of their type.
  • Once a day passes, its recurring entries become temps (❗️✔️ and so on), so later changes to a recurring entry never change days that already happened.
  • Changing a recurring entry on the calendar changes only that day (it becomes a temp). Moving or deleting it leaves a $0.00 blocker on its day.
  • Changing a recurring entry's title or refraction type takes those changed days along: from today on, its temps and $0.00 blockers (on the calendar and in Upcoming) get the new title and type, so a changed day keeps its amount and a deleted day stays deleted. Days that passed keep theirs.
  • Moving a recurring entry's End Date earlier lets go of the days after it: from today on, its temps there (on the calendar and in Upcoming) become plain unique entries and its $0.00 blockers go away, since there's nothing left for them to hold back. (If the End Date moves later again, the recurring entry lands on those days again and adds into a matching unique entry there, like on any day.)
  • Deleting a recurring entry ends it yesterday: it stops from today on, its temps from today on become plain unique entries (and its blockers go away), and days that passed keep theirs.
  • With Placeholder on (in its panel), it lands as a placeholder on each of its days, in yellow, and each one is deleted once its day passes, so it never leaves a temp behind (see Placeholders). The switch shows while it can still land: with no End Date, or one from today on.

Monthly And Yearly

The top of the Recurring Entries page shows (each on one line, so a minus sign stays with its amount):

  • Monthly: about how much the recurring costs add up to each month (every 2 weeks counts as about 2.17 times a month, every week about 4.35, every 3 months a third).
  • Yearly: what was spent so far this year (costs on past days and today), plus every recurring cost still to land from tomorrow through December 31.

Neither counts gains or transfers (transferred money is still yours). Hidden costs count, like any cost.

6. Connected Spreadsheets

A spreadsheet you already keep, like a list of bills, paychecks, or a shift schedule, can be connected on the Spreadsheets page (in the menu). Its entries then land on your calendar by themselves, from today on, like recurring entries, behind a yellow plus: ➕❗️ Regular, ➕✖️ Hidden, or ➕⭕️ Transfer. When the spreadsheet changes (an amount, a date, a new row, a deleted row), the calendar follows it. The Spreadsheets page's Help explains it in short.

Sharing It

  • A verified email first: connecting (and reading) spreadsheets needs the account's email to be verified, with the link emailed when the account was made or Send Verification Email (on the Spreadsheets page, or in Settings under Email). Until then, the Spreadsheets page says so and Add Spreadsheet is off, and a spreadsheet that's already connected (like after the email changed) keeps its last entries and says to verify the email.
  • Anyone with the link (Viewer): works for Google Sheets (its sharing link, or a File > Share > Publish to web link), files in Google Drive (like an Excel file kept there), Excel in OneDrive or SharePoint, and Dropbox. Anyone who has a public link can see the whole spreadsheet, so the link's ? suggests a separate spreadsheet with only what Prismal Budget needs, filled from the main one (in Google Sheets, =IMPORTRANGE("main spreadsheet's link", "Bills!A2:C50") brings those cells over and keeps them up to date).
  • Shared with admin@prismalbudget.org (Viewer): for Google Sheets and Google Drive files, nothing has to be public. Prismal Budget's Google account reads it for the Prismal Budget account whose verified email, or one of its confirmed Other Emails, is the email of the Google account that owns the spreadsheet or shared it with admin@prismalbudget.org, and for no one else. Gmail addresses match with or without dots, and without anything after a +, the way Gmail delivers them. (This uses the server's Google sign-in for that account, set up once; see Notes for developers. Without it, only links anyone can open work.)
  • Other Emails: spreadsheets kept in a Google account with another email than the Prismal Budget account's can be shared with admin@prismalbudget.org too. Add that email under Other Emails in Settings (under Email; the Spreadsheets page links there), and we email it a link that works for 24 hours (Send Again sends a new one, and the one before stops working). Once it's opened (the login page checks it, signed in or not), the spreadsheets that email shares count as the account's, and the email signs in to the account too (with the password). Up to 5 per account. Removing one stops its spreadsheets from being read for the account (and it stops signing in), and one that's never confirmed goes away when its link runs out.
  • Apple Numbers doesn't share a spreadsheet in a way another site can read, so its links say to keep the spreadsheet in Google Sheets or Excel instead (in Numbers, File > Export To > Excel). Older Excel files (.xls) are asked to be saved as .xlsx.
  • The server downloads the file from where it's kept and passes it straight to the page, without keeping it. Microsoft doesn't say how a sharing link downloads, so for OneDrive and SharePoint links the server tries the known ways in turn.

Adding One

  1. Add Spreadsheet opens a box that asks for the link. Open Spreadsheet downloads it, says what it found (its name, how many tabs, and how it's shared), and the box grows into the entry form, named after the file (the name can be changed).
  2. Each part of an entry has a yellow ➕ to the left of its box: Date, Title, Amount, Refraction Type, and Other Account (which shows for Hidden or Transfer, or when the type or the account comes from cells). Tapping ➕ opens a second box over the first: the tab (a box to type its name, with the spreadsheet's tabs under it to tap, hidden ones too) and the cells, typed as columns and rows: From Column B Row 2 To Column B Row 50, which is B2:B50. The "To" boxes are optional (just one cell), a column can be letters or its number (2 is B), and typing B2:B50 into the first box fills all four. As it's typed, the box shows the range and what's in it ("= Bills!B2:B50: 49 cells, like Rent, Phone, Internet"). Use These Cells puts the range where the part's box was ("Bills · B2:B50"; tap it to change it), and the ➕ fills in. Type It Instead goes back to typing.
  3. Only the dates have to come from the spreadsheet. The title, amount (with Cost or Gain), refraction type, and Other Account can come from cells, or be typed (or picked) once for every entry. A part from cells needs as many cells as the dates, or just one cell, which then goes with every entry.
  4. Each date cell makes one entry, with the cells in the same spot of the other parts (cells are read row by row, left to right). Empty date cells are skipped, so picking more rows than are used now picks up rows added later.
  5. Under the parts, Entries shows what it finds from today on (the first 8, and how many), and Needs a look lists anything it can't read. Save Spreadsheet connects it. With nothing to add and something that needs a look, it says to fix that first.

How Cells Are Read

  • Dates: date cells are dates (whatever format they show in). A number in a date column is a date's serial number (a date without a date format). Text is read like a typed date (except digits alone, which a cell holds as a number): the device's date order, YYYY-MM-DD, or month names like "Oct 20, 2026" or "20 October" (a weekday in front is fine, and a missing year is the nearest one). A cell that isn't a date is listed under Needs a look.
  • Amounts: number cells as they are; text with a currency sign or code, thousands separators, a minus before or after, or accountants' parentheses (($45.50) is a cost). As Written reads a minus as a cost and anything else as a gain; most bill lists write costs without a minus, so All Costs makes every one a cost (and All Gains every one a gain). A cell that's a percent (3%, or a number formatted as a percent) makes a % entry, a percent of its Other Account's balance, so its entry needs an Other Account; a typed amount can be a percent the same way, with the $ / % switch, once an account is picked.
  • Refraction types: Regular, Hidden, or Transfer (any capitals, or just R, H, or T, or the emoji); an empty cell is Regular.
  • Other Accounts: the account's name (any capitals); an empty cell is none. The entry keeps its title, with the account after it ("Payday → Savings", or "→ Savings" with no title), so it changes that account like any entry for it (a Transfer by the opposite of its amount, a Hidden entry by its own). It has to be one of your Other Accounts, and the entry has to be Hidden or Transfer.
  • Titles get each word's first letter capitalized, like any entry, up to 100 characters. Formulas count as what they show, and text with formatting in it reads as plain text.

On The Calendar

  • Its entries are written onto today and every later day of the calendar (never days before today), and taken off and written again with every change, from the spreadsheet as it was last read. Entries with the same title and type on the same day are one entry, their amounts added.
  • They work like recurring entries: a unique entry with the same title and type on that day takes in the amount (it becomes a temp, like ❗️➕); changing one for its day (Set to, Add to it, Move to) makes a temp that keeps the spreadsheet's entry off that day even when the spreadsheet changes; deleting one leaves a $0.00 temp; and Undo works on them like any calendar change.
  • An entry with the same title and refraction type as a recurring entry that lands the same day is left out (a day has one of each), and the spreadsheet's box on the Spreadsheets page names it.
  • Once a day passes, its spreadsheet entries become temps, so they stay as they were, even if the spreadsheet changes later or is deleted.
  • Placeholder (a pill switch in its panel, under its parts): every entry it adds is a placeholder, in yellow, deleted once its day passes instead of becoming a temp (see Placeholders). Its button on the Spreadsheets page then counts "placeholders from today on", in yellow.
  • They count everywhere the budget looks ahead: In Bank, Lowest in bank after today and the 28 days after the calendar, Search (past the calendar too), the daily reminders, Other Accounts' balances, and Jeremy's page (Payday counts their Regular gains as paychecks, Spending Pace leaves their costs out like recurring bills, Big Bills counts a big one with no other like it within 90 days, and Accounts and Goals count them ahead). Like recurring entries, they aren't listed in Upcoming.

Staying Up To Date

  • Every spreadsheet that's on is read again when Home opens (at most every minute and a half each, on that device) or comes back into view, and every time the Spreadsheets page opens. The budget only changes when something in a spreadsheet did, and the change is logged as "➕ Spreadsheet Update".
  • A row the spreadsheet drops comes off the calendar from today on. A day changed for it becomes a plain unique entry (a $0.00 one goes away), since there's nothing left for it to hold back.
  • If a spreadsheet can't be read (sharing turned off, say, or the account's email isn't verified), its last entries stay, and its box says why. A dropped connection isn't shown; it's just read again next time.

On, Off, And Delete

  • Off takes its entries off the calendar (and it isn't read) until it's turned back On, which reads it again right away. Days changed for it keep their changes.
  • Delete takes its entries off from today on; days changed for it become plain unique entries, its $0.00 temps go away, and days that passed keep theirs. The spreadsheet itself is never touched.

Needs A Look

Each problem names its cell (like Bills!A5) and says how to fix it: a date or amount that can't be read, an amount over $1,000,000,000, a refraction type that isn't one, an Other Account you don't have (add it on the Other Accounts page, or fix the cell), an account on a Regular entry, a percent with no Other Account, a missing tab, parts whose cells aren't the same size as the dates, or a spreadsheet that isn't shared or is too big. A cell's problem only leaves out its own entry; the first 6 are listed, then how many more. A recurring entry keeping one off is listed too.

Files And Limits

  • Files: .xlsx (Excel; Google Sheets downloads as one, which keeps real dates), .ods (LibreOffice and others), and .csv (comma, tab, or semicolon separated). The browser reads them itself (the zip inside an .xlsx or .ods is opened with the browser's own DecompressionStream), so a browser from before 2023 can't.
  • Up to 10 spreadsheets, 8 MB each, and 5,000 cells for each part. Entries are kept from 100 days before today (Jeremy's Big Bills card looks for ones like them) to 800 days after, up to 3,000 each, and only today's and later ones land.

7. The Daily Update

The first time the budget opens on a new day, it brings everything up to date, no matter how many days were missed, in one pass:

  1. Days that joined the calendar while you were away get their Upcoming entries, then their recurring entries, then their connected spreadsheets' entries (as they were last read).
  2. Away over a month: only the first month after the budget was last used gets recurring and spreadsheet entries. The days after that get just their Upcoming entries and In Bank, so months of entries nobody checked don't have to be deleted by hand (and catching up stays quick). Adding an entry with the Quick Entry icon counts as using the budget. Days from today on get their recurring and spreadsheet entries as always.
  3. Every day that passed loses its placeholders (its own, Upcoming's, and its recurring and spreadsheet entries' that are placeholders).
  4. A day that joined the calendar while you were away works out its % entries, from the Other Accounts' balances when the budget was last open and each day before it.
  5. Every day that passed has its recurring and spreadsheet entries turned into temps (❗️✔️, ❗️➕, and so on), and its % entries into plain amounts (what they came to), so they stay as they were that day.
  6. In Bank keeps going from the last known balance, and from the first day that lost a placeholder on, it's worked out again without it.
  7. Every week before the current week moves to History (newest on top), and the calendar becomes the current four weeks.
  8. The change is logged as "🕛 Daily Update", and the date it's done through is saved only after the data itself is saved, so a failed save simply happens again next time.

A brand-new budget starts today, with nothing to catch up. If the page stays open past midnight, the next change reloads it so the daily update runs first.

8. Other Accounts (Like Savings)

Other Accounts keeps track of money outside the budget's bank account, like savings, cash, investments, or a credit card.

  • Linking: a Hidden or Transfer entry for an account moves money into or out of that account. When you pick Hidden or Transfer for a new entry (on the calendar, in Recurring Entries, or with the Quick Entry icon), an Other Account choice appears, right under the refraction type and before the amount (which can be a % of the account): None, then each account as a button with its name, and its balance today on a second line (red below $0; with a Max, "$500.00/$2000.00"). Goals pick what they're for the same way. Picking one never changes the title: the account is kept after it, shown as "Payday → Savings" (with no title, "→ Savings"). Only an entry with an account picked is for it: one titled with an account's name and no account picked is an ordinary entry, and changes no account. A title can't be typed with → in it (New Entry, Recurring Entries, prisms, and spreadsheets say so), since the arrow is what marks the account picked. Each account after a title is its own entry, so "Payday → Savings" and "Payday → HSA" can be on the same day; the tracker and Search go by the title ("Payday"), and Search finds it by the account's name too.
  • Transfers give the account the opposite amount: a Transfer moves money between the budget's account and the other account, so In Bank changes by the entry's amount and the account by the opposite. Moving $100 into savings is a −$100 Transfer: In Bank −$100, Savings +$100. Taking $200 out is a +$200 Transfer: In Bank +$200, Savings −$200.
  • Hidden entries give the account their own amount: a Hidden entry is money moving in the other account itself, so In Bank stays the same and the account changes by the entry's own amount, with the same sign. Putting $50 cash into savings is a +$50 Hidden entry (Savings +$50); paying $30 straight out of savings is a −$30 one (Savings −$30).
  • Balances: an account's balance is its starting amount plus all of its entries' changes (the opposite of each Transfer, and each Hidden entry's own amount), through today. Its panel lists its latest entries and the ones coming up, each with its own title (or its type, for one with no title). Tapping an account and typing what's in it today (like $3,000 in savings) sets the starting amount so today's balance comes out to it; no entry is made, so the calendar, tracker, and Search stay the same. It's logged in Change Logs as a change to the account.
  • % entries: a Hidden or Transfer entry for an account can be a percent of the account's balance as its day starts instead of an amount (see % amounts).
  • A balance can be below $0, like a credit card's: its box has Negative and Positive buttons (the amount is typed like any other, see Entries).
  • Interest: an account's panel has an Add interest each month switch and, while it's on, the yearly rate (APR, like a credit card's 24.99; up to 1000). Once a month, on the day of the month the interest started counting, the balance changes by that morning's balance (its starting amount, its entries before that day, and the interest before it) times a twelfth of the rate, to the cent: a balance below $0 goes further below, and one above $0 grows. Each month's interest shows in the panel's entries as % Interest and counts in Balance, In 6 Months, and the Quick Entry icon's account list, but it isn't an entry, so the calendar, tracker, and Search don't show it. Turning it on, changing the rate, or typing a new balance starts the count again from that day, with the interest so far kept in today's balance (so it's never counted twice); turning it off keeps today's balance as it is.
  • The page shows, at the top, the total of everything: In Bank (the budget's own account) plus every account, today and in 6 months ("Total in 6 Months"), and In Bank today on its own. Then each account's balance today, and In 6 Months (six months from today, to the day): what's still to come by then counts, its entries on the calendar and in Upcoming, the ones its recurring entries and connected spreadsheets will add (the way the daily update will fill those days in), and interest. The total's In Bank counts the same way. Tapping an account shows its latest and upcoming entries and lets you rename it or set its interest.
  • Savings and Cash are in every budget and can't be deleted (each shows a 🔒 in Edit, and its panel has no Delete), but either can be renamed. A budget that didn't have Cash gets it, right after Savings, the next time it opens (an account already called Cash becomes it). Savings is the default: Getting Started's savings goes into it.
  • Entries: each account's row ends in an Entries button, which opens a floating list of every entry made on the account (on the calendar, in History, and in Upcoming; a recurring or spreadsheet entry once it's on the calendar), newest first, each with its day ("coming up" after today), the entry, and what it put in or took out of the account (green in, red out). Tapping one opens its options over the list, like an entry on a zoomed day (one in History keeps the balances), and the list follows the change. A $0.00 blocker isn't listed.
  • Max (optional, in an account's panel, typed like any amount): the most it can hold, like a credit card's limit. With one, its balance today shows over it on Other Accounts, in its panel's account pickers, and in Goals: "$500.00/$2000.00". Blank is none. It only shows; it never stops an entry.
  • Renaming an account renames its entries (on the calendar, in History, Upcoming, and Recurring), the tracker aliases for it, connected spreadsheets set up with its name typed in (and the entries they read), and goals for it (see Goals), so they all stay with it. A spreadsheet whose cells name the account needs those cells changed to the new name, and its box on the Spreadsheets page says so. A rename that would make two recurring entries match (the same title, type, and account) is refused, saying which (see Recurring entries).
  • Deleting an account leaves its entries where they are; they just stop changing an account.
  • Entries for an account keep their type everywhere else: a Transfer for one is still a transfer (it isn't a cost or gain, and only tracker rows set to count transfers count it), and a Hidden one is a cost or gain like any Hidden entry (the day's Gains and Costs show it, and the tracker, Search, and the Monthly and Yearly totals count it).

9. The Tracker

The tracker adds up entries by category over the last 4 weeks (today and the 27 days before), last 3 months (90 days), and last 365 days. It counts today and every earlier day, from the calendar and History.

  • Rows and aliases: each row counts the entries its aliases point to. An alias is an entry title, set to count All of its amounts, only its Costs, or only its Gains. If several rows have the same alias, each counts it.
  • Hidden entries always count, like Regular ones, even ones for an Other Account.
  • Transfers: rows leave out transfers unless the row's "Count ⭕️ Transfers in this row" switch is on (in its panel); such a row's title starts with ⭕️. A transfer only ever counts in a row like that.
  • Costs and Gains rows fill in by themselves with every cost and every gain (Regular and Hidden), except transfers.
  • Transfers fills in by itself with every transfer (all of their amounts) and lists their titles, so transfers never land in Undefined. A budget without it gets it just above Undefined. Searching it (Tracker) finds every transfer, checked.
  • Undefined fills in by itself with the titles no other row fully counts, and adds them up (never transfers). A title stays there if a row only counts its costs (or gains) and it has the other kind too. Its list is made again from the entries every time, so a title leaves it once a row counts it.
  • Costs, Gains, Transfers, and Undefined can't be deleted (they show a 🔒 in Edit, and their panels have no Delete button), since the tracker fills them in by itself. Every other row can be.
  • A new budget's tracker starts with an Unknown row too, counting Misc, Catchup, and Other: an ordinary row, so it can be renamed, changed, or deleted for good.
  • One row per title and transfer setting: a title can have one row that counts transfers and one that doesn't. Adding (or changing a row into) a match for one that exists is refused with a message, and Costs, Gains, Transfers, and Undefined can't be used as titles.
  • The panel shows each alias on its own line with All / Costs / Gains buttons (only the buttons pick it, and a title is kept as it's typed, like Disney+), and the table shows them in words, like "rent (costs)". Download My Data says them in words too.
  • In the table, a row's aliases scroll in a box about two lines tall, so a long list doesn't make the row tall (a box with more in it fades at the bottom). Tapping that box opens it all the way, with no scrolling, and tapping it again closes it; tapping a box that already shows everything opens the row, like the rest of it.
  • The table fits the screen's width, with no sideways scrolling: a row's title and the column titles take two lines when they need to, and phones get less space around each. On a phone the title column keeps about a fifth of the table, so a long title goes on two lines, and a word too long for it breaks (with a hyphen where the browser can); the Aliases column gets the room that's left. Edit (with its ≡ and checkbox columns) and the tracker's Search results pack the same way on every screen, and on the narrowest phones (under 360 pixels wide), Edit leaves the Aliases column out to make room.

10. Search

Search (the Search button above the calendar opens it, floating over the page; ×, Escape, or the button again closes it) finds entries by title in History, the calendar, Upcoming, and (through the To date, when it's after the calendar) recurring entries projected up to two years ahead and connected spreadsheets' entries after the calendar.

  • Each title is its own line, with its own buttons: All (every entry with that title), Costs (only its costs), Gains (only its gains), or Tracker (the title is a tracker row: find what that row counts, each alias with its own All, Costs, or Gains setting). A small ? just left of the first title's All shows what each of them (and Exact) finds, under the titles; tapping it again hides it.
  • Exact (a pill switch beside each title box, off to start): off, a title finds every entry whose title has it in it, compared the way titles are (capitals and punctuation don't matter), so "gas" finds Gas and Gas Station; on, only entries with exactly that title. A title's Other Account counts both ways ("sav" finds "Payday → Savings"). A Tracker line has no switch: its row's aliases are always exact, like the tracker counts them.
  • Enter (or + Add Title) starts the next line; × takes a line out. Enter on an empty line, or the Search button, searches.
  • With no titles, the first line's buttons decide: everything, every cost, or every gain.
  • A title can have two tracker rows (one counting transfers, like ⭕️Food, and one not). Typing the ⭕️, or "Food (counts transfers)", picks that one; otherwise the one that doesn't count transfers is used. The suggestions list every row, a row that counts transfers as "Food (counts transfers)" (a suggestion list can't show the site's ⭕️ image).
  • Filters: one refraction type (Regular, Hidden, or Transfer) and one Other Account (or entries with none). An entry has to match both, as well as a title line.
  • From and To are optional. A blank From means from the start; a blank To means a month after today (or a month after From, when From is later), so what's coming up shows too: Upcoming, and recurring and connected spreadsheets' entries past the calendar. Each has a calendar button to pick a day (see Picking a date), and days on the calendar can be tapped to fill them.
  • Results are newest first, each with a checkbox, and a total of the checked ones. Results after today come first, tinted, under a "Coming up" heading, and a "Today and before" heading sets them apart from the rest. Transfers start unchecked (they aren't really costs or gains), unless a tracker row that counts transfers found them.
  • Changing a result: tapping a result (anywhere but its checkbox) opens its options, the same as an entry on a zoomed day: one in History keeps the balances, one on the calendar or in Upcoming changes like any. A result that hasn't happened yet and isn't in the budget yet (a recurring entry's or a connected spreadsheet's, past the calendar) can be changed too: the change goes into Upcoming for that day as a temp (like ❗️✔️), the way a changed day on the calendar does, so when that day joins the calendar it keeps the change instead of the recurring entry landing again; deleting one leaves a $0.00 blocker there. Open results are found again after every change (yours, or another device's), so they always show the budget as it is.
  • Only the buttons pick what a line finds: a title is searched for as it's typed.
  • On History: the same Search, from the Search button beside the tabs. While a From or To box is waiting (History gets a dashed outline), tapping a day in History fills it in.
  • On the Tracker and Change Logs: Search at the top looks through that page's own rows instead. It floats over the page the same way, and as you type, it lists the rows with every word typed (in any order, upper or lower case) in any column: on the tracker, a row's title and aliases; in Change Logs, the time, action, and details. Tapping a tracker row it found opens that row.

11. Quick Entry (Home Screen Icon)

The Quick Entry button (on Home, above the calendar) sets up an icon for a phone's home screen that opens a New Entry form with no login.

  • Setting it up makes a long random key, and the page opens with the key in its link, to be added to the home screen, with the steps for the phone it's on (a list picks another: see Steps for each phone). A page opened inside another app (like Gmail or Instagram) can't add one, so the steps say to open it in the browser first. An account can have 10 icons; making an 11th stops the oldest one.
  • The key can only add entries to a waiting list, see, change, or take back the ones it added while they're still waiting, see In Bank today and the lowest it gets (with its day), see the account's Other Accounts with their balances (for the account picker), and read and change the account's prisms. With Show balances on Quick Entry icons off in Settings, it gets no balances at all: no In Bank, no lowest, and the Other Accounts' names only. It can't see anything else in the budget (no entries, titles, or History).
  • Account balances: the Other Accounts' balances are as of the phone's date, even when the budget hasn't been opened for days: each account's balance from the last time the budget was open, plus its entries (and interest) whose day has come since then (the ones on the calendar, and in the 28 days after it its recurring entries, connected spreadsheets' entries, and Upcoming entries, the way the budget will add them), plus Hidden and Transfer entries still waiting to be added for it, each from its own day on. An entry dated later (like next week) doesn't change a balance until its day, the same as on the calendar, and a waiting placeholder stops counting once its day has passed (the budget leaves it out). The list updates after each entry is added, changed, or taken back.
  • In Bank at the top: In Bank today and the Lowest it gets from today on (with its day, red below $0), side by side over the form. They come from In Bank on each day as the budget last saved it (today through the 28 days after the calendar), plus the entries waiting on this page, each from its own day on (a Regular or Transfer one changes In Bank, a Hidden one doesn't, one left on Auto counts as Regular, and a placeholder only until its day passes). They update after each entry is added, changed, or taken back. Until the budget has saved In Bank by day (any change does, and so does the daily update), they don't show, and they never do while Settings has balances off (the account picker then lists the accounts without their balances).
  • On an Apple Watch (a Quick Entry link sent to yourself in Messages or Mail, then tapped on the watch), the page shows only In Bank today over the Lowest it gets (with its day), big, the same two as the top of the full page, one over the other (with balances off, just whose budget it adds to). The page knows a watch by its browser's name (or a screen too small for the form).
  • From the Lock Screen: the page's Help shows how to make an iPhone shortcut that opens the link (the Shortcuts app's Open URLs action, with a Copy Link button for it) and put it on the Lock Screen (iOS 18 or newer), the Action button, or Back Tap. Android phones' Lock Screen shortcuts can't open a web page, so the Help says so for each kind and suggests the home screen's bottom row instead.
  • Undo and Redo on the Quick Entry page: after adding an entry, Undo takes back the newest one added there while it's still waiting (only the icon that added an entry can take it back), and Redo adds it again. Once the budget has added it, the page says to undo it on the calendar instead, where it's an ordinary Undo step ("quick entry Tea (-$3.00) on 10/05"). The page forgets its Undo list when it's closed.
  • Waiting: beside Undo and Redo, Waiting (with how many) shows while entries added with this icon are still waiting, and opens a list of them over the form, newest first, each with its type, title, amount, and day (a placeholder's in yellow). Change puts one into the form as Change Entry (it glows blue-violet; Save Changes or Cancel, and either one brings back what New Entry had), where its title, amount, date, type, Other Account, and Placeholder switch can all be changed; × deletes one, after asking, so it never joins the budget. Undo follows a change (it takes back the entry as it is now). Only the icon that added an entry sees it here; the list says how many more are waiting from the account's other icons. The moment a budget picks this icon's waiting entries up to add them (a budget on the device the icon belongs to, as it opens or, while it's open, within seconds; or Home's Add on another device), they're on their way in: the list still shows them, marked "Picked up by your budget", but they can't be changed, deleted, or undone here anymore (an entry the budget picked up but couldn't save yet comes back with the next open). Changing one or taking it back then says to do it in the budget instead. One waiting on another device's Home, in Quick Entries To Add, stays this icon's to change or take back until someone taps Add there.
  • Up to 100 entries can wait. The next time the budget opens on the device the icon belongs to (the one whose Quick Entry button made it, or a browser there that opened the icon's page), each of that icon's entries is added like a New Entry (to History if it's dated in the past, to Upcoming if it's after the calendar). Entries from other devices' icons, like a partner's phone on a budget you share, wait on Home instead, in Quick Entries To Add, where whoever has the budget open adds them or deletes them. Each one leaves the waiting list in the same database transaction that saves the table it was added to, so none are lost or added twice. One that can't be added (like a date before History begins) is dropped, and a message says why. (On an iPhone or iPad, a Home Screen icon keeps its own browser storage, so an icon made on another device and only opened from the Home Screen waits on Home too.)
  • % amounts: with Hidden or Transfer and an Other Account picked, the amount can be a percent of the account's balance (see % amounts), and so can a prism's. The page works out about what it comes to from the account's balance today for its own balances, and the budget works it out for its day.
  • Prisms: entries someone adds often, saved as presets under the form. A prism keeps any of a title, an amount (cost or gain), a refraction type, and an Other Account (with Hidden or Transfer, kept apart from the title), at least one of them, and never a date (New Prism has no date box). It can keep the Placeholder switch too (along with at least one of those): its button is yellow, and tapping it turns New Entry's switch on. Tapping a prism only fills those into New Entry (it adds nothing by itself; an Other Account renamed or deleted since the prism was saved isn't picked, and a note says to pick one), scrolls to it, and puts the cursor in the first box still empty, so the rest can be filled in, or Add Entry tapped. Add Prism turns New Entry into New Prism (it glows; what's already filled in counts), and Save Prism keeps what's filled in or picked (Auto counts as no type). Edit Prisms drags them into order by their ≡ and deletes them with ×. The ? next to Add Prism explains them. Prisms are saved with the account (up to 30, the whole list with every change), so every icon, and a new one after a password change, has the same ones.
  • A key stops working when the password is changed or reset, on Sign Out Everywhere, when Settings turns the icons off, or when the account is deleted. Settings shows how many icons work. An icon whose key stopped working goes to the login page, which says to delete it and make a new one; the page opened without a key goes there too.
  • Why the key is in the link: it's what lets the icon skip logging in, and iPhones only save a home screen icon's address (not anything stored in the browser). Anyone with the link can add entries to the budget (and see what the icon sees), so keep it to yourself; changing the password, or Turn Off Icons in Settings, stops it.
  • Back to Quick Entry: opened from the home screen, the icon runs as an app of its own. Its Home button can go on to the budget (signing in first, if needed), but when the app is left and opened again on another page, it goes back to the Quick Entry page it started on (unless something there isn't saved yet). A Face ID or passkey sheet over it (under 3 seconds) doesn't count as leaving. In a browser's tab, a page stays where it was left.
  • The page isn't linked anywhere, is kept out of search engines, loads no analytics, and isn't tied to the web app manifest (so iPhones save its own link, key included).

12. Paycheck Calculator

The calculator works out a paycheck's take-home pay. Its 17 inputs come in four groups, and each calculation's inputs are saved to start the next one. Each box takes a number from 0 to 1,000,000,000 (a blank box counts as 0). The dollar boxes ($/hr and $) are typed like a cash register, filling in from the cents (2550 is $25.50, see Entries); hours, multipliers, and percentages are typed as they are.

  • Earnings: Base Pay ($/hr), Regular Hours, Overtime Multiplier (1.5× to start) and Hours, Premium Multiplier (2× to start) and Hours, Taxable Allowances.
  • Pre-tax Deductions: Medical (a percentage and a fixed amount), Retirement (a percentage and a fixed amount).
  • Taxes: FICA (7.65% to start), Federal, and State percentages.
  • Post-tax: Deductions (a percentage and a fixed amount), Non-taxable Reimbursements.

The math, rounded to cents at each step like payroll software: Gross = regular pay + overtime pay + premium pay + taxable allowances. Pre-tax medical lowers the pay that FICA and income taxes are figured on; pre-tax retirement lowers only the income-tax pay. FICA is figured on the FICA pay, and federal and state taxes on the income-tax pay. Take home = income-tax pay − taxes − post-tax deductions + non-taxable reimbursements. The page shows Take Home, Taxes, and Gross, and how it adds up.

13. Change Logs

Every change is logged, newest first: the time it was asked for, how long it took (from then until the page had drawn it; the page shows a change right away and saves it right after; for the daily update, from asking the server for the budget until it's caught up), the action (like "❗️ Create Unique", "✔️ Create Recurring", "🔧 Tracker Entries", "🏦 New Account", "➕ Connect Spreadsheet", "➕ Spreadsheet Update", "🔎 Search", "🧮 Calculator", "↩️ Undo", "↪️ Redo", or "🕛 Daily Update"), and up to four details (an undo or redo says what it took back or put back, and which days). A change that couldn't be made is logged in red as an Error (and nothing was changed); one that didn't find its entry is marked Not Found. The newest 1,000 are kept. The table fits the screen's width: an action too long for its column ends in "..." (tap it to see it whole), and the details scroll in a box about two lines tall that a tap opens all the way (see Recurring Entries, History, Other Accounts, Tracker).

14. Daily Reminders

On days the budget needs a look, a "🗓️ Check Budget" reminder can arrive at a chosen time (8:00 AM to start, in the device's time zone). A day needs one when:

  • ⛔️ its In Bank is below $0;
  • ✔️ an uncommon recurring payment lands between that day and the end of its four-week calendar;
  • ❗️ it has a cost.

The website works out which days need one (from today through the 28 days after the calendar) and sends that list to the server whenever it changes. Reminders arrive two optional ways (with both off, Settings hides the Daily Reminder card and its time, which come back when either is turned on):

  • Push notifications on each device that turns them on (up to 10 devices). The server checks every 5 minutes and sends any that are due. On iPhone and iPad, the site has to be added to the Home Screen first.
  • A calendar link (an .ics feed with a private code) for Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or most calendar apps. Each reminder is a 30-minute event with an alert.

15. Accounts, Sign-In, And Security

  • A person, not a bot: creating an account includes Cloudflare Turnstile's check that you're a person. It runs by itself while the form is filled in, and only shows a box (above Create Account) when it needs a tap. An account can't be made without it, from the page or any other way.
  • 18 and older: Prismal Budget is intended for people 18 and older. Creating an account asks for a birth date (typed, or picked with the calendar button); under 18 on that day, or a day more than 120 years ago, and an account can't be made. The birth date is stored with the account, and Settings can fix one typed wrong (never to under 18). The Privacy Policy & Terms of Use say so too.
  • Verified emails: a new account's email gets a link to verify it, and so does a changed email (which isn't verified until then; links sent to the old address stop working). Each link works for 24 hours, and tapping it again says the same thing instead of an error. Settings' Send Verification Email sends a new one. A password reset link that works also verifies the email when it went there (one sent to an Other Email doesn't). Connecting and reading spreadsheets need a verified email, since sharing a spreadsheet privately depends on it, and so does adding Other Emails; nothing else needs it. Other Emails (Settings, under Email) are confirmed the same way, each by its own link, and only a confirmed one signs in or gets reset links.
  • Usernames: 5 to 30 characters, counted the way a person would (an accented letter, or an emoji, even one made of several joined emoji, is one). Any characters you can see work: letters in any language, numbers, symbols, emoji, @, and spaces (extra spaces are tidied). Characters you can't see, or that change which way text runs, aren't allowed, since they could make one username pass for another. "Sam", "sam", and "ＳＡＭ" count as the same username, so only one of them can exist.
  • Signing in works with the username (in any capitals), the account's email, or one of its confirmed Other Emails, and Forgot Password works with any of those emails too (the link goes to the email typed). After too many wrong passwords, signing in pauses for a while; Forgot Password and passkeys still work.
  • Passwords: at least 8 characters and at most 256, and any characters work (spaces, symbols, emoji, other languages). The same password typed on another keyboard or device still matches. A new password (making an account, changing it, or resetting it) that has shown up in data breaches is refused. Passwords are never stored as they're typed.
  • Staying signed in: signing in (with the password or a passkey) lasts up to 24 hours, and 15 minutes without activity signs you out, with a counting-down notice in the last minute. Logout signs that device out. A device signed out from somewhere else (by Sign Out Everywhere, or a new password on another device) goes to the login page at its next request, and the login page says why. Logout with a change that hasn't reached the server yet (offline) asks first.
  • A new password: changing it in Settings (with the current one) signs out every other device and stops every Quick Entry icon; this device stays signed in. Resetting it with an emailed link (which works once, for an hour) signs out every device, stops every Quick Entry icon, and turns passkey sign-in off. Sign Out Everywhere signs out every device, that one too, and stops the icons.
  • Privacy Policy & Terms of Use updates: when they change, every account with a verified email gets an email saying so, with when the new version takes effect, what changed, and a link to it (a few accounts every few minutes, until all have it). An account made after the change already agreed to the new version, so it isn't emailed. The policy page carries its version (its Effective Date) and a short note on what changed, which the server reads once an hour.
  • Security notices: a password change is reported to the account's email, an email change to the old address (with the new address partly hidden; the new one gets its verification link), and a new passkey to the account's email. Emails are sent through Resend.
  • Changing the email or password, adding a passkey, or deleting the account needs the password. Deleting the account deletes all of its data, passkeys included.
  • Analytics: Google Analytics loads only when the device hasn't turned it off in Settings and the browser doesn't send Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track. The page addresses it gets leave out everything after the page's name, so private codes in links (like a reset or verification link's) never reach it. It never sees budget data.
  • Security: Prismal Budget uses reasonable security measures to protect accounts and their data. How they work isn't published here.

Passkeys (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello)

A passkey signs you in with your face, your fingerprint, or your device's PIN instead of your password. It's the web standard (WebAuthn) behind "sign in with Face ID", and it works with Face ID and Touch ID on Apple devices, Windows Hello, Android's fingerprint or face unlock, password managers, and security keys. Passkeys are optional, and the password keeps working either way, so a lost phone never locks anyone out.

  • The account's setting: passkey sign-in is unset, on, or off for each account. It starts unset, and adding a passkey turns it on.
  • Turning it on when signing in: turning on Turn on passkey sign-in on the login page, then signing in with the password, makes a passkey for that device and turns it on (or, if the device already has one of the account's passkeys, just turns it on). Yes, Turn It On tries again if it was cancelled, and No Thanks skips it for now without changing anything.
  • Signing in with a passkey: Sign In With A Passkey, or picking a passkey under the username box. The passkey says whose it is, so no username is needed. The device's check opens right away (the page keeps a sign-in ready, since Safari only opens it right in a tap). If passkey sign-in is off for the account, the page says so and turns the switch on, so signing in with the password turns it back on.
  • The device's last passkey: the device remembers which passkey it last signed in with or made (just the passkey's ID, which says nothing about the person, kept after signing out), when that passkey is on the device itself: one used from a phone (by its QR code) or a security key isn't remembered, and removing a passkey in Settings forgets it. Each time the login page shows, except right after Logout, Sign Out Everywhere, or deleting the account, it asks the device for that one passkey by itself, so signing back in is one step. A browser that only opens passkey requests in a tap (like Safari) says no at once, and the page carries on as usual; a passkey the server doesn't know anymore, or one for an account with passkey sign-in off, is forgotten so it isn't tried again, and so is one whose request was cancelled twice in a row (a device that lost the passkey, or that someone else uses, stops asking for it; the next passkey sign-in there is remembered again).
  • Turning it on or off in Settings: On and Off apply to the whole account. Off keeps the passkeys, but none of them can sign in until it's turned back on. Turn On needs at least one passkey; with none, the Turn On button adds one. Adding a passkey always turns it on.
  • Making one: the password is checked, then the device asks for the face, fingerprint, or PIN and saves the passkey (often in iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager, or Windows Hello, which may sync it to the person's other devices). Each passkey gets a name, like "iPhone" or "Windows", that can be changed when adding it. An account can have up to 20, and a device that already has one of the account's passkeys says so instead of making another.
  • What's stored: for each passkey, its public key, its ID, its name, the site it was made on, when it was added and last used, whether the device said it's synced, and a sign-in counter; for the account, the setting and a random ID that devices keep with its passkeys (it isn't the username or anything else about the person). The private key never leaves the device or password manager, and faces, fingerprints, and PINs never leave the device: the site never receives them.
  • Only on Prismal Budget: a passkey only works on the site it was made on, so a look-alike site can't use it. Which company made the device isn't asked for or stored.
  • Removing: Remove in Settings deletes a passkey from the server, so it can't sign in. Browsers that support it are told to stop offering it, and it can also be deleted in the device's password settings. A removed passkey that's still on a device gets "That passkey isn't on any account anymore".
  • A password reset turns it off, so a passkey someone else added stops working too. The owner checks the list in Settings, removes any that aren't theirs, and turns it back on.

16. How The Data Is Stored

Data lives in a Cloudflare D1 (SQLite) database. Each part of a budget is one table, holding one row per user whose content is the whole part as JSON text. When something changes, only the parts that changed are saved, each in one piece. If a part's stored copy can't be read when the budget opens, it's never overwritten, so it can still be recovered.

Every date in these tables is stored the same way whatever the device's language, region, or time zone (see Dates, time zones, and formats). Each device also keeps its own choices in the browser (localStorage), and they stay after signing out: the date format (prismal_date_format: mdy, dmy, or ymd, or none for the device's own) analytics (prismal_analytics), the kind of phone picked in a Steps for list (prismal_device), the ID of the passkey it last signed in with (prismal_last_passkey, which the login page tries by itself; |declined after the ID once a try was cancelled), whether music and sound effects are off (prismal_music and prismal_sound_effects: off, or none for on), the Quick Entry icons it made or used (prismal_quick_icons: the first 16 hex digits of the SHA-256 of each icon's key, up to 20, newest first; their entries are added when the budget opens there), and the day picked for Jeremy's Accounts card (prismal_jeremy_accounts_day, MM/DD/YYYY). A Quick Entry icon opened as an app keeps the Quick Entry page it opened on (prismal_quick_app) and when it was last left (prismal_app_left) in the app's sessionStorage.

17. How The Website Is Built

  • The website is plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with no build step, hosted on GitHub Pages at prismalbudget.org.
  • The budget's logic runs in the browser. When the budget opens, the website loads every table, runs the daily update, and works out everything that's computed (In Bank, the 28-day lines, Jeremy's message, the tracker, account balances). Every change goes through one function that runs changes one at a time, takes a snapshot first (and puts it back if anything fails, so half-made changes are never saved), refreshes the computed parts, logs the change, saves the tables that changed, and tells the page to redraw.
  • The server is a Cloudflare Worker (an API) with a D1 database. It handles accounts, sign-in (passwords and passkeys), storing tables, Quick Entry, reminders, cleaning up expired records, and emailing accounts about an updated Privacy Policy (a scheduled job every 5 minutes), email (through Resend), and downloading connected spreadsheets (which the page reads itself). It doesn't do budget math.
  • Saving safely: each account's budget has a revision number that goes up with every saved table. The page keeps the number it loaded (and each save's new one), and sends it with every save. The server writes a table only when the number still matches, all in one transaction; otherwise nothing is written, the reply is 409, and the page reloads with the newest budget and says the last change needs to be made again. While a page is on the screen, it asks for the number every 5 seconds (GET /api/data/revision, which also sums up the waiting quick entries), and when the budget changed elsewhere it loads the budget again in place and redraws (Live updates); a page coming back into view asks right away. A save that couldn't reach the server stays marked, is sent again when the connection comes back (and every 30 seconds), and leaving before then asks first.
  • Undo and Redo: a calendar change or quick entry gives runBudgetAction an undo label and the days it touches. Before and after the change, it keeps each of those days' own entry lines (❗️, ✖️, ⭕️, and temps; never the lines the budget writes itself), plus the Other Accounts' equity tables if the change touched them, and only the days that changed become the step. Undo puts each day's "before" lines back, but only while every one of those days still holds its "after" lines (Redo is the same the other way); then everything computed is rebuilt like after any change, and it's saved and logged like one. A step that no longer matches is refused and dropped. Steps are kept per tab in sessionStorage (prismal_undo_<username>), 25 at most, and cleared on sign-out. The Quick Entry page keeps its own list in memory, takes entries back with /api/quick/unqueue, and follows a waiting entry changed in its Waiting list (Undo takes back the entry as it is now).
  • Loading and updates: every page starts with Boot.js, which works out which page it is, sends signed-out visitors to the login page (except on public pages), and loads that page's scripts. GitHub Pages shows 404.html at any missing address, so that page marks itself (data-page="notfound") to be recognized. A browser that blocks site data gets a message saying how to allow it instead of a blank page.
  • Caching and releases: pages and Boot.js are always checked for a newer copy (Cloudflare sends them with Cache-Control: no-cache, so one that hasn't changed costs a quick "not modified" answer), so a release reaches everyone on their next page, with no reload. Every other file's address carries its fingerprint, the first 10 hex digits of the SHA-256 of its contents (like /Javascript/Layout.js?v=d669486b33), so browsers and Cloudflare keep their saved copy until that file really changes, a changed file's new address gets the new copy right away, and files that didn't change stay saved. Pages and stylesheets carry fingerprints in their links, and Boot.js carries them for the scripts and sprites it loads (FILE_VERSIONS, used through assetUrl); _tools/stamp.mjs writes them all (see Notes for developers).
  • Speed: the site's font, Bpmf Huninn, comes in two parts made from BpmfHuninn-Regular.ttf (which no page loads): its Latin letters, numbers, Greek, and symbols (Fonts/MainFont-Latin.woff2, 24 KB, preloaded by every page) and its Chinese, Japanese, and Bopomofo characters (Fonts/MainFont-More.woff2, 1.8 MB), which a browser only downloads for a page that shows one of them (CSS unicode-range). The header buttons', tabs', and other buttons' icons are small WebP images in Resources/Icons/ (under 6 KB each). The background picture (Resources/Background.webp, 5 KB) is built into stylesheet.css, with its average color under it, so it paints in a page's very first frame instead of a moment later, and the starfield gets a head start: Boot.js, which runs first, starts the starfield's worker and loads and decodes its star image (a 34 KB WebP) while the rest of the page loads, and Starfield Setup.js hands both over, so the stars are drawn as soon as the page shows. Google Analytics loads after the page has, so it never holds the page up, and pages slide in when they've loaded (or after 1.5 seconds at most). While a page slides out, the browser already fetches the next page, Boot.js, and the page's scripts (<link rel="prefetch">, in browsers that have it), so they're on hand when it opens, which matters most the first time a page opens after an update (the page and Boot.js are still checked for a newer copy then, as always, so updates show right away). The next page opens as soon as the last one is out of sight (85% of the way through its slide, which eases to a stop past the screen's edge) instead of at the slide's very end. The server makes its tables once per Worker instance instead of on every request. Every change works out the whole budget again, so that work is kept small: each day's lines are read once and remembered by their text (readLine), so all of History isn't split up again on every change, and the Recurring page's Yearly counts each recurring cost's days with arithmetic (countRecurringHits) instead of checking every day to December 31. With over a year of History, a whole change takes a few milliseconds on a computer. Jeremy's page works out its cards only while it's open, and only the ones that are on; a change anywhere else only works out his message on Home. A budget with no % entries skips working them out, and one with them walks its days once per change. The sounds (Resources/Sounds/, small MP3s, about 230 KB together) are fetched at the first tap or key press, at their fingerprinted addresses, and the music's only once it starts.

19. Why It Works This Way

  • A calendar instead of categories first: knowing which day the balance dips is what keeps you from overdrafting; the tracker covers categories.
  • Entries are lines of text: it's easy to read and store, and a day's whole story is in one cell.
  • Refraction types: not every dollar that moves is spending. Hidden covers money outside the budget's account; Transfer covers money that only changes places.
  • One entry per title and type per day, one recurring entry per title, type, and Other Account, one tracker row per title and transfer setting: there's never a question of which one a change, a temp, or a search means.
  • Temps and blockers: changing one day of a recurring entry shouldn't change every day, and days that passed should never change.
  • Accounts are linked through the entry's title: the account picked is kept after it ("Payday → Savings"), so there's no extra field to keep in sync, and moving, combining, or deleting an entry, or a recurring entry landing, carries the link along by itself. Only a picked account links, never a title that happens to be an account's name. A balance is a starting amount plus those entries, so typing in what's already in an account never makes up an entry (or a gain) that didn't happen.
  • Budget math in the browser: changes feel instant, the server stays simple and cheap, and the math lives in one place.
  • Whole tables saved at once, with a snapshot before every change: a change either fully happens or doesn't happen at all.
  • The Quick Entry key can only add: a phone left unlocked can't be used to read the budget (just In Bank today, the lowest ahead, and the Other Accounts' balances). Its prisms are only what the person saved there.
  • Quick Entry's balances from what the budget leaves for it: the icon can't open the budget, so each time the budget is worked out it leaves each account's changes still to come next to its balances, and In Bank on each day ahead, and the page counts the ones whose day has come. So the page shows what the budget will say once it's opened, without seeing anything else in it.
  • Prisms kept with the account, not the phone: a new icon (after a password change, say) or another phone has the same ones, and a phone's home screen apps can lose what they keep in the browser.
  • Passkeys next to the password, not instead of it: a lost or replaced phone never locks anyone out, and the emailed reset link stays the way back in. Passkeys can't be phished or reused on another site, and nothing about a face or fingerprint ever reaches the server.
  • Passkeys only when asked for: signing in with a password never stops to ask about passkeys unless the switch was on, and skipping one never turns passkey sign-in off for the account (only Settings does).
  • A revision check instead of "last save wins": each table is saved whole, so a page holding an older copy (another device, or a tab left open) could quietly erase changes made elsewhere. Refusing that save and reloading costs one redo at worst, and never loses data.
  • A starting balance as an ordinary entry: it shows on the calendar and can be changed or deleted like anything else, instead of being a hidden number. A Transfer changes In Bank without counting as income.
  • Usernames with any characters you can see, but no invisible ones: people can use their name in any language, or an emoji. Characters you can't see, or that flip which way text runs, could make two different usernames look the same, so they're refused, and look-alike forms count as the same username.
  • Length and a breach check instead of "one number and one symbol": rules like that make passwords harder to remember without making them much harder to guess. Refusing passwords attackers already have on their lists does more, and any characters (even a whole sentence) are welcome.
  • Undo by day, and only while the day still matches: each part of the budget is saved whole, so putting back an old copy of a whole table could erase changes made since. Putting back only the entries on the days a change touched, and only while those days are as it left them, can't.
  • Analytics with the private parts left out: page addresses without their ?query, nothing about the budget, none of Google's advertising features, and off for anyone whose browser asks or who turns it off.
  • Floating boxes: the page never jumps around while you work.
  • Floating boxes and zoomed days are part of the page once they're open: a box kept on the screen at its own size couldn't be zoomed in on to read, or scrolled to when it was taller than the screen. Opening where the screen is, then moving and zooming with the page, gives both: it's in sight when it opens, and the page's own zoom and scroll work on it.
  • Live updates by asking every few seconds: a page asks the server whether the budget changed (one small request) instead of keeping a connection open, which needs nothing more on the server and stops by itself when the page is off the screen. Loading the budget again in place, and only while nothing's being changed here, means another device's change never lands in the middle of yours.
  • Titles from today on stay with what lands them: a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet writes its title on each day it lands, so a new title on one day would let it land there again next to the renamed one. Days that passed can't be landed on anymore, so any entry there can be renamed.
  • Savings and Cash in every budget: most people keep both outside their bank account, so they're ready to pick for a transfer or for spending cash (a Hidden entry), without setting them up first.
  • Hidden entries change an account by their own amount, Transfers by the opposite: a Transfer is one movement seen from two sides (out of In Bank and into savings), while a Hidden entry happens in the other account itself, so its sign already says what happened there.
  • Temps let go when their recurring entry ends: a temp only keeps its recurring entry from landing on its day, so once the recurring entry can't land there anymore, the temp is just an entry.
  • Days stored as text, the same way everywhere: a budget day is a calendar day, not a moment in time, so a time zone or a date format can never move an entry. Only what's shown and typed follows the person's format.
  • The browser's translation instead of translated pages: every major browser can translate a page, on the person's own device and into their language, and it keeps up with every change to the site with nothing to maintain. Keeping the person's entries, names, amounts, and dates out of it means their budget never changes under them.
  • Connected spreadsheets work like recurring entries: they're written from today on and taken off and written again with every change, so a spreadsheet that changes or drops an entry changes the calendar, while a day changed here (a temp) and days that passed never change under you.
  • The server downloads a spreadsheet, and the browser reads it: the page can't fetch other sites itself, but reading a file takes work, and the budget's other work already runs in the browser. The server just passes the file along, never keeping it, so it costs little and holds nothing.
  • Shared with admin@prismalbudget.org only for whoever shared it: Prismal Budget's Google account can open everything shared with it, so a link alone is never enough: the spreadsheet is only read for the account whose verified email (or a confirmed Other Email) owns or shared it. An Other Email is confirmed the same way as the account's email, by opening a link sent to it, so an account can only add an email whose inbox it can open.
  • A verified email for spreadsheets and Other Emails, and only for them: that match only means something when the account's email is really its owner's, so connecting and reading spreadsheets wait for a verified email, and so does adding Other Emails (which connect spreadsheets too). Nothing else does (signing in, the budget, and reset links work the same), so an email typed wrong never locks anyone out of their budget.
  • A zoomed day laid out big instead of scaled up: a phone draws a scaled-up element from its small picture, so a tiny day scaled to fill the screen came out blurry. Laying it out at the zoomed size draws its text at full size, and the scale only runs during the quick grow and shrink.
  • A phone's zoomed day scrolls in an area as big as the screen: a box that scrolls cuts off whatever goes past its edges, so a day that scrolled by itself couldn't show its lines past it. Its content covers the whole screen instead, and draws the day inside it, so a day with more lines than fit scrolls them together with the day.
  • Every day zooms to the same size: when a day zoomed less to fit its widest entry, one day came out bigger or smaller than the next, and a day with a long title had small text. One size for every day keeps the text easy to read and swiping from day to day steady; a title too long for the day ends in "...", and its options show it whole.
  • Changing History keeps the balances: History is what already happened, and today's In Bank and account balances are what the bank says now. Fixing a typo or a wrong amount in the past shouldn't change them, so the change is made to the entry only, and an Other Account's starting amount takes up the difference.
  • The site's own date picker: each device's own looked different, and some only scroll through dates, so a birth date took a long time to reach. The site's matches the calendar, has a month box and a year box to type in, and the date box still takes a typed date.
  • The last passkey tried by itself, except after signing out on purpose: most sign-ins on a device are its owner coming back, so asking for that passkey right away saves a step. Logout (or Sign Out Everywhere) often means someone else is about to sign in, so then the page waits.
  • Placeholders are deleted when their day passes, not kept: a placeholder is a guess about a day still to come. Once the day is over, what really happened is what belongs in the budget (and History), so the guess goes, and In Bank from that day on follows without it. Until then, it counts like any entry, so the days ahead show the money it stands for.
  • Two entries that become one are a placeholder only if both were: otherwise a real amount added into a placeholder (or a placeholder landing on a real entry) would be deleted along with it, and real money would vanish from the budget.
  • A placeholder is a mark on its emoji: keeping it as a ⏳ at the end of the entry's emoji means it moves, combines, and is saved with the entry with nothing extra to keep in step, and every rule that reads an entry's type and mark reads it the same way.
  • Other Accounts look 6 months ahead: the calendar's last day is never more than four weeks off, which says little about a savings goal or a card being paid down. Six months counts what's coming the way the daily update will fill those days in, recurring entries and spreadsheets too.
  • A % entry works from the balance as its day starts: every entry on a day then works from the same number, whatever order they're in, so two % entries on one day never depend on each other, and the next day's start from what that day left.
  • A % entry stays what it came to once its day passes, and merging makes a plain amount: a day that happened shouldn't change because a balance changed later, and two entries that became one have no single percent to keep.
  • A % goal is fixed when it's set: a goal that moved with the balance it's measured against could never be reached.
  • Quick entries from another device wait on Home: on a shared budget, the other person sees what was added and decides, while a device's own icon (its owner's own entries) still adds by itself.
  • Sounds mix with other audio, and pick up again after an interruption: on an iPhone, the page's ambient audio session never stops the person's own audio, so there's no need to stay silent for the rest of a visit after something (like a call) takes the speaker; the next tap plays them again.
  • Fingerprinted files instead of a reload per release: pages are always fresh, so they always point at exactly the right files, and a release only downloads again the files that changed.

21. Glossary

In Bank
The money in the budget's bank account at the end of a day, worked out from the last History balance and each day's entries.
Entry
One line on a day: an emoji, an amount, and a title, like ❗️ -$6.50 Coffee.
Refraction type
How an entry affects the budget: Regular (❗️), Hidden (✖️), or Transfer (⭕️).
Recurring entry
An entry that repeats every N days, weeks, or months, written onto the calendar for you; it starts with ✔️.
Unique entry
An entry you added or changed yourself.
Temporary recurring entry (temp)
A unique entry made from a recurring entry on one day (❗️✔️, ✖️✔️, ⭕️✔️). It keeps the recurring entry of its type from being written again that day, and becomes a plain unique entry once that recurring entry can't land there anymore.
Placeholder
An entry that stands in for one you expect, like an estimate, made with the Placeholder switch. It counts like any entry, shows in yellow, and is deleted once its day passes.
% entry
An entry for an Other Account whose amount is a percent of that account's balance as its day starts, worked out again with every change until its day passes.
Goal
An amount for In Bank, the Total, or an Other Account to reach (by a day, if it has one), on Jeremy's page, with how far along it is and what reaching it takes.
Blocker
A $0.00 temp left when a recurring (or spreadsheet) entry's day is moved or deleted.
Connected spreadsheet
A spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel, and others) connected on the Spreadsheets page, whose entries land on the calendar from today on behind a ➕ (➕❗️, ➕✖️, ➕⭕️) and follow it as it changes.
Upcoming (Future Dates)
Entries dated after the calendar, waiting for their day to join it.
History
Past weeks, kept as they were, newest first.
Transfer
A ⭕️ entry: money changing places (like between In Bank and an Other Account, or depositing cash) without being gained or spent. It changes In Bank, but isn't a cost or gain.
Max
An Other Account's optional limit, like a credit card's, shown after its balance: "$500.00/$2000.00".
Live updates
Another device's changes showing up on an open page within about 5 seconds, without a reload.
Other Account
An account outside the budget's bank account, like Savings or Cash (every budget has both). Its balance is its starting amount (set by typing in its balance on Other Accounts) plus its entries' changes: the opposite of each Transfer, and each Hidden entry's own amount.
Tracker row and alias
A category in the tracker, and an entry title it counts (all of the amounts, only costs, or only gains).
Transfers (tracker row)
The tracker row that adds up every transfer, so they never land in Undefined.
Undefined
The tracker row that collects titles no other row fully counts (never transfers).
Uncommon recurring payment
A recurring entry that lands every 3 months or less often.
Daily update
The catch-up that runs the first time the budget opens on a new day.
Jeremy
The character on the home page who says what to keep in mind, with a daily affirmation; his card opens his page of cards (see Jeremy's Page).
Purpose (Needs, Wants, Future)
What a title's money is for, which every entry with the title shares; entry forms ask for it once Jeremy's Needs, Wants, And Future card is on.
Quick Entries To Add
Home's card of entries another device's Quick Entry icon added, for whoever has the budget open to add or delete.
Starting Balance
A new budget's first entry: a ⭕️ Transfer on the day it's set up, holding what was in the bank then.
Revision
A number that goes up with every saved budget table. A save from a page with an older number is refused, so it can't overwrite newer changes.
Passkey
A sign-in key a device makes for Prismal Budget and unlocks with a face, fingerprint, or PIN (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello). The server keeps only its public half.
Quick Entry key
The secret in a home screen icon's link that lets it add entries (and take back ones still waiting) and keep prisms, and nothing else, without logging in.
Prism
A saved entry on the Quick Entry page that fills in New Entry with what it keeps (a title, an amount, a type, or an Other Account), leaving the rest to fill in.
Session
One signed-in device or browser, good for up to 24 hours. Logout ends it.
Undo step
One change Undo can take back: the days it touched, with each day's own entries from before and after it.
Sprite
The site's own image for ❗️ (red), ✖️ (purple), ⭕️ (green), ✔️ (orange), or ➕ (yellow), shown in place of the emoji.
Starfield
The animated stars behind every page. The pointer, taps, and hidden keyboard keys move them, and the device's reduced motion setting turns it off (see The starfield background).
Verified email
An account email whose emailed link was opened, which shows the address is really the account owner's. Connecting spreadsheets needs one.
Other Emails
Other emails of an account's owner, added in Settings (under Email) and confirmed the same way as a verified email. A confirmed one signs in to the account and gets its reset links, and the spreadsheets its Google account shares with admin@prismalbudget.org can be connected.
Date picker
The small calendar the calendar button beside a date box opens, to pick a day instead of typing it.
Date format
How dates are shown and typed on a device: MM/DD/YYYY, DD/MM/YYYY, or YYYY-MM-DD.
Fingerprint
A short hash of a file's contents in its address (?v=…), so browsers keep the file until it changes.
Change Log
The record of every change to the budget.

24. Common Questions

Does Prismal Budget connect to my bank account?
No. Prismal Budget never connects to a bank. Entries are typed in (New Entry, or a Quick Entry icon on a phone's home screen), fill in by themselves from recurring entries, or come from a spreadsheet you connect. To match the bank, tap a day's In Bank on the calendar and type what the bank says: the difference goes on that day as a Catchup transfer.
What does In Bank mean in Prismal Budget?
In Bank is how much will be in your budget's bank account at the end of a day: the In Bank of the day before, plus that day's Regular gains, minus its Regular costs, plus its transfers. Hidden entries (like spending cash) never change it. Every day on the four-week calendar shows its own In Bank, and a day whose In Bank is below $0 turns red.
What's the difference between Regular, Hidden, and Transfer entries?
Regular (a red circle) is an everyday cost or gain: it changes In Bank and counts in the tracker. Hidden (a purple circle) is money spent or received outside your budget's account, like spending cash: it counts as a cost or gain, but In Bank stays the same. Transfer (a green circle) is money moving between your own accounts, like moving $100 into savings: it changes In Bank, but isn't a cost or gain.
How do I add an entry to a past day or a future day?
Tap the day on Home's calendar (or a day in History) to zoom in on it, then tap its round + button: New Entry opens with that day in its date box. Or use New Entry on Home and type or pick the date. A day after the four-week calendar goes into Upcoming until its day joins the calendar.
Can I change an entry's title?
Yes. Tap the entry on a zoomed day (or in Search's results, an account's Entries, or Upcoming) and change its Title. Any entry on a day before today can be renamed, History included. From today on, an entry from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet keeps its title; change it on the Recurring Entries or Spreadsheets page instead.
Can two people or two devices use the same budget at once?
Yes. Sign in to the same account on each device, and each one shows the others' changes within about 5 seconds, with no reload. A partner can also add entries with a Quick Entry icon set up from your account; entries from another device's icon wait on Home, in Quick Entries To Add, for whoever has the budget open to add or delete.
Which accounts does every Prismal Budget budget have?
Savings and Cash. Both are on the Other Accounts page from the start, and can be renamed but not deleted. You can add others, like a credit card (with its monthly interest and its limit, called Max) or an HSA.
How far ahead does Prismal Budget look?
The calendar shows four weeks, starting with this week, and Lowest in bank after today also counts the 28 days after the calendar. Other Accounts shows each balance six months ahead, and Jeremy's Accounts card shows every balance on any day up to 10 years ahead. Search can look up to two years ahead for recurring entries.
What is a placeholder in Prismal Budget?
An entry that stands in for one you expect but don't know yet, like an estimate for groceries. It shows in yellow and counts like any entry until its day passes; then the budget deletes it the next time it opens, so it's never left in History.
Does Prismal Budget work offline?
Partly. A change made while offline is kept on the page and saved as soon as the connection comes back (the page tries every 30 seconds, and leaving before then asks first). Signing in and loading the budget need the internet.
Is there a Prismal Budget app?
Prismal Budget runs in any web browser, on a phone or a computer, with nothing to install. A Quick Entry icon on a phone's home screen opens a New Entry form with no sign-in, and opens like an app of its own. The site can also be installed from the browser, which opens Home.
How do I delete my Prismal Budget account?
Settings, Delete Account, with your password. It deletes the account and all of its data at once. Download My Data (in Settings, under Your Data And Privacy) saves everything first, if you'd like a copy.
