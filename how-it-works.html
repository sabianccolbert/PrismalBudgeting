How Prismal Budget Works

Pages, features, rules, and data

What Prismal Budget Is

Prismal Budget (prismalbudget.org) is a web budgeting app built around a four-week calendar. Every day on the calendar lists that day's entries (costs and gains), and the budget adds them up into In Bank: how much money will be in your budget's bank account at the end of each day. Recurring entries like paychecks and bills fill themselves in, and so do entries from spreadsheets you connect (like a list of bills you already keep in Google Sheets or Excel), so you can see days where your balance would go negative before they happen, and the lowest your balance gets in the 28 days after the calendar. Prismal Budget is for people 18 and older.

This page explains every page, feature, rule, and data format in detail, in plain words, with exact formats and examples. Prismal Budget started as a Google Sheets budget; the website keeps its ideas (like refraction types, temporary recurring entries, and tracker aliases), but some rules changed on purpose, and this page describes the website as it works now.

Things Every Page Shares

  • Header: the page title and image buttons (Back, Home, Menu, Settings, Logout, depending on the page; Logout only shows while signed in, so the public pages don't offer it to visitors).
  • Gradient borders: chosen, hovered, and focused controls (like a primary button, the picked refraction type, or the box being typed in) have a red-orange diagonal gradient border.
  • Pill switches: on/off choices (agreeing to the Privacy Policy & Terms of Use, Turn on passkey sign-in, a tracker row's Count ⭕️ Transfers, and each connected spreadsheet's On/Off on the Spreadsheets page) are pill-shaped switches: off is a dark pill with its knob at the left, on fills with the red-orange gradient with its knob at the right. Tapping the words flips them too. Checkboxes that pick rows from a list (search results, and the rows to delete in Edit) stay checkboxes.
  • Animated starfield background and page transitions: pages slide in and out instead of flashing. The starfield's stars carry over from page to page, and react to the pointer, taps, and (secretly) the keyboard: see The starfield background.
  • Footer: trademark note, contact, Privacy Policy & Terms of Use, and How Prismal Budget Works.
  • Help button (the word Help, in a small pill) at the very bottom of Home, Other Accounts, Recurring Entries, Tracker, History, Paycheck Calculator, Spreadsheets, and Settings (its Help covers the account's email and birth date). It opens that page's help in a box that floats over a dimmed page; ×, Escape, or tapping outside closes it.
  • Floating boxes: boxes like New Entry, an entry's options, and the panels for recurring entries, tracker rows, and accounts float over the page, so nothing under them moves. So do Search (with its results under its form) and Help. Each one zooms out of the button (or entry) that opened it and back into it when it closes, and has a purple to blue diagonal background. One can open over another (a connected spreadsheet's ➕ boxes open over its panel): the one under it waits, dimmed, and comes back when the top one closes; Escape closes only the top one. Leaving the page closes them right away (and zooms a zoomed day back out), so the browser's Back button never brings a page back with one still open.
  • Signed out, a page that needs signing in goes to the login page. The site's own address shows the front page instead.
  • Without JavaScript, pages other than this one and the front page only ask you to turn it on, since nothing works without it. The same goes for a browser that blocks site data (cookies and site data turned off): signing in is kept there, so the login and budget pages say how to allow it, while the front page, this page, the Privacy Policy, Quick Entry, and Not Found still work.
  • Less motion: when the device asks for reduced motion, pages appear without sliding, links go right away, and the starfield doesn't run (see Reduced motion).
  • Notes (like "Undone: …") show at the bottom of the screen for a few seconds; two at once stack instead of covering each other.
  • Missing images show their words instead of a broken-image icon, so a button still says what it does.
  • Sprites: the five special emoji show as the site's own small images on every page, the same on every device, each in its own color: ❗️ Regular is red, ✖️ Hidden purple, ⭕️ Transfer green, ✔️ Recurring orange, and ➕ (a connected spreadsheet's) yellow (see Sprites). The text underneath keeps the emoji.
  • Other languages: the site is written in English, and the browser's own translation (in Chrome, Edge, Safari, or Firefox) shows it in another language. The person's entries, names, amounts, and dates are marked to stay just as they were typed (translate="no"), and the site's own words translate. Settings says how to turn it on.
  • Dates show, and are typed, in the device's date format (like 10/05/2026, 05/10/2026, or 2026-10-05), or the one picked in Settings. They're always stored the same way (see Dates, time zones, and formats).
  • Links within the site slide the page out first. Links to a spot on the same page (like this page's Contents) just scroll there, and links opened in a new tab (Ctrl or ⌘ click, or the middle button) open there as usual.
  • Idle sign-out warning: on signed-in pages, the last minute before the 15-minute idle sign-out shows a notice counting down; any tap, key, or scroll keeps you signed in. A page left in the background just signs out when it's opened again, and the login page then says why.
  • Saving safely: every change saves right away. If the server can't be reached, the change is kept on the page and sent again when the connection comes back (and every 30 seconds until then), and leaving the page before then asks first. If the budget was saved on another device or tab since the page loaded, the page reloads with the newest budget instead of saving over it (see How the website is built).

Login

  • Create an account: a username (5 to 30 characters, and any characters you can see work: letters in any language, numbers, symbols, emoji, and spaces), an email address, your birth date (Prismal Budget is for people 18 and older: under 18, no account is made, and the server refuses it too), a password of at least 8 characters (any characters, typed twice), and agreeing to the Privacy Policy & Terms of Use. Usernames and passwords follow the rules in Accounts, sign-in, and security; in short, "Sam" and "sam" are the same username, and a password known from data breaches is refused.
  • Signing in: the username (in any capitals) or the account's email, and the password. After too many wrong passwords, signing in pauses for a while.
  • Verifying the email: a new account's email gets a link to verify it (it works for 24 hours), and "Account created!" says so. The link opens the login page, signed in or not, which checks it and says whether the email is verified, with Open Spreadsheets (signed in on that device) or Sign In next. Connecting spreadsheets needs a verified email; nothing else does (see Accounts, sign-in, and security). The link that confirms one of the Spreadsheets page's Other Emails opens the login page the same way ("Confirm Your Email").
  • Forgot Password: enter the account's email and a one-time reset link is emailed (it works for an hour).
  • Reset form: the emailed link opens the login page to choose a new password, which signs out every device and turns passkey sign-in off.
  • Sign In With A Passkey: signs in with Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, a fingerprint, or the device's PIN, with no username or password to type (see Passkeys). Browsers with passkey autofill also list the site's passkeys under the username box.
  • Turn on passkey sign-in: a switch under the password, named for the device (like "(Face ID or Touch ID)" on an iPhone or "(Windows Hello)" on Windows). When it's on, signing in with the password goes to "Turn On Passkey Sign-In?" and makes a passkey for this device right away (Safari only makes one right in a tap, so there it says to press Yes, Turn It On). Off, signing in goes straight to the budget, and nothing about passkeys changes. It doesn't need to be on to sign in with a passkey.
  • The switch and the passkey button only show in browsers that can use passkeys.
  • Signing in keeps you signed in for up to 24 hours, but 15 minutes without activity signs you out. Logout (and the idle sign-out) signs that device out. After a sign-out the site did (15 idle minutes, a session that ended, or Sign Out Everywhere), or a Quick Entry icon that stopped working, the login page says why.

Home

  • Getting Started: a new budget starts at $0, so a Welcome card at the top walks through three steps: the starting balance (what's in the bank now, after today's purchases so far, and optionally what's in savings), recurring entries (a link to Recurring Entries), and a daily reminder (a link to Settings). Setting the starting balance adds a ⭕️ Transfer titled "Starting Balance" on today (it changes In Bank without counting as a gain) and, for savings, a ✖️ Hidden gain for the default account, shown as "→ Savings" (it puts the money in that account without changing In Bank; like any Hidden gain, it shows in that day's Gains and the tracker counts it). Both are ordinary entries that can be changed. The card goes away once the starting balance (or any History) and a recurring entry are there, or when it's hidden with × (remembered per account on that device).
  • The buttons above the calendar: on phones, Undo at the top left with Redo under it, a big round + (New Entry, with its name under it) in the middle, and 🔎 Search at the top right with Quick Entry under it. On wider screens they're one row: Undo at the very left, then Redo, New Entry in the middle, Quick Entry, and Search at the very right. Then the four-week calendar.
  • Search (see Search): the 🔎 Search button opens it, floating over the page.
  • Under the calendar (after a divider), side by side on wider screens: Lowest in bank after today (the lowest In Bank from today through the 28 days after the calendar), Dave (a character who reads your budget and says what to keep in mind, with a new affirmation every day; see the calendar), and Upcoming (entries dated after the calendar).
  • Undo and Redo take back calendar changes and quick entries the budget added, newest first, then put them back. They cover New Entry; an entry's Set to, Add to it, Move to, and Delete; the starting balance; and quick entries. Each button says what it would do (like "Undo: added Coffee (-$4.50) on 10/05"), and a note says what was undone or redone. On a computer, Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac) undoes, and Ctrl+Shift+Z (⌘⇧Z) or Ctrl+Y redoes, except while typing in a box or with a box open. Undo never erases anything changed since: if a day it would change was changed some other way in the meantime (like on another device), it says so and lets that step go, and the one before it is next. Each tab remembers its last 25 steps while it's open (going to another page and back keeps them), and signing out forgets them. Every undo and redo is in the Change Logs.
  • Zooming a day: tap a day to zoom in on it, then tap an entry to change its amount (Set to, or Add to it), its refraction type, or its Other Account, move it to another day, or delete it. Tapping the day's In Bank sets it to what the bank says (see Entries). On a phone (a touch screen, or a window 760 pixels wide or less), the day fills almost the whole screen, centered over a dimmed page, with all of its entries showing, its color at the top fading to dark at the bottom like every day. It's laid out at that size (its text, sprites, and spacing grow with it) instead of being a tiny day scaled up, so everything in it stays sharp; it only scales while it grows out of the day and shrinks back into it. On a computer, it doubles in size. Its small v, in its very top right corner (about 22 pixels on screen however far it's zoomed), or a tap anywhere outside it, zooms back out.
  • Other ways to look closer: two fingers pinching or stretching on one day zoom (and turn and move) just that day, or across days the whole calendar, which settles back when they let go. On a computer, holding the mouse button on a day magnifies it, and sliding across days while holding magnifies each one. Home's Help lists these too.
  • Picking dates: 📅 next to any date box (on Home, Recurring Entries, the Quick Entry page, and Search's From and To) opens the device's own date picker, starting on the date that's typed. And while a date box is waiting (it glows, and the calendar gets a dashed outline), tapping a day on the calendar fills it in.

Recurring Entries, History, Other Accounts, Tracker

These pages show their data as tables. Recurring Entries and Tracker have New Entry and Edit buttons: tap a row to change or delete it; Edit shows a ≡ bar to drag rows into order, + Section buttons to add section headings between rows, and checkboxes to delete rows. Other Accounts has only Edit, which adds accounts (New Account), drags them into order, and deletes them. Each page is described in its own section below.

Spreadsheets

  • Connected Spreadsheets (their own page, Spreadsheets, in the menu): each spreadsheet is a button with its name and how many entries it lands from today on (tap it to change it), an On/Off pill switch to its right, and Delete at the very right, with a "Needs a look" box under it when a cell can't be read or a recurring entry keeps one of its entries off. Add Spreadsheet connects another (up to 10). Connecting one needs a verified email: until the account's email is verified, a note says so (with Send Verification Email) and Add Spreadsheet is off. Other Emails, under it, lists the emails of other Google accounts the spreadsheets are shared from, each confirmed or waiting (with Send Again), with Remove; Add Email (once the account's email is verified) emails one a link to confirm it. See Connected spreadsheets.

Settings

  • Daily Reminder: the time of day, and the time zone it uses (see Daily reminders).
  • Push Notifications: always a Turn On or Turn Off button for this device (with Send Test while it's on). Where push can't work on the device (the server isn't set up for it, the browser can't, an iPhone or iPad not opened from its Home Screen, or notifications are blocked), the status says why, and so does Turn On.
  • Calendar Link: a private link for Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or other calendar apps; it can be copied, replaced with a new one, or turned off.
  • Language And Dates: how to have the browser translate the site, and this device's date format: its own (from its language and region), MM/DD/YYYY, DD/MM/YYYY, or YYYY-MM-DD. It's kept on the device, even after signing out, and works without the server.
  • Email: add or change it (needs the password). It says whether the email is verified. A new email isn't, until the link it's sent is opened (the old address gets a security notice); until then, Send Verification Email sends a new link. Coming back to Settings after opening the link shows it verified.
  • Birth Date: the birth date given when signing up, which can be fixed here if it was typed wrong (18 or older; never younger). An account made before birth dates has only an age, and can add its birth date here.
  • Passkeys (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello): turn passkey sign-in On or Off for the account; see each passkey (its name, when it was added and last used, and whether it's synced) and remove it; or add one for this or another device (needs the password). With no passkeys yet, adding the first one (the Turn On button) is what turns it on. See Passkeys.
  • Password: change it with the current password and the new one (typed twice; at least 8 characters, any characters, not the current one, and not one known from data breaches). It signs out every other device and turns off Quick Entry icons; this device stays signed in, and a security notice is emailed. "Forgot It?" emails a one-time reset link to the account's email instead.
  • Signed-In Devices: how many Quick Entry icons work, with Turn Off Icons (they stop at once; entries already made with them are still added), and Sign Out Everywhere, which signs out every device and browser (this one too) and turns off the icons, for a lost phone or a computer that isn't yours.
  • Your Data And Privacy: Download My Data saves everything stored for the account as a JSON file, including when each signed-in device signed in (passwords, keys, session IDs, and private link codes are left out), and Analytics On This Device turns Google Analytics off or on for that device (it's always off in browsers that send Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track).
  • Delete Account: needs the password and a confirmation; it deletes the account and every bit of its data.

2. The Starfield Background (And Its Hidden Controls)

Every page of Prismal Budget has a moving starfield behind it: up to 400 small stars (fewer on smaller screens) that drift, twinkle (each one fades out and comes back, now and then with a white spark), and are joined by faint lines, like constellations, when they're near each other. It's only decoration: it never touches the budget, and nothing about it leaves the device.

Moving The Stars With A Pointer Or A Finger

  • Moving the mouse, or dragging a finger, gives the stars near it energy: they're pulled toward it and pushed away from it (harder the faster it moves), so they swirl around it, and a ring follows it.
  • Tapping or clicking anywhere pokes the stars: the ones nearby burst away from that spot, and a ring spreads out from it.
  • A star that drifts off one edge comes back on the other, except right around a moving pointer, where the stars bounce off the edges instead.

Hidden Keyboard Controls

On a computer (or a tablet or phone with a keyboard), these keys move the stars, on any page. Holding a key down keeps it going. They work even while typing in a box (typing a title nudges the stars too), and they never change anything in the budget.

Q E Z XPush them diagonally: up and left, up and right, down and left, or down and right.
Y U I, H J K, B N MMagnets on a 3 × 3 grid of the screen, laid out like the keys: Y is the top left, J the middle, and M the bottom right. The stars are pulled toward that spot and orbit around it.

The Not Found Page's Starfield Controls

The Not Found (404) page, which shows at any address that doesn't exist, hides a control panel far below its first screen, so the page looks empty until it's scrolled down:

  • Controller: a box for typing the keys above (it empties itself as you go).
  • Readouts: live numbers for how long each frame takes to work out (Misc), the pointer's speed, and the ring and poke timers.
  • Attract (Strength, Radius, and Scale), Clamp, Repel (Strength, Radius, and Scale), and Poke: sliders, each with a number box and − and + buttons (hold one to keep stepping), for how the pointer moves the stars. Attract sets how hard and how far the pointer pulls them in, and Scale how sharply that fades with distance; Repel is the same for pushing them away; Clamp is the stars' top speed; and Poke is how strong a tap's burst is. The settings are saved on that device and used on every page.

Across Pages, And Out Of The Way

  • The same stars carry over from page to page: when a page is left, the stars, where the pointer was, and the slider settings are saved in the browser on that device (constellationStars and constellationMeta), and the next page picks them up, fitted to its screen. Clearing the browser's site data starts a new starfield.
  • It runs in a background thread (a Web Worker drawing on an OffscreenCanvas; a browser without one runs it on the page instead), pauses while the page is hidden or sliding to another page, and shortens its lines on a slow device to stay smooth.
  • It shows right away when a page opens: the background picture is part of the site's stylesheet, so it's there in the page's first frame, and the starfield starts loading (its engine and its star image) the moment a page starts, so the stars are drawn as soon as the page shows instead of blinking in a moment later.

Reduced Motion (Accessibility)

Prismal Budget follows the device's own reduced motion setting: on iPhone and iPad, Settings > Accessibility > Motion > Reduce Motion; on Android, Settings > Accessibility > Remove animations; on Windows, Settings > Accessibility > Visual effects > Animation effects (off); and on a Mac, System Settings > Accessibility > Display > Reduce motion. With it on:

  • the starfield is hidden and doesn't run at all (nothing is drawn or worked out, which saves battery too);
  • pages appear without sliding, and links go right away;
  • floating boxes (New Entry, Help, Search, and the rest) open and close without zooming, and a day zooms in and out without easing into it;
  • smaller animations are skipped too, like the switches sliding, Dave's tip glowing, and a connected spreadsheet's form growing in.

There's no separate setting for it in Prismal Budget: the device's own setting is used everywhere, so it's the same in every app and site.

3. Entries And Refraction Types

An entry is one line of text: an emoji (its "sprite"), an amount, and a title, separated by spaces.

❗️ -$6.50 Coffee
✔️ $1200.00 Paycheck
⭕️ -$100.00 Savings

Costs are negative and gains are positive. Amounts are written like -$1234.56 (no thousands commas). When you type one, a $ and thousands commas are fine, and a comma before the last one or two digits is a decimal point, the way phones in many countries type it: 4,50 is $4.50, and 1.234,50 is $1234.50. An amount can be up to $1,000,000,000, cost or gain.

Titles are compared without regard to capital letters, extra spaces, or punctuation, so "Coffee", "coffee", and "COFFEE!" are the same title. Titles can be in any language: letters and numbers in any script count, so "Кофе" and "Чай" are two titles, and full-width letters match their plain forms. A title with no letters or numbers at all (like ☕) keeps its symbols, so ☕ and 🍔 are different titles. New titles get each word's first letter capitalized, in any language ("école" becomes "École"). Other Accounts' names follow the same rules. Titles can be up to 100 characters, and account names up to 60.

Refraction Types

An entry's refraction type says how it affects the budget:

A new entry can also be Auto: it takes the type of an entry with the same title already on that day, or Regular if there isn't one.

For a Hidden or Transfer entry for an Other Account, a Transfer changes the account by the opposite of its amount and a Hidden entry by its own amount. In New Entry (on Home and the Quick Entry page) and the recurring entry panel, a small ? next to Refraction Type shows what each type does.

Recurring, Unique, And Temporary Entries

  • Recurring entries (✔️, ✔️✖️, ✔️⭕️) start with a checkmark. They come from the Recurring Entries page and are written onto today and every later day they land on.
  • Unique entries (❗️, ✖️, ⭕️) are ones you add or change yourself.
  • Connected spreadsheets' entries (➕❗️, ➕✖️, ➕⭕️) start with a yellow plus. They come from spreadsheets connected on the Spreadsheets page and are written onto today and every later day of the calendar they're dated on (see Connected spreadsheets). Their temps (❗️➕, ✖️➕, ⭕️➕, the plus second) work just like recurring entries' temps.
  • Temporary recurring entries ("temps": ❗️✔️, ✖️✔️, ⭕️✔️) are unique entries made from a recurring entry, with the checkmark second. A recurring entry becomes a temp when its day passes, or when it's changed on the calendar for that day only. A temp keeps the recurring entry from being written again on its day (for its refraction type). Moving or deleting a recurring entry's day leaves a $0.00 temp behind as a "blocker" for the same reason. Once the recurring entry can't land on a temp's day anymore (its End Date moved before it, or it was deleted), the temp from today on becomes a plain unique entry, and a blocker goes away.

One Entry Per Title And Type, Per Day

A day has at most one entry for each title and refraction type, so a title can appear at most three times on a day: once as Regular, once as Hidden, and once as Transfer. An Other Account after a title makes it a different title here, so "Payday → Savings" and "Payday → HSA" are two entries. Adding an entry that matches one already there adds the two amounts together. Recurring entries and spreadsheets' entries landing on a day with a matching unique entry are added into it too (it becomes a temp). A spreadsheet's entry with the same title and type as a recurring entry landing that day is left out (Settings says so). If matching entries ever end up on the same day another way (like renaming an account to a title already on that day), they're combined the next time the budget refreshes.

Changing An Entry

Tapping an entry on a zoomed day offers: Set to a new amount, Add to it, Move to another day (it combines with a matching entry there), and Delete. If the entry is for an Other Account, its options say so. Its refraction type and Other Account can change too: it then comes off its day the way Delete takes it off (one that landed by itself leaves a $0.00 temp), and goes on as the new one, adding into an entry already on that day with the same title and type (a day has one of each). That's one step for Undo.

Tapping the day's In Bank on a zoomed day asks what the bank says for that day. The difference goes on it as a Regular Catchup entry (added into a Catchup already there), so that day's In Bank matches and the days after it follow.

An entry can go on any day the budget has, from History's oldest day on (for a new budget with no History yet, from the calendar's first day, this week's Sunday), and any later day goes to Upcoming. A day before that has nowhere to hold an entry, so New Entry, Move to, and the Quick Entry icon say so instead of adding it.

4. The Calendar And The Budget Math

The calendar always shows four weeks, Sunday through Saturday, starting with the current week. Each day's cell is text:

10/05
✅ $1528.50 In Bank
❇️ $45.00 Gains
✴️ -$6.50 Costs
❗️ -$6.50 Coffee
❗️ $45.00 Sold Lamp
  • The first line is the date, stored as MM/DD and shown in the device's date format (like 10/05 or 05/10).
  • In Bank (✅, or ⛔️ below $0): what will be in the budget's account at the end of that day.
  • Gains (❇️) and Costs (✴️): that day's gains and costs added up, Regular and Hidden (shown only when not $0). Transfers are left out.
  • Then the entries, grouped by kind (unique, temps, hidden, transfers, recurring, then connected spreadsheets') and in title order within each group.
  • Today is highlighted. A day turns red when its In Bank is below $0.

How In Bank Is Worked Out

In Bank is a running total. It starts from the In Bank of the last day in History (the most recent Saturday), or $0 for a new budget. Each day: In Bank = the day before's In Bank + that day's Regular gains − Regular costs + transfers. Hidden entries show in the day's Gains and Costs, but never change In Bank (they happen outside the budget's account). It's worked out again after every change, so it's always up to date.

The Last Day's Extra Lines

The calendar's last day has two more lines, right under its In Bank, looking at the 28 days after the calendar (recurring entries, connected spreadsheets' entries, and Upcoming entries, but not Hidden ones):

  • ✅ $812.40 Lowest: 11/14: the lowest In Bank in those 28 days, and the day it happens.
  • ✅ $1650.00 Day 28 In Bank: In Bank on the 28th day.

If the lowest is below $0, the line shows ⛔️ and the last day turns red too.

Dave

Dave's message starts with a greeting (it changes daily), then lists what needs attention, then gives the day's affirmation (each date has its own, the same one every year, with a leap year one on February 29 and a new year's one on December 31). Dave mentions:

  • days on the calendar from today on whose In Bank is below $0;
  • going below $0 in the 28 days after the calendar;
  • uncommon recurring payments (ones that land every 3 months or less often) on the calendar from today on;
  • how many Upcoming entries there are (not $0.00 temps, which only keep a recurring entry off a day; a $0.00 note, like "Add To Savings", counts).

If there's nothing to mention, Dave says everything looks fine. A budget with no recurring entries yet gets a welcome instead of a greeting.

Dave's Tips

Besides that everyday message, Dave has tips. The first time Home opens on a day, he shows one of the day's tips for 10 minutes: his card gets the red-orange gradient border, a slow glow, and a "Dave's Tip" label. Each later visit to Home (once the tip before has had its 10 minutes) shows the next one, until every tip for that day has been shown; then he's back to his everyday message. Left open, the page goes back to the everyday message by itself when the 10 minutes are up. Which tips were shown is kept on that device (in its browser storage), and they're worked out once per visit, never on every change, so they can't slow anything down. In this order:

  • Payday: when today has a paycheck (a Regular gain from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet), "Hey, you just got paid! Keep in mind, besides your recurring bills, you tend to spend about $X in the next N days, so be careful what you spend it on!" N is the days until the next payday from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet, at least 4 days away, since paychecks a few days apart (like two people's, a day apart) are one payday (14 if there isn't one). $X is the average of what went out of In Bank in the N days after each payday in the last 4 months, besides recurring bills (Regular costs that didn't come from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet). It only shows when History reaches back 4 months.
  • Spending pace: when the last 7 days (today too) added up, besides recurring bills, are at least $50 and 25% over a usual week (the 4 months before them, averaged), Dave says how much was spent and how much more than usual that is. It also needs 4 months of History.
  • Savings: when a big uncommon bill is coming up in the next 60 days (a recurring cost of $200 or more that lands every 3 months or less often, a connected spreadsheet's cost of $200 or more with no other one like it (same title and type) within 90 days, or a one-time cost of $200 or more added for a later day, on the calendar or in Upcoming, not a transfer), Dave names the soonest one (and how many more there are), says to make sure you're saving enough to afford it, and says how much to set aside from each paycheck (from recurring entries, today's too) until then: the bill divided by those paychecks, rounded up to a dollar. If it lands before the next paycheck, he says to check that what's there now covers it.
  • Weekly In Bank check: once a week, Dave asks to compare today's In Bank with the real bank account, find the purchases that make up any difference and add them (on the day they happened, or just today), and for anything that can't be found, tap today's In Bank and type what the bank says (a Catchup entry makes up the difference).
  • Catchup trend: at most once a week, when entries titled Catchup (or Catch Up) add up to $100 or more in costs over the last 30 days, Dave says purchases are slipping by and suggests adding each one as it happens, with the Quick Entry icon.
  • Refund: when a purchase (a Regular cost added with New Entry or the Quick Entry icon, dated on the calendar or later) takes Lowest in bank after today below $0, or further below, Dave says "Hey, that purchase (its title, amount, and day) seems to risk your budget balance. Is a refund possible?" right away on Home (from the Quick Entry icon, on the next visit to Home), for its own 10 minutes.

Upcoming (Future Dates)

An entry dated after the calendar waits in Upcoming, in date order. When its day joins the calendar, it moves onto that day (combining with a matching entry there). The 28-day lines and Lowest in bank after today count Upcoming entries.

Dates, Time Zones, And Formats

  • Stored the same way everywhere: a day is stored as text, MM/DD/YYYY (MM/DD on a calendar day's first line, YYYY-MM-DD for the API), never as a moment in time, and read back as that same calendar day in whatever time zone the device is in. Changing the device's language, region, date format, or time zone never moves an entry to another day.
  • Shown and typed in the device's format: its own (from its language and region: MM/DD/YYYY in the US, DD/MM/YYYY in most of the world, YYYY-MM-DD in some places), or the one picked in Settings, Language And Dates (kept on that device). Typed dates are read the same way, with /, ., -, or spaces between the numbers. A year first (2026-10-05) always works, a missing year is the nearest one, a 2-digit year is in the 2000s, and a day that doesn't exist (like 31/02) is refused instead of rolling over. Month names work too, in English, either way around and with or without a year or a weekday (Oct 5, 5 October 2026, Mon, Oct 5, 2026), and read the same in every browser. Change Logs are written in the format in use at the time.
  • Today is the device's date. Days are counted on the calendar, never in 24-hour steps, so daylight saving time can't skip or repeat one.
  • The budget's day never goes back: if the device's date is before the last day the budget was brought up to date (after flying west across midnight, with the clock set back, or after another device that's a day ahead opened it), the budget keeps using that later day until the device catches up. Otherwise the calendar could be laid out on the week before, with every day's entries on the wrong day.
  • A page left open past midnight, or on a device that moved to another time zone, reloads when it's next brought into view or used (unless it has a change it couldn't save yet), so the daily update runs first. Reminders follow the device's current time zone, which is sent with every reminder plan.

5. Recurring Entries

Each recurring entry has a title, an amount, a Start Date, a frequency, an optional End Date, and a refraction type (shown as ✔️ Regular, ✔️✖️ Hidden, or ✔️⭕️ Transfer). Hidden and Transfer ones can be for an Other Account, like a transfer into Savings every payday.

Frequency: Every N Days, Weeks, Or Months

  • Frequency is a whole number from 1 to 999 and a unit: Days, Weeks, or Months. It's stored and shown like "Every Day", "Every 2 Weeks", "Every Month", or "Every 12 Months".
  • It counts from the Start Date. Every 2 weeks lands on the Start Date, then every 14 days. Every 6 days lands every 6th day.
  • Months land on the Start Date's day of the month, or the month's last day when the month is shorter: an entry starting on the 31st lands on the 30th in 30-day months and on February 28 (29 in leap years). Every 12 months is once a year.
  • It never lands before its Start Date or after its End Date. No End Date means it keeps going.
  • The spreadsheet's names still work: Weekly, Biweekly, Monthly, 3 Month, 6 Month, and Yearly are every 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months, and get rewritten that way. The spreadsheet's Semi-Monthly (the 1st and the 15th) has no every-N version, so entries that already have it keep landing on the 1st and 15th. For something new on the 1st and 15th, make two monthly entries with different titles, like "Paycheck 1st" and "Paycheck 15th".
  • Uncommon recurring entries are ones that land every 3 months or less often (every 3+ months, 13+ weeks, or 90+ days). Dave and the daily reminders point them out.

One Per Title And Type

A title can have one recurring entry of each refraction type: one Regular, one Hidden, and one Transfer. Adding one (or changing one) to match a title and type that already exists is refused with a message saying the entry already exists, so the existing one gets changed instead. This keeps it clear which recurring entry a day's entry came from.

On The Calendar

  • Recurring entries are written onto today and every later day of the calendar they land on. Their amount counts in In Bank like any entry of their type.
  • Once a day passes, its recurring entries become temps (❗️✔️ and so on), so later changes to a recurring entry never change days that already happened.
  • Changing a recurring entry on the calendar changes only that day (it becomes a temp). Moving or deleting it leaves a $0.00 blocker on its day.
  • Changing a recurring entry's title or refraction type takes those changed days along: from today on, its temps and $0.00 blockers (on the calendar and in Upcoming) get the new title and type, so a changed day keeps its amount and a deleted day stays deleted. Days that passed keep theirs.
  • Moving a recurring entry's End Date earlier lets go of the days after it: from today on, its temps there (on the calendar and in Upcoming) become plain unique entries and its $0.00 blockers go away, since there's nothing left for them to hold back. (If the End Date moves later again, the recurring entry lands on those days again and adds into a matching unique entry there, like on any day.)
  • Deleting a recurring entry ends it yesterday: it stops from today on, its temps from today on become plain unique entries (and its blockers go away), and days that passed keep theirs.

Monthly And Yearly

The top of the Recurring Entries page shows:

  • Monthly: about how much the recurring costs add up to each month (every 2 weeks counts as about 2.17 times a month, every week about 4.35, every 3 months a third).
  • Yearly: what was spent so far this year (costs on past days and today), plus every recurring cost still to land from tomorrow through December 31.

Neither counts gains or transfers (transferred money is still yours). Hidden costs count, like any cost.

6. Connected Spreadsheets

A spreadsheet you already keep, like a list of bills, paychecks, or a shift schedule, can be connected on the Spreadsheets page (in the menu). Its entries then land on your calendar by themselves, from today on, like recurring entries, behind a yellow plus: ➕❗️ Regular, ➕✖️ Hidden, or ➕⭕️ Transfer. When the spreadsheet changes (an amount, a date, a new row, a deleted row), the calendar follows it. Settings' Help explains it in short.

Sharing It

  • A verified email first: connecting (and reading) spreadsheets needs the account's email to be verified, with the link emailed when the account was made or Send Verification Email (on the Spreadsheets page, or in Settings under Email). Until then, the Spreadsheets page says so and Add Spreadsheet is off, and a spreadsheet that's already connected (like after the email changed) keeps its last entries and says to verify the email.
  • Anyone with the link (Viewer): works for Google Sheets (its sharing link, or a File > Share > Publish to web link), files in Google Drive (like an Excel file kept there), Excel in OneDrive or SharePoint, and Dropbox. Anyone who has a public link can see the whole spreadsheet, so the link's ? suggests a separate spreadsheet with only what Prismal Budget needs, filled from the main one (in Google Sheets, =IMPORTRANGE("main spreadsheet's link", "Bills!A2:C50") brings those cells over and keeps them up to date).
  • Shared with admin@prismalbudget.org (Viewer): for Google Sheets and Google Drive files, nothing has to be public. Prismal Budget's Google account reads it for the Prismal Budget account whose verified email, or one of its confirmed Other Emails, is the email of the Google account that owns the spreadsheet or shared it with admin@prismalbudget.org, and for no one else. Gmail addresses match with or without dots, and without anything after a +, the way Gmail delivers them. (This uses the server's Google sign-in for that account, set up once; see Notes for developers. Without it, only links anyone can open work.)
  • Other Emails: spreadsheets kept in a Google account with another email than the Prismal Budget account's can be shared with admin@prismalbudget.org too. Add that email under Other Emails on the Spreadsheets page, and we email it a link that works for 24 hours (Send Again sends a new one, and the one before stops working). Once it's opened (the login page checks it, signed in or not), the spreadsheets that email shares count as the account's. Up to 5 per account. Removing one stops its spreadsheets from being read for the account, and one that's never confirmed goes away when its link runs out.
  • Apple Numbers doesn't share a spreadsheet in a way another site can read, so its links say to keep the spreadsheet in Google Sheets or Excel instead (in Numbers, File > Export To > Excel). Older Excel files (.xls) are asked to be saved as .xlsx.
  • The server downloads the file from where it's kept and passes it straight to the page, without keeping it. Microsoft doesn't say how a sharing link downloads, so for OneDrive and SharePoint links the server tries the known ways in turn.

Adding One

  1. Add Spreadsheet opens a box that asks for the link. Open Spreadsheet downloads it, says what it found (its name, how many tabs, and how it's shared), and the box grows into the entry form, named after the file (the name can be changed).
  2. Each part of an entry has a yellow ➕ to the left of its box: Date, Title, Amount, Refraction Type, and Other Account (which shows for Hidden or Transfer, or when the type or the account comes from cells). Tapping ➕ opens a second box over the first: the tab (a box to type its name, with the spreadsheet's tabs under it to tap, hidden ones too) and the cells, typed as columns and rows: From Column B Row 2 To Column B Row 50, which is B2:B50. The "To" boxes are optional (just one cell), a column can be letters or its number (2 is B), and typing B2:B50 into the first box fills all four. As it's typed, the box shows the range and what's in it ("= Bills!B2:B50: 49 cells, like Rent, Phone, Internet"). Use These Cells puts the range where the part's box was ("Bills · B2:B50"; tap it to change it), and the ➕ fills in. Type It Instead goes back to typing.
  3. Only the dates have to come from the spreadsheet. The title, amount (with Cost or Gain), refraction type, and Other Account can come from cells, or be typed (or picked) once for every entry. A part from cells needs as many cells as the dates, or just one cell, which then goes with every entry.
  4. Each date cell makes one entry, with the cells in the same spot of the other parts (cells are read row by row, left to right). Empty date cells are skipped, so picking more rows than are used now picks up rows added later.
  5. Under the parts, Entries shows what it finds from today on (the first 8, and how many), and Needs a look lists anything it can't read. Save Spreadsheet connects it. With nothing to add and something that needs a look, it says to fix that first.

How Cells Are Read

  • Dates: date cells are dates (whatever format they show in). A number in a date column is a date's serial number (a date without a date format). Text is read like a typed date: the device's date order, YYYY-MM-DD, or month names like "Oct 20, 2026" or "20 October" (a weekday in front is fine, and a missing year is the nearest one). A cell that isn't a date is listed under Needs a look.
  • Amounts: number cells as they are; text with a currency sign or code, thousands separators, a minus before or after, or accountants' parentheses (($45.50) is a cost). As Written reads a minus as a cost and anything else as a gain; most bill lists write costs without a minus, so All Costs makes every one a cost (and All Gains every one a gain).
  • Refraction types: Regular, Hidden, or Transfer (any capitals, or just R, H, or T, or the emoji); an empty cell is Regular.
  • Other Accounts: the account's name (any capitals); an empty cell is none. The entry keeps its title, with the account after it ("Payday → Savings", or "→ Savings" with no title), so it changes that account like any entry for it (a Transfer by the opposite of its amount, a Hidden entry by its own). It has to be one of your Other Accounts, and the entry has to be Hidden or Transfer.
  • Titles get each word's first letter capitalized, like any entry, up to 100 characters. Formulas count as what they show, and text with formatting in it reads as plain text.

On The Calendar

  • Its entries are written onto today and every later day of the calendar (never days before today), and taken off and written again with every change, from the spreadsheet as it was last read. Entries with the same title and type on the same day are one entry, their amounts added.
  • They work like recurring entries: a unique entry with the same title and type on that day takes in the amount (it becomes a temp, like ❗️➕); changing one for its day (Set to, Add to it, Move to) makes a temp that keeps the spreadsheet's entry off that day even when the spreadsheet changes; deleting one leaves a $0.00 temp; and Undo works on them like any calendar change.
  • An entry with the same title and refraction type as a recurring entry that lands the same day is left out (a day has one of each), and the spreadsheet's box on the Spreadsheets page names it.
  • Once a day passes, its spreadsheet entries become temps, so they stay as they were, even if the spreadsheet changes later or is deleted.
  • They count everywhere the budget looks ahead: In Bank, Lowest in bank after today and the 28 days after the calendar, Search (past the calendar too), the daily reminders, Other Accounts' balances, and Dave (his payday tip counts their Regular gains as paychecks, his spending tips leave their costs out like recurring bills, and his savings tip counts a big one with no other like it within 90 days). Like recurring entries, they aren't listed in Upcoming.

Staying Up To Date

  • Every spreadsheet that's on is read again when Home opens (at most every minute and a half each, on that device) or comes back into view, and every time Settings opens. The budget only changes when something in a spreadsheet did, and the change is logged as "➕ Spreadsheet Update".
  • A row the spreadsheet drops comes off the calendar from today on. A day changed for it becomes a plain unique entry (a $0.00 one goes away), since there's nothing left for it to hold back.
  • If a spreadsheet can't be read (sharing turned off, say, or the account's email isn't verified), its last entries stay, and its box says why. A dropped connection isn't shown; it's just read again next time.

On, Off, And Delete

  • Off takes its entries off the calendar (and it isn't read) until it's turned back On, which reads it again right away. Days changed for it keep their changes.
  • Delete takes its entries off from today on; days changed for it become plain unique entries, its $0.00 temps go away, and days that passed keep theirs. The spreadsheet itself is never touched.

Needs A Look

Each problem names its cell (like Bills!A5) and says how to fix it: a date or amount that can't be read, an amount over $1,000,000,000, a refraction type that isn't one, an Other Account you don't have (add it on the Other Accounts page, or fix the cell), an account on a Regular entry, a missing tab, parts whose cells aren't the same size as the dates, or a spreadsheet that isn't shared or is too big. A cell's problem only leaves out its own entry; the first 6 are listed, then how many more. A recurring entry keeping one off is listed too.

Files And Limits

  • Files: .xlsx (Excel; Google Sheets downloads as one, which keeps real dates), .ods (LibreOffice and others), and .csv (comma, tab, or semicolon separated). The browser reads them itself (the zip inside an .xlsx or .ods is opened with the browser's own DecompressionStream), so a browser from before 2023 can't.
  • Up to 10 spreadsheets, 8 MB each, and 5,000 cells for each part. Entries are kept from 100 days before today (Dave's savings tip looks for ones like them) to 800 days after, up to 3,000 each, and only today's and later ones land.

7. The Daily Update

The first time the budget opens on a new day, it brings everything up to date, no matter how many days were missed, in one pass:

  1. Days that joined the calendar while you were away get their Upcoming entries, then their recurring entries, then their connected spreadsheets' entries (as they were last read).
  2. Away over a month: only the first month after the budget was last used gets recurring and spreadsheet entries. The days after that get just their Upcoming entries and In Bank, so months of entries nobody checked don't have to be deleted by hand (and catching up stays quick). Adding an entry with the Quick Entry icon counts as using the budget. Days from today on get their recurring and spreadsheet entries as always.
  3. Every day that passed has its recurring and spreadsheet entries turned into temps (❗️✔️, ❗️➕, and so on), so they stay as they were that day.
  4. In Bank keeps going from the last known balance.
  5. Every week before the current week moves to History (newest on top), and the calendar becomes the current four weeks.
  6. The change is logged as "🕛 Daily Update", and the date it's done through is saved only after the data itself is saved, so a failed save simply happens again next time.

A brand-new budget starts today, with nothing to catch up. If the page stays open past midnight, the next change reloads it so the daily update runs first.

Bringing Over A Spreadsheet Budget (For Now)

Until the last budgets kept in the old Google Sheets version are moved, Home's very bottom has Bring Over A Spreadsheet Budget. The sheet is shared as "Anyone with the link" (Viewer), its link is pasted in, and Import brings it into the signed-in account, replacing that budget's calendar, History, recurring entries, tracker, Upcoming, Change Logs, and (when it has numbers in it) paycheck calculator. A budget that already has entries asks first.

  • Reading the sheet: the server (POST /api/import/sheet, signed in) downloads the sheet's tabs from Google. It finds every tab by name on the sheet's edit page, which lists hidden tabs too (the public view page, which only lists the ones showing, is the fallback), and downloads each as CSV, up to 3 MB a tab. Tabs are looked up by name every time, since the sheet's code can remake a tab with a new ID.
  • Real tabs and display copies: the sheet's code keeps each part of the budget in a hidden "C" tab (C Budget, C Recurring, C History, C Tracker, C Form Entries, C Paycheck Calculator, C Savings), hidden so a stray tap can't change it, and shows a copy of it under the plain name that it only refreshes when that part changes (so a copy's totals can lag behind, and a sheet that was just set up has no copies yet). Each part is read from its C tab, and from the copy only when there's no C tab. Future Dates has no copy: its tab is the real one.
  • Turned into the website's ways:
    • Savings: the sheet stores an entry titled Savings from the savings account's side (moving $100 into savings shows as ⭕️ $100.00 Savings, and In Bank goes down $100). Here a Transfer is stored from In Bank's side, and the account gets the opposite, so each Savings transfer flips sign (⭕️ -$100.00 Savings): In Bank and Savings come out the same. A Hidden one is the account's own amount both ways, so it stays.
    • Amounts are written the website's way ($274 is $274.00, $-29.98 is -$29.98, and ($150.00) is -$150.00), dates as MM/DD/YYYY, and types the standard way (a recurring entry's ✔️ comes first, a temp's second). Emoji saved without their invisible "show as emoji" character (✔ instead of ✔️) are fixed.
    • A tracker row of the sheet's own called Transfers comes over as "My Transfers", aliases and all, since the website's own Transfers row counts every transfer.
    • Each tracker alias keeps what it counted in the sheet (All, only Costs, or only Gains), and shows with those buttons here. The sheet's own way of marking them never shows: a search in the change logs comes over in words, the way the website says them ("rent (costs only), the Food tracker row").
    • An Upcoming row without a title is left out (the sheet never lands one either).
  • Nothing is saved until it all checks out: the Budget, Recurring, History, Tracker, Future Dates, and Form Entries parts all have to be there (hidden is fine; a missing one is named), while Paycheck Calculator and Savings are optional. Every recurring entry needs a title, an amount, a start date, an end date (or None), a frequency the website knows, and a recurring type; Upcoming needs real dates and amounts; History's days need real dates, each day once and all before the calendar, and its newest Saturday needs its In Bank line (the calendar's In Bank starts from it); and the calendar's four rows need to be 28 days in a row starting on a Sunday, with only entries on them (a line that isn't one would be dropped the first time the budget is worked out, just as the sheet's own code drops it). Anything that can't be read is listed (up to 6, and how many more) instead of being guessed at, so it can be fixed in the sheet first. The business (LLC) version of the sheet can't be brought over. The calendar is found wherever its four rows are, each tab's column titles wherever they are, and tab names match without regard to capitals or extra spaces.
  • A last look, then a check: before anything is saved, a box says what's about to come over (how many weeks of History and which days, the calendar's days, how many recurring entries and sections, Upcoming, tracker rows, and change logs (and how many of those came from the Change Logs tab), whether the paycheck calculator has numbers, the sheet's last Daily Update, any optional tabs it doesn't have, and whether it replaces entries already there), and under "Good to know", anything that comes over as it is but is worth knowing: History weeks that don't follow each other, lines in History that aren't the usual kind of entry, Savings transfers that flip sign, or a renamed tracker row. Nothing happens unless it's OK'd. After saving, every table is read back from the server and compared with what was sent; if anything differs (or didn't save), it says so and doesn't reload, and importing again is safe (it replaces what's there).
  • Change Logs: Form Entries comes over as the Change Logs (without its old column titles at the bottom). Form Entries was added to the sheet later than its form, so the earliest changes are only in the Change Logs tab (the form's answers, which the sheet's setup names): each answer from before Form Entries' first row comes over too, under the rest, with its time, what was asked for, and each answer (like "Amount: 28.53"), but no "Took" or outcome, which the sheet didn't keep. A sheet without that tab brings Form Entries alone. Changes made by hand in the sheet were never logged, so they aren't in the logs, but they come over like everything else, since the import reads the budget itself, not its logs.
  • Search Results, the Done cell, Dave's message, the notification time, and Savings are left out.
  • Brought up to today: the daily update counts from the calendar's first day. Each run of the sheet's update starts the calendar on the week of the day it ran, so that day is always in the calendar's first week, and starting from the first day is never too late: a day the sheet already turned into temps has nothing left to turn. The page reloads, and the daily update catches it up to today like any budget. In Bank, Gains, Costs, the tracker's sums, and everything else worked out from the entries are worked out again, the website's way.
  • After the reload, the card says what came over (with the "Good to know" notes again), and if the sheet's In Bank on the latest day on both calendars (the sheet added Hidden costs back into In Bank) or its Savings total differ from the website's, what to do about it.
  • A Change Logs row, "📥 Spreadsheet Import", records it. Once the last spreadsheet is moved, the card, Javascript/Sheet Import.js, and the API's src/sheetimport.js are deleted.

8. Other Accounts (Like Savings)

Other Accounts keeps track of money outside the budget's bank account, like savings, cash, or investments.

  • Linking: a Hidden or Transfer entry for an account moves money into or out of that account. When you pick Hidden or Transfer for a new entry (on the calendar, in Recurring Entries, or with the Quick Entry icon), an Other Account list appears, showing each account's balance today. Picking one never changes the title: the account is kept after it, shown as "Payday → Savings" (with no title, "→ Savings"). Only an entry with an account picked is for it: one titled with an account's name and no account picked is an ordinary entry, and changes no account. A title can't be typed with → in it (New Entry, Recurring Entries, prisms, and spreadsheets say so), since the arrow is what marks the account picked. Each account after a title is its own entry, so "Payday → Savings" and "Payday → HSA" can be on the same day; the tracker and Search go by the title ("Payday"), and Search finds it by the account's name too.
  • Transfers give the account the opposite amount: a Transfer moves money between the budget's account and the other account, so In Bank changes by the entry's amount and the account by the opposite. Moving $100 into savings is a −$100 Transfer: In Bank −$100, Savings +$100. Taking $200 out is a +$200 Transfer: In Bank +$200, Savings −$200.
  • Hidden entries give the account their own amount: a Hidden entry is money moving in the other account itself, so In Bank stays the same and the account changes by the entry's own amount, with the same sign. Putting $50 cash into savings is a +$50 Hidden entry (Savings +$50); paying $30 straight out of savings is a −$30 one (Savings −$30).
  • Balances: an account's balance is its starting amount plus all of its entries' changes (the opposite of each Transfer, and each Hidden entry's own amount), through today. Its panel lists its latest entries and the ones coming up, each with its own title (or its type, for one with no title). Tapping an account and typing what's in it today (like $3,000 in savings) sets the starting amount so today's balance comes out to it; no entry is made, so the calendar, tracker, and Search stay the same. It's logged in Change Logs as a change to the account.
  • The page shows each account's balance today, and on the calendar's last day ("By MM/DD", counting the entries still to come on the calendar). Tapping an account shows its latest and upcoming entries and lets you rename it.
  • Savings is there by default and can't be deleted (it shows a 🔒 in Edit), but it can be renamed.
  • Renaming an account renames its entries (on the calendar, in History, Upcoming, and Recurring), the tracker aliases for it, and connected spreadsheets set up with its name typed in (and the entries they read), so they all stay with it. A spreadsheet whose cells name the account needs those cells changed to the new name, and its box on the Spreadsheets page says so.
  • Deleting an account leaves its entries where they are; they just stop changing an account.
  • Entries for an account keep their type everywhere else: a Transfer for one is still a transfer (it isn't a cost or gain, and only tracker rows set to count transfers count it), and a Hidden one is a cost or gain like any Hidden entry (the day's Gains and Costs show it, and the tracker, Search, and the Monthly and Yearly totals count it).

9. The Tracker

The tracker adds up entries by category over the last 4 weeks (today and the 27 days before), last 3 months (90 days), and last 365 days. It counts today and every earlier day, from the calendar and History.

  • Rows and aliases: each row counts the entries its aliases point to. An alias is an entry title, set to count All of its amounts, only its Costs, or only its Gains. If several rows have the same alias, each counts it.
  • Hidden entries always count, like Regular ones, even ones for an Other Account.
  • Transfers: rows leave out transfers unless the row's "Count ⭕️ Transfers in this row" switch is on (in its panel); such a row's title starts with ⭕️. A transfer only ever counts in a row like that.
  • Costs and Gains rows fill in by themselves with every cost and every gain (Regular and Hidden), except transfers.
  • Transfers fills in by itself with every transfer (all of their amounts) and lists their titles, so transfers never land in Undefined. A budget without it gets it just above Undefined. Searching it (Tracker) finds every transfer, checked.
  • Undefined fills in by itself with the titles no other row fully counts, and adds them up (never transfers). A title stays there if a row only counts its costs (or gains) and it has the other kind too. Its list is made again from the entries every time, so a title leaves it once a row counts it.
  • Costs, Gains, Transfers, and Undefined can't be deleted (they show a 🔒 in Edit, and their panels have no Delete button), since the tracker fills them in by itself. Every other row can be.
  • A new budget's tracker starts with an Unknown row too, counting Misc, Catchup, and Other: an ordinary row, so it can be renamed, changed, or deleted for good.
  • One row per title and transfer setting: a title can have one row that counts transfers and one that doesn't. Adding (or changing a row into) a match for one that exists is refused with a message, and Costs, Gains, Transfers, and Undefined can't be used as titles.
  • The panel shows each alias on its own line with All / Costs / Gains buttons (only the buttons pick it, and a title is kept as it's typed, like Disney+), and the table shows them in words, like "rent (costs)". Download My Data says them in words too.

10. Search

Search (the 🔎 Search button above the calendar opens it, floating over the page; ×, Escape, or the button again closes it) finds entries by title in History, the calendar, Upcoming, and (when the To date is after the calendar) recurring entries projected up to two years ahead and connected spreadsheets' entries after the calendar.

  • Each title is its own line, with its own buttons: All (every entry with that title), Costs (only its costs), Gains (only its gains), or Tracker (the title is a tracker row: find what that row counts, each alias with its own All, Costs, or Gains setting).
  • Enter (or + Add Title) starts the next line; × takes a line out. Enter on an empty line, or the Search button, searches.
  • With no titles, the first line's buttons decide: everything, every cost, or every gain.
  • A title can have two tracker rows (one counting transfers, like ⭕️Food, and one not). Typing the ⭕️, or "Food (counts transfers)", picks that one; otherwise the one that doesn't count transfers is used. The suggestions list every row, a row that counts transfers as "Food (counts transfers)" (a suggestion list can't show the site's ⭕️ image).
  • Filters: one refraction type (Regular, Hidden, or Transfer) and one Other Account (or entries with none). An entry has to match both, as well as a title line.
  • From and To are optional (blank means from the start, or to the end). 📅 opens the device's own date picker for each, and days on the calendar can be tapped to fill them.
  • Results are newest first, each with a checkbox, and a total of the checked ones. Transfers start unchecked (they aren't really costs or gains), unless a tracker row that counts transfers found them.
  • Only the buttons pick what a line finds: a title is searched for as it's typed.
  • On History: the same Search, from the 🔎 Search button at the top of the page. While a From or To box glows, tapping a day in History fills it in.
  • On the Tracker and Change Logs: 🔎 Search at the top looks through that page's own rows instead. It floats over the page the same way, and as you type, it lists the rows with every word typed (in any order, upper or lower case) in any column: on the tracker, a row's title and aliases; in Change Logs, the time, action, and details. Tapping a tracker row it found opens that row.

11. Quick Entry (Home Screen Icon)

The Quick Entry button (on Home, above the calendar) sets up an icon for a phone's home screen that opens a New Entry form with no login.

  • Setting it up makes a long random key, and the page opens with the key in its link, to be added to the home screen: on an iPhone or iPad from Safari itself, on Android from Chrome. A page opened inside another app (like Gmail or Instagram) can't add one, so the steps say to open it in Safari or Chrome first. An account can have 10 icons; making an 11th stops the oldest one.
  • The key can only add entries to a waiting list, take back ones it added that are still waiting, see the account's Other Accounts with their balances (for the account picker), and read and change the account's prisms. It can't see anything else in the budget. The balances are as of the last time the budget was open, with the icon's own waiting entries counted in.
  • Undo and Redo on the Quick Entry page: after adding an entry, Undo takes back the newest one added there while it's still waiting (only the icon that added an entry can take it back), and Redo adds it again. Once the budget has added it, the page says to undo it on the calendar instead, where it's an ordinary Undo step ("quick entry Tea (-$3.00) on 10/05"). The page forgets its Undo list when it's closed.
  • Up to 100 entries can wait. The next time the budget opens, each is added like a New Entry (to History if it's dated in the past, to Upcoming if it's after the calendar). Each one leaves the waiting list in the same database transaction that saves the table it was added to, so none are lost or added twice. One that can't be added (like a date before History begins) is dropped, and a message says why.
  • Prisms: entries someone adds often, saved as presets under the form. A prism keeps any of a title, an amount (cost or gain), a refraction type, and an Other Account (with Hidden or Transfer, kept apart from the title), at least one of them, and never a date (New Prism has no date box). Tapping a prism only fills those into New Entry (it adds nothing by itself; an Other Account renamed or deleted since the prism was saved isn't picked, and a note says to pick one), scrolls to it, and puts the cursor in the first box still empty, so the rest can be filled in, or Add Entry tapped. Add Prism turns New Entry into New Prism (it glows; what's already filled in counts), and Save Prism keeps what's filled in or picked (Auto counts as no type). Edit Prisms drags them into order by their ≡ and deletes them with ×. The ? next to Add Prism explains them. Prisms are saved with the account (up to 30, the whole list with every change), so every icon, and a new one after a password change, has the same ones.
  • A key stops working when the password is changed or reset, on Sign Out Everywhere, when Settings turns the icons off, or when the account is deleted. Settings shows how many icons work. An icon whose key stopped working goes to the login page, which says to delete it and make a new one; the page opened without a key goes there too.
  • Why the key is in the link: it's what lets the icon skip logging in, and iPhones only save a home screen icon's address (not anything stored in the browser). Anyone with the link can add entries to the budget (and see what the icon sees), so keep it to yourself; changing the password, or Turn Off Icons in Settings, stops it.
  • The page isn't linked anywhere, is kept out of search engines, loads no analytics, and isn't tied to the web app manifest (so iPhones save its own link, key included).

12. Paycheck Calculator

The calculator works out a paycheck's take-home pay. Its 17 inputs come in four groups, and each calculation's inputs are saved to start the next one. Each box takes a number from 0 to 1,000,000,000 (a blank box counts as 0).

  • Earnings: Base Pay ($/hr), Regular Hours, Overtime Multiplier (1.5× to start) and Hours, Premium Multiplier (2× to start) and Hours, Taxable Allowances.
  • Pre-tax Deductions: Medical (a percentage and a fixed amount), Retirement (a percentage and a fixed amount).
  • Taxes: FICA (7.65% to start), Federal, and State percentages.
  • Post-tax: Deductions (a percentage and a fixed amount), Non-taxable Reimbursements.

The math, rounded to cents at each step like payroll software: Gross = regular pay + overtime pay + premium pay + taxable allowances. Pre-tax medical lowers the pay that FICA and income taxes are figured on; pre-tax retirement lowers only the income-tax pay. FICA is figured on the FICA pay, and federal and state taxes on the income-tax pay. Take home = income-tax pay − taxes − post-tax deductions + non-taxable reimbursements. The page shows Take Home, Taxes, and Gross, and how it adds up.

13. Change Logs

Every change is logged, newest first: the time it was asked for, how long it took (from then until the page had drawn it; the page shows a change right away and saves it right after; for the daily update, from asking the server for the budget until it's caught up), the action (like "❗️ Create Unique", "✔️ Create Recurring", "🔧 Tracker Entries", "🏦 New Account", "➕ Connect Spreadsheet", "➕ Spreadsheet Update", "🔎 Search", "🧮 Calculator", "↩️ Undo", "↪️ Redo", or "🕛 Daily Update"), and up to four details (an undo or redo says what it took back or put back, and which days). A change that couldn't be made is logged in red as an Error (and nothing was changed); one that didn't find its entry is marked Not Found. The newest 1,000 are kept.

14. Daily Reminders

On days the budget needs a look, a "🗓️ Check Budget" reminder can arrive at a chosen time (8:00 AM to start, in the device's time zone). A day needs one when:

  • ⛔️ its In Bank is below $0;
  • ✔️ an uncommon recurring payment lands between that day and the end of its four-week calendar;
  • ❗️ it has a cost.

The website works out which days need one (from today through the 28 days after the calendar) and sends that list to the server whenever it changes. Reminders arrive two optional ways:

  • Push notifications on each device that turns them on (up to 10 devices). The server checks every 5 minutes and sends any that are due. On iPhone and iPad, the site has to be added to the Home Screen first.
  • A calendar link (an .ics feed with a private code) for Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or most calendar apps. Each reminder is a 30-minute event with an alert.

15. Accounts, Sign-In, And Security

  • A person, not a bot: creating an account includes Cloudflare Turnstile's check that you're a person. It runs by itself while the form is filled in, and only shows a box (above Create Account) when it needs a tap. An account can't be made without it, from the page or any other way.
  • 18 and older: Prismal Budget is intended for people 18 and older. Creating an account asks for a birth date (the device's own date picker); under 18 on that day, or a day more than 120 years ago, and an account can't be made. The birth date is stored with the account, and Settings can fix one typed wrong (never to under 18). The Privacy Policy & Terms of Use say so too.
  • Verified emails: a new account's email gets a link to verify it, and so does a changed email (which isn't verified until then; links sent to the old address stop working). Each link works for 24 hours, and tapping it again says the same thing instead of an error. Settings' Send Verification Email sends a new one. A password reset link that works also verifies the email, since it went there. Connecting and reading spreadsheets need a verified email, since sharing a spreadsheet privately depends on it; nothing else needs it. Other Emails for spreadsheets are confirmed the same way, each by its own link.
  • Usernames: 5 to 30 characters, counted the way a person would (an accented letter, or an emoji, even one made of several joined emoji, is one). Any characters you can see work: letters in any language, numbers, symbols, emoji, @, and spaces (extra spaces are tidied). Characters you can't see, or that change which way text runs, aren't allowed, since they could make one username pass for another. "Sam", "sam", and "ＳＡＭ" count as the same username, so only one of them can exist.
  • Signing in works with the username (in any capitals) or the account's email. After too many wrong passwords, signing in pauses for a while; Forgot Password and passkeys still work.
  • Passwords: at least 8 characters and at most 256, and any characters work (spaces, symbols, emoji, other languages). The same password typed on another keyboard or device still matches. A new password (making an account, changing it, or resetting it) that has shown up in data breaches is refused. Passwords are never stored as they're typed.
  • Staying signed in: signing in (with the password or a passkey) lasts up to 24 hours, and 15 minutes without activity signs you out, with a counting-down notice in the last minute. Logout signs that device out. A device signed out from somewhere else (by Sign Out Everywhere, or a new password on another device) goes to the login page at its next request, and the login page says why. Logout with a change that hasn't reached the server yet (offline) asks first.
  • A new password: changing it in Settings (with the current one) signs out every other device and stops every Quick Entry icon; this device stays signed in. Resetting it with an emailed link (which works once, for an hour) signs out every device, stops every Quick Entry icon, and turns passkey sign-in off. Sign Out Everywhere signs out every device, that one too, and stops the icons.
  • Security notices: a password change is reported to the account's email, an email change to the old address (with the new address partly hidden; the new one gets its verification link), and a new passkey to the account's email. Emails are sent through Resend.
  • Changing the email or password, adding a passkey, or deleting the account needs the password. Deleting the account deletes all of its data, passkeys included.
  • Analytics: Google Analytics loads only when the device hasn't turned it off in Settings and the browser doesn't send Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track. The page addresses it gets leave out everything after the page's name, so private codes in links (like a reset or verification link's) never reach it. It never sees budget data.
  • Security: Prismal Budget uses reasonable security measures to protect accounts and their data. How they work isn't published here.

Passkeys (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello)

A passkey signs you in with your face, your fingerprint, or your device's PIN instead of your password. It's the web standard (WebAuthn) behind "sign in with Face ID", and it works with Face ID and Touch ID on Apple devices, Windows Hello, Android's fingerprint or face unlock, password managers, and security keys. Passkeys are optional, and the password keeps working either way, so a lost phone never locks anyone out.

  • The account's setting: passkey sign-in is unset, on, or off for each account. It starts unset, and adding a passkey turns it on.
  • Turning it on when signing in: turning on Turn on passkey sign-in on the login page, then signing in with the password, makes a passkey for that device and turns it on (or, if the device already has one of the account's passkeys, just turns it on). Yes, Turn It On tries again if it was cancelled, and No Thanks skips it for now without changing anything.
  • Signing in with a passkey: Sign In With A Passkey, or picking a passkey under the username box. The passkey says whose it is, so no username is needed. The device's check opens right away (the page keeps a sign-in ready, since Safari only opens it right in a tap). If passkey sign-in is off for the account, the page says so and turns the switch on, so signing in with the password turns it back on.
  • Turning it on or off in Settings: On and Off apply to the whole account. Off keeps the passkeys, but none of them can sign in until it's turned back on. Turn On needs at least one passkey; with none, the Turn On button adds one. Adding a passkey always turns it on.
  • Making one: the password is checked, then the device asks for the face, fingerprint, or PIN and saves the passkey (often in iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager, or Windows Hello, which may sync it to the person's other devices). Each passkey gets a name, like "iPhone" or "Windows", that can be changed when adding it. An account can have up to 20, and a device that already has one of the account's passkeys says so instead of making another.
  • What's stored: for each passkey, its public key, its ID, its name, the site it was made on, when it was added and last used, whether the device said it's synced, and a sign-in counter; for the account, the setting and a random ID that devices keep with its passkeys (it isn't the username or anything else about the person). The private key never leaves the device or password manager, and faces, fingerprints, and PINs never leave the device: the site never receives them.
  • Only on Prismal Budget: a passkey only works on the site it was made on, so a look-alike site can't use it. Which company made the device isn't asked for or stored.
  • Removing: Remove in Settings deletes a passkey from the server, so it can't sign in. Browsers that support it are told to stop offering it, and it can also be deleted in the device's password settings. A removed passkey that's still on a device gets "That passkey isn't on any account anymore".
  • A password reset turns it off, so a passkey someone else added stops working too. The owner checks the list in Settings, removes any that aren't theirs, and turns it back on.

16. How The Data Is Stored

Data lives in a Cloudflare D1 (SQLite) database. Each part of a budget is one table, holding one row per user whose content is the whole part as JSON text. When something changes, only the parts that changed are saved, each in one piece. If a part's stored copy can't be read when the budget opens, it's never overwritten, so it can still be recovered.

Every date in these tables is stored the same way whatever the device's language, region, or time zone (see Dates, time zones, and formats). Each device also keeps its own choices in the browser (localStorage), and they stay after signing out: the date format (prismal_date_format: mdy, dmy, or ymd, or none for the device's own) and analytics (prismal_analytics).

17. How The Website Is Built

  • The website is plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with no build step, hosted on GitHub Pages at prismalbudget.org.
  • The budget's logic runs in the browser. When the budget opens, the website loads every table, runs the daily update, and works out everything that's computed (In Bank, the 28-day lines, Dave, the tracker, account balances). Every change goes through one function that runs changes one at a time, takes a snapshot first (and puts it back if anything fails, so half-made changes are never saved), refreshes the computed parts, logs the change, saves the tables that changed, and tells the page to redraw.
  • The server is a Cloudflare Worker (an API) with a D1 database. It handles accounts, sign-in (passwords and passkeys), storing tables, Quick Entry, reminders and cleaning up expired records (a scheduled job every 5 minutes), email (through Resend), and downloading connected spreadsheets (which the page reads itself). It doesn't do budget math.
  • Saving safely: each account's budget has a revision number that goes up with every saved table. The page keeps the number it loaded (and each save's new one), and sends it with every save. The server writes a table only when the number still matches, all in one transaction; otherwise nothing is written, the reply is 409, and the page reloads with the newest budget and says the last change needs to be made again. A page that comes back into view (another tab, a phone waking up) checks the number and reloads when the budget changed elsewhere. A save that couldn't reach the server stays marked, is sent again when the connection comes back (and every 30 seconds), and leaving before then asks first.
  • Undo and Redo: a calendar change or quick entry gives runBudgetAction an undo label and the days it touches. Before and after the change, it keeps each of those days' own entry lines (❗️, ✖️, ⭕️, and temps; never the lines the budget writes itself), plus the Other Accounts' equity tables if the change touched them, and only the days that changed become the step. Undo puts each day's "before" lines back, but only while every one of those days still holds its "after" lines (Redo is the same the other way); then everything computed is rebuilt like after any change, and it's saved and logged like one. A step that no longer matches is refused and dropped. Steps are kept per tab in sessionStorage (prismal_undo_<username>), 25 at most, and cleared on sign-out. The Quick Entry page keeps its own list in memory and takes entries back with /api/quick/unqueue.
  • Loading and updates: every page starts with Boot.js, which works out which page it is, sends signed-out visitors to the login page (except on public pages), and loads that page's scripts. GitHub Pages shows 404.html at any missing address, so that page marks itself (data-page="notfound") to be recognized. A browser that blocks site data gets a message saying how to allow it instead of a blank page.
  • Caching and releases: pages and Boot.js are always checked for a newer copy (Cloudflare sends them with Cache-Control: no-cache, so one that hasn't changed costs a quick "not modified" answer), so a release reaches everyone on their next page, with no reload. Every other file's address carries its fingerprint, the first 10 hex digits of the SHA-256 of its contents (like /Javascript/Layout.js?v=d669486b33), so browsers and Cloudflare keep their saved copy until that file really changes, a changed file's new address gets the new copy right away, and files that didn't change stay saved. Pages and stylesheets carry fingerprints in their links, and Boot.js carries them for the scripts and sprites it loads (FILE_VERSIONS, used through assetUrl); _tools/stamp.mjs writes them all (see Notes for developers).
  • Speed: the site's font, Bpmf Huninn, comes in two parts made from BpmfHuninn-Regular.ttf (which no page loads): its Latin letters, numbers, Greek, and symbols (Fonts/MainFont-Latin.woff2, 24 KB, preloaded by every page) and its Chinese, Japanese, and Bopomofo characters (Fonts/MainFont-More.woff2, 1.8 MB), which a browser only downloads for a page that shows one of them (CSS unicode-range). The header images are 384×384 WebP copies (about 15 to 32 KB each) of the 1024×1024 PNGs in Resources/. The background picture (Resources/Background.webp, 5 KB) is built into stylesheet.css, with its average color under it, so it paints in a page's very first frame instead of a moment later, and the starfield gets a head start: Boot.js, which runs first, starts the starfield's worker and loads and decodes its star image (a 34 KB WebP) while the rest of the page loads, and Starfield Setup.js hands both over, so the stars are drawn as soon as the page shows. Google Analytics loads after the page has, so it never holds the page up, and pages slide in when they've loaded (or after 1.5 seconds at most). The server makes its tables once per Worker instance instead of on every request. Every change works out the whole budget again, so that work is kept small: each day's lines are read once and remembered by their text (readLine), so all of History isn't split up again on every change, and the Recurring page's Yearly counts each recurring cost's days with arithmetic (countRecurringHits) instead of checking every day to December 31. With over a year of History, a whole change takes a few milliseconds on a computer. Dave's tips are worked out once per visit to Home, never on a change.

19. Why It Works This Way

  • A calendar instead of categories first: knowing which day the balance dips is what keeps you from overdrafting; the tracker covers categories.
  • Entries are lines of text: it's how the original spreadsheet worked, it's easy to read and store, and a day's whole story is in one cell.
  • Refraction types: not every dollar that moves is spending. Hidden covers money outside the budget's account; Transfer covers money that only changes places.
  • One entry per title and type per day, one recurring entry per title and type, one tracker row per title and transfer setting: there's never a question of which one a change, a temp, or a search means.
  • Temps and blockers: changing one day of a recurring entry shouldn't change every day, and days that passed should never change.
  • Accounts are linked by title: no extra field to keep in sync. Moving, combining, or deleting an entry, or a recurring entry landing, carries the link along automatically. Balances come only from entries so there's one source of truth.
  • Budget math in the browser: changes feel instant, the server stays simple and cheap, and the math lives in one place.
  • Whole tables saved at once, with a snapshot before every change: a change either fully happens or doesn't happen at all.
  • The Quick Entry key can only add: a phone left unlocked can't be used to read the budget. Its prisms are only what the person saved there.
  • Prisms kept with the account, not the phone: a new icon (after a password change, say) or another phone has the same ones, and a phone's home screen apps can lose what they keep in the browser.
  • Passkeys next to the password, not instead of it: a lost or replaced phone never locks anyone out, and the emailed reset link stays the way back in. Passkeys can't be phished or reused on another site, and nothing about a face or fingerprint ever reaches the server.
  • Passkeys only when asked for: signing in with a password never stops to ask about passkeys unless the switch was on, and skipping one never turns passkey sign-in off for the account (only Settings does).
  • A revision check instead of "last save wins": each table is saved whole, so a page holding an older copy (another device, or a tab left open) could quietly erase changes made elsewhere. Refusing that save and reloading costs one redo at worst, and never loses data.
  • A starting balance as an ordinary entry: it shows on the calendar and can be changed or deleted like anything else, instead of being a hidden number. A Transfer changes In Bank without counting as income.
  • Usernames with any characters you can see, but no invisible ones: people can use their name in any language, or an emoji. Characters you can't see, or that flip which way text runs, could make two different usernames look the same, so they're refused, and look-alike forms count as the same username.
  • Length and a breach check instead of "one number and one symbol": rules like that make passwords harder to remember without making them much harder to guess. Refusing passwords attackers already have on their lists does more, and any characters (even a whole sentence) are welcome.
  • Undo by day, and only while the day still matches: each part of the budget is saved whole, so putting back an old copy of a whole table could erase changes made since. Putting back only the entries on the days a change touched, and only while those days are as it left them, can't.
  • Analytics with the private parts left out: page addresses without their ?query, nothing about the budget, none of Google's advertising features, and off for anyone whose browser asks or who turns it off.
  • Floating boxes: the page never jumps around while you work.
  • Hidden entries change an account by their own amount, Transfers by the opposite: a Transfer is one movement seen from two sides (out of In Bank and into savings), while a Hidden entry happens in the other account itself, so its sign already says what happened there.
  • Temps let go when their recurring entry ends: a temp only keeps its recurring entry from landing on its day, so once the recurring entry can't land there anymore, the temp is just an entry.
  • Days stored as text, the same way everywhere: a budget day is a calendar day, not a moment in time, so a time zone or a date format can never move an entry. Only what's shown and typed follows the person's format.
  • The browser's translation instead of translated pages: every major browser can translate a page, on the person's own device and into their language, and it keeps up with every change to the site with nothing to maintain. Keeping the person's entries, names, amounts, and dates out of it means their budget never changes under them.
  • Connected spreadsheets work like recurring entries: they're written from today on and taken off and written again with every change, so a spreadsheet that changes or drops an entry changes the calendar, while a day changed here (a temp) and days that passed never change under you.
  • The server downloads a spreadsheet, and the browser reads it: the page can't fetch other sites itself, but reading a file takes work, and the budget's other work already runs in the browser. The server just passes the file along, never keeping it, so it costs little and holds nothing.
  • Shared with admin@prismalbudget.org only for whoever shared it: Prismal Budget's Google account can open everything shared with it, so a link alone is never enough: the spreadsheet is only read for the account whose verified email (or a confirmed Other Email) owns or shared it. An Other Email is confirmed the same way as the account's email, by opening a link sent to it, so an account can only add an email whose inbox it can open.
  • A verified email for spreadsheets, and only for them: that match only means something when the account's email is really its owner's, so connecting and reading spreadsheets wait for a verified email. Nothing else does (signing in, the budget, and reset links work the same), so an email typed wrong never locks anyone out of their budget.
  • A zoomed day laid out big instead of scaled up: a phone draws a scaled-up element from its small picture, so a tiny day scaled to fill the screen came out blurry. Laying it out at the zoomed size draws its text at full size, and the scale only runs during the quick grow and shrink.
  • Fingerprinted files instead of a reload per release: pages are always fresh, so they always point at exactly the right files, and a release only downloads again the files that changed.

20. Notes For Developers

  • The engine is Javascript/Process Budget.js. The page calls these, and each runs through runBudgetAction (queued, snapshot and rollback, refresh, log, save, then a budget:updated event): addCalendarEntry, changeCalendarEntry, saveRecurringEntry, saveTrackerEntry, saveAccount, moveBudgetRows, addCategoryRow, renameCategoryRow, deleteBudgetRows, searchBudget, savePaycheck, and for connected spreadsheets saveSheetConnection, turnSheetConnection, deleteSheetConnection, and updateSheetReads. Results look like { ok, saved, notFound, message }; a BudgetInputError becomes a message for the person instead of an error.
  • refreshBudgetData rebuilds everything computed, in order: frequencies rewritten the new way, Upcoming moved onto the calendar, recurring entries written, connected spreadsheets' entries written (writeSheetEntries), temps let go (releaseEndedTemps, releaseSheetTemps), expired recurring entries removed, each day's entries combined and sorted, In Bank and the 28-day lines, Dave, the tracker and the recurring summary, then account balances.
  • Rules to keep: one entry per title and refraction type per day; one recurring entry per title and refraction type; one tracker row per title and transfer setting; an entry is for an Other Account when it's Hidden or Transfer and its title ends with " → " and the account's name (splitAccount, withAccount; a title that's only the account's name doesn't count), and the account changes by accountChange (a Transfer the opposite of its amount, a Hidden entry its own; the API's readAccounts in src/quick.js has to match it); Hidden entries are costs and gains (the day's lines, the tracker, Search) but never change In Bank, and transfers change In Bank but aren't costs or gains (dayMath, isTransferEntry); titles compare with cleanString (lowercase letters and numbers in any language, and single spaces; symbols only when there are no letters or numbers; the invisible "show as emoji" variation selectors are dropped first, so "⭕️Food" compares as "food"), and the API's cleanTitle in src/quick.js has to match it. Typed amounts go through readMoneyInput (which reads a decimal comma).
  • Frequencies parse with parseFrequency and are written with formatFrequency; a recurring entry lands when recurringRowHits says so.
  • Lookups keyed by titles use Map, never plain objects, so titles like "constructor" or "__proto__" are just titles.
  • Passkeys are Javascript/Passkeys.js (with Login.js and Settings Page.js) on the site and src/passkey.js in the API. Browsers only allow passkeys on a web address, so test at http://localhost:…, not 127.0.0.1; passkeys made there only work there.
  • Saves go through saveChanges, which sends the page's dataRevision with each table and takes the new one back. A 409 sets budgetConflict, stops the rest of the saves, and reloads with a notice. Test stand-ins for D1 must report meta.changes like D1 does, since the API checks the revision update's meta.changes.
  • Security details (the server's secrets, its limits, and how each defense works) stay out of this public page. They're in SECURITY-NOTES.md, in the API's private repository; update it there when a change touches security.
  • Accounts are src/account.js and src/passwords.js in the API. Login.js and Settings Page.js check the username and password rules before sending, and the API checks them again, so keep them in step.
  • Undo: a change that should be undoable passes { label, dates } as runBudgetAction's third argument (see addCalendarEntry, changeCalendarEntry, and applyQuickEntries). undoCalendarChange and redoCalendarChange take a step, and undoInfo() says what each button would do.
  • Test stand-ins for D1 must also return meta.last_row_id (a queued quick entry's id). Tests against wrangler dev reach the real Have I Been Pwned, so their passwords can't be common ones.
  • Requests to the API with the login token go through budgetApi; a 401 means the session ended, and signedOutElsewhere signs the page out. Saving stops after a 401 or a 409.
  • Images: a header icon that changes needs a new 384×384 WebP copy next to its PNG (the pages use the WebP). Dave's picture works the same way: Dave.webp (384×384, what Home shows) made from the full-size Dave.jpg. (Help, New Entry, and Quick Entry are plain buttons, so they don't need pictures.) After changing an image, stamp the site (below).
  • Dates: tables store MM/DD/YYYY (written with formatToMMDDYYYY and formatToMMDD, read with createSafeMidnight). People see showDate, showDay, or showStoredDate, and what they type goes through parseTypedDate (with datePattern() as the box's hint; month names go through namedDate, so no browser's own guessing is used), all following dateFormat(). Never show a stored date as it is, and never read a typed one with createSafeMidnight. Step days with addDays, never by adding 86,400,000 ms. pageDay is the device's date when the page loaded; today is the budget's, set by setBudgetDay (see Dates).
  • Zooming a day is in Process Budget.js's LAYOUT FUNCTIONS (zoomDay, fitZoomedDay, closeZoomedDay). On a computer, CSS scales the day 2× (--zoom: 2, which the v divides by to stay the same size). On phones, fitZoomedDay measures the day at its own size (--zoom: 1, with nothing drawn in between), sets --zoom to the size that fills the screen, and moves it there with a translate only: the CSS lays the day's content out at --zoom times its width, height, padding, font size, and corners, so its text is drawn at full size. A scale only runs in the Web Animations that grow it out of the day and shrink it back (isZoomClosing while it shrinks). The dimming backdrop sits in #transitionContainer, just under the day.
  • Change Logs timing: runBudgetAction notes when a change was asked for, and processBudgetAction draws the change (budget:updated), waits for the next paint (afterNextPaint), then finishes the log row and saves.
  • A change runs after stripRecurringEntries has taken the recurring entries off the calendar, and gets the tables as they were before (change(before), from snapshotTables). Search reads that calendar, so it finds the recurring entries the calendar shows.
  • The starfield measures its canvas (STARFIELD.canvasBox) for its size and for where a tap lands, never innerWidth/innerHeight, which Safari shrinks while pinch-zoomed.
  • Translation: an element that shows the person's entries, names, amounts, or dates gets translate="no" (BudgetUI.noTranslate); the site's own words don't.
  • Temps: releaseEndedTemps (in refreshBudgetData, before expired recurring entries are removed) turns temps after their recurring entry's last day, from today on, into plain entries, and releaseSheetTemps does the same for spreadsheet temps whose title and type no connected spreadsheet has anymore.
  • Marks: a sprite's entryMark says where an entry comes from: ✔️ a recurring entry, ➕ a connected spreadsheet, or none. Before the type it's still as it landed (✔️⭕️, ➕❗️); after it, it's a temp (⭕️✔️, ❗️➕). unique() finds what lands on a day by itself with scheduledHit (a recurring entry first, then a spreadsheet's, sheetHit), and keeps that entry's mark on the temps and $0.00 blockers it makes. A spreadsheet's entries by day come from sheetEntriesByDay (only spreadsheets that are on, without the ones a recurring entry keeps off), which the daily update, the 28-day lines, Search, reminders, and Dave all use.
  • Connected spreadsheets: the API's src/sheets.js downloads (readLink says what a link is, and fileFormat what the file is), and the site's Spreadsheets.js reads (readWorkbook, readConnection). Each connection's entries are read once into dates and compared titles (parsedSheetEntries, kept until its entries change). Test files for .xlsx and .ods can be made by hand: a zip of a few XML files is enough.
  • Verified emails: src/account.js sends the links (emailVerifyLink, with kind new, changed, or again) and checks them (handleVerifyEmail, POST /api/email/verify), and src/sheets.js checks that the email is verified first. Other Emails are in src/sheets.js too (handleSheetEmailsRoute sends their links, handleSheetEmailVerify checks them, and readShared accepts the confirmed ones), and their links open /login.html?sheet-email=. Tests add verification links to the database themselves (see test/index.spec.js), since they never send email.
  • Prismal Budget's Google account (admin@prismalbudget.org, for spreadsheets shared with it: the site's Google Workspace account, which also gets the site's mail in its own Workspace inbox) needs three Worker secrets, made once while signed in to that account: in the Google Cloud console, make a project, turn on the Google Drive API, set up the OAuth consent screen (External, published to production, so its permission doesn't run out after 7 days) with the scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.readonly, and make an OAuth client (Desktop app). Its site key is in Login.js (TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY), and its secret key is the Worker secret TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY (npx wrangler secret put TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY). The money in the budget's bank account at the end of a day, worked out from the last History balance and each day's entries.
Entry
One line on a day: an emoji, an amount, and a title, like ❗️ -$6.50 Coffee.
Refraction type
How an entry affects the budget: Regular (❗️), Hidden (✖️), or Transfer (⭕️).
Recurring entry
An entry that repeats every N days, weeks, or months, written onto the calendar for you; it starts with ✔️.
Unique entry
An entry you added or changed yourself.
Temporary recurring entry (temp)
A unique entry made from a recurring entry on one day (❗️✔️, ✖️✔️, ⭕️✔️). It keeps the recurring entry of its type from being written again that day, and becomes a plain unique entry once that recurring entry can't land there anymore.
Blocker
A $0.00 temp left when a recurring (or spreadsheet) entry's day is moved or deleted.
Connected spreadsheet
A spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel, and others) connected on the Spreadsheets page, whose entries land on the calendar from today on behind a ➕ (➕❗️, ➕✖️, ➕⭕️) and follow it as it changes.
Upcoming (Future Dates)
Entries dated after the calendar, waiting for their day to join it.
History
Past weeks, kept as they were, newest first.
Transfer
A ⭕️ entry: money changing places (like between In Bank and an Other Account, or depositing cash) without being gained or spent. It changes In Bank, but isn't a cost or gain.
Other Account
An account outside the budget's bank account, like Savings. Its balance is its entries' changes added up: the opposite of each Transfer, and each Hidden entry's own amount.
Tracker row and alias
A category in the tracker, and an entry title it counts (all of the amounts, only costs, or only gains).
Transfers (tracker row)
The tracker row that adds up every transfer, so they never land in Undefined.
Undefined
The tracker row that collects titles no other row fully counts (never transfers).
Uncommon recurring payment
A recurring entry that lands every 3 months or less often.
Daily update
The catch-up that runs the first time the budget opens on a new day.
Dave
The character on the home page who says what to keep in mind, with a daily affirmation.
Starting Balance
A new budget's first entry: a ⭕️ Transfer on the day it's set up, holding what was in the bank then.
Revision
A number that goes up with every saved budget table. A save from a page with an older number is refused, so it can't overwrite newer changes.
Passkey
A sign-in key a device makes for Prismal Budget and unlocks with a face, fingerprint, or PIN (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello). The server keeps only its public half.
Quick Entry key
The secret in a home screen icon's link that lets it add entries (and take back ones still waiting) and keep prisms, and nothing else, without logging in.
Prism
A saved entry on the Quick Entry page that fills in New Entry with what it keeps (a title, an amount, or a type), leaving the rest to fill in.
Session
One signed-in device or browser, good for up to 24 hours. Logout ends it.
Undo step
One change Undo can take back: the days it touched, with each day's own entries from before and after it.
Sprite
The site's own image for ❗️ (red), ✖️ (purple), ⭕️ (green), ✔️ (orange), or ➕ (yellow), shown in place of the emoji.
Starfield
The animated stars behind every page. The pointer, taps, and hidden keyboard keys move them, and the device's reduced motion setting turns it off (see The starfield background).
Verified email
An account email whose emailed link was opened, which shows the address is really the account owner's. Connecting spreadsheets needs one.
Other Emails
The emails of other Google accounts an account's spreadsheets are shared from (with admin@prismalbudget.org), added on the Spreadsheets page and confirmed the same way as a verified email.
Date format
How dates are shown and typed on a device: MM/DD/YYYY, DD/MM/YYYY, or YYYY-MM-DD.
Fingerprint
A short hash of a file's contents in its address (?v=…), so browsers keep the file until it changes.
Change Log
The record of every change to the budget.
