Know what's in your bank account every day, four weeks ahead

Prismal Budget is a budget built around a calendar. Add what you spend and what you earn, and it works out what will be in your bank account at the end of every day, so you can see a tight week coming long before it gets here.

What It Is

Prismal Budget is a web app for planning your money day by day. Instead of sorting last month's spending into categories, it looks ahead: a four-week calendar where each day lists its entries (costs and gains) and In Bank, what your bank account will hold at the end of that day.

It runs in any web browser, on your phone or your computer, with nothing to install. Your budget is saved with your account, so it's the same everywhere you sign in.

What It Does

  • Shows In Bank for every day. Each day on the calendar shows what your bank account will hold at the end of it, and a day that would go below $0 turns red, weeks before it happens.
  • Fills in your bills and paychecks. Add rent, a paycheck, or a subscription once, with how often it comes (every week, every 2 weeks, every month, and so on), and it lands on every day it should.
  • Connects your own spreadsheet. Already keep bills or a pay schedule in Google Sheets or Excel? Verify your email, connect it, and pick which cells hold the dates and amounts, and its entries land on your calendar and follow the spreadsheet as it changes.
  • Looks past the calendar. Lowest in bank after today shows the lowest your balance gets, counting the 28 days after the calendar too.
  • Dave keeps an eye on it. Dave looks over your budget, points out days that go below $0 and big bills coming up, shares tips (like how much you tend to spend before payday), and has a new affirmation for you every day.
  • Tracks savings and other accounts. Keep Savings (and accounts like Cash, or a credit card with its monthly interest) next to your budget, with everything added up at the top. Moving money in or out keeps both balances right.
  • Shows where your money goes. The tracker adds up your spending and income by category over the last 4 weeks, 3 months, and 365 days. Search finds any entry, and History keeps every week that's passed.
  • Adds entries in seconds. Put a Quick Entry icon on your phone's home screen that opens a New Entry form with no sign-in, shows what's in the bank today, and saves the entries you add often. It even shows In Bank on an Apple Watch.
  • Reminds you. A daily "Check Budget" reminder on the days your budget needs a look, as a notification or on Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or most calendar apps.
  • Works out a paycheck. The Paycheck Calculator figures your take-home pay from your hours, overtime, taxes, and deductions.
  • Keeps you in control. Sign in with a passkey (Face ID, Touch ID, or Windows Hello) if you like, download everything in your budget anytime, or delete your account and all of its data.

How It Works

  1. Create an account with a username, your email, your birth date, and a password (Prismal Budget is for people 18 and older), then open the link we email you to verify your email.
  2. Set your starting balance: what's in your bank account right now (and in savings, if you like).
  3. Add your recurring entries once each: paychecks, rent, bills, and subscriptions.
  4. Add what you spend as you go, from the calendar or the Quick Entry icon. In Bank updates for every day ahead, and each week moves into History as it passes.

Three Kinds Of Entries

Every entry has a type, so the budget knows how it changes your money:

  • ❗️ Regular: everyday costs and gains, like groceries or a paycheck. They change In Bank.
  • ✖️ Hidden: money spent or received outside your budget's account, like spending cash. It counts as a cost or gain, but In Bank stays the same.
  • ⭕️ Transfer: money moving between your own accounts, like putting $100 into savings. It changes In Bank, but it isn't a cost or gain.

Recurring entries start with a ✔️, and entries from a connected spreadsheet with a ➕. Change one on a single day, and only that day changes.

    Getting Started

    A new budget starts at $0, so the first time you sign in, the Welcome card at the top asks what's in your bank account now (and, if you like, in savings). Your bank balance goes on today as a ⭕️ Starting Balance entry, which you can change like any entry, and savings becomes your Savings account's balance (on Other Accounts). Then add your paychecks and bills on Recurring Entries. The card goes away once both are done, or tap × to hide it, and it doesn't come back the next time you sign in.

    Viewing The Calendar

    • Tap a day to zoom in on it, over a dimmed page. On a phone, it grows as big as fits the screen, sharp and easy to read; on a computer, it doubles in size. Tap its v (in its top right corner), or anywhere outside it, to zoom back out.
    • Swipe a zoomed day left or right to slide over to the next day or the day before (on a computer, press the → and ← keys).
    • Entries stay on one line. A day cuts a long one short with "..." at its edge until you zoom in on it; then they run past its edge, over the dimmed page. On a phone, entries past the bottom of the screen (or a very long one past its side) are still there: slide the zoomed day with a finger to see them.
    • On a zoomed day, tap an entry to change its amount, refraction type, or Other Account, move it to another day, or delete it. Changing its type or account adds it into an entry with that title and type already on the day.
    • On a zoomed day, tap In Bank and type what your bank says for that day (tap Negative if it's below zero). The difference goes on it as a ⭕️ Transfer titled Catchup (with no Other Account), so it fixes In Bank without counting as a cost or gain, and the days after it follow.
    • Search finds entries by title: any title with what you type in it, or with Exact on, only that title. It can keep to one refraction type or one Other Account. With no To date, it looks a month ahead too (recurring and connected spreadsheets' entries included), and what's after today shows first, apart from the rest.
    • Pinch or stretch with two fingers on one day to zoom just that day out or in (it turns and moves with your fingers too). Let go, and it settles back. Two fingers across different days zoom the whole page, like anywhere else.
    • On a computer, hold the mouse button down on a day to magnify it, and keep holding while you slide across the days to magnify each one you pass.
    • To zoom the whole page, pinch with two fingers on a phone (anywhere but a single day), or press Ctrl and + (⌘ and + on a Mac) on a computer.

    Typing Amounts

    Amounts fill in from the cents, like a cash register, so there's no decimal point to type: 5 is $0.05, and 1234 is $12.34. A blank amount is $0.00. Cost and Gain (or Negative and Positive, for a balance) pick its sign.

    Pages

    The tabs at the top open your budget's four pages: Home, Recurring Entries, History, and Other Accounts (the one you're on glows). Going from one of them to another, the top of the page stays put while the rest slides down and the next page slides up. Menu, at the top right, has the rest: Spreadsheets, Tracker, Paycheck Calculator, and Change Logs (each of those has a Back button instead of Menu).

    Entry Types

    The emojis at the start of each entry show what kind of entry it is.

    • Recurring entries (✔️, ✔️✖️, ✔️⭕️) always start with a checkmark. They come from your Recurring Entries page (like paychecks and bills), and are added for you on today and every day after. Once a day passes, its recurring entries turn into unique entries, so later changes to a recurring entry never change days that already happened.
    • Unique entries (❗️, ✖️, ⭕️, ❗️✔️, ✖️✔️, ⭕️✔️) are the ones you add or change yourself, like fast food, or an electric bill that's higher than normal. Most don't have a checkmark, but when one does, the checkmark is always second.
    • Temporary recurring entries (❗️✔️, ✖️✔️, ⭕️✔️) are unique entries made from a recurring entry: one on a day that passed, or one you changed for just that day. Changing a recurring entry on the calendar only changes that one day. Moving or deleting it leaves a $0.00 entry on its day, which keeps the recurring entry from coming back there.
    • If a recurring entry's End Date moves before a day you changed (or you delete the recurring entry), that day's temporary entry from today on becomes a plain unique entry, like ❗️, since there's nothing left for it to hold back. A $0.00 one just goes away.
    • Spreadsheet entries (➕❗️, ➕✖️, ➕⭕️) start with a yellow plus. They come from spreadsheets you connect on the Spreadsheets page in the menu (once your email is verified), land on today and every day after, and follow the spreadsheet when it changes. They work just like recurring entries: change one for its day and it becomes ❗️➕ (the plus second), which keeps your change, and once a day passes its spreadsheet entries stay as they were. The Spreadsheets page's Help has more.
    • A day has one entry per title, refraction type, and Other Account. So when New Entry's title, type, account, and day match an entry already there (a recurring or spreadsheet entry landing there too), it shows that entry and asks whether to add to it or set it to the new amount. Changing a recurring or spreadsheet entry that way makes it a temporary entry for that day, like ❗️✔️.

    Refraction Types

    Besides the checkmark (or plus), an entry's emoji is its refraction type (a recurring entry with only ✔️ is Regular):

    • ❗️ Regular: a normal cost or gain. It changes In Bank, and the tracker counts it.
    • ✖️ Hidden: money spent or received outside your budget's account, like spending cash. It's a cost or gain (the day's Gains and Costs show it, and the tracker counts it), but In Bank stays the same.
    • ⭕️ Transfer: money moving between your own accounts without really being gained or lost, like depositing cash. It changes In Bank, but it isn't a cost or gain: the day's Gains and Costs leave it out, and in the tracker, only its Transfers row (and rows set to count transfers) count it.
    • Auto, when you add an entry, uses the type of an entry with that title already on that day, or Regular if there isn't one.
    • In New Entry, the little ? next to Refraction Type shows these anytime.

    Other Accounts

    When you pick Hidden or Transfer for a new entry, you can also pick one of your Other Accounts, like Savings. The list shows each account's balance today. The entry keeps its own title, and the account shows after it, like "Payday → Savings".

    • A Transfer moves money between your budget's account and that one, so the account gets the opposite amount. Moving $100 into savings is a $100 Transfer cost: In Bank goes down $100, and Savings goes up $100. Taking $200 out is a $200 Transfer gain: In Bank goes up $200, and Savings goes down $200.
    • A Hidden entry happens in the account itself, so the account changes by the same amount, and In Bank stays the same. Putting $50 in cash into savings is a $50 Hidden gain (Savings goes up $50); paying $30 straight out of savings is a $30 Hidden cost (Savings goes down $30).
    • An account's balance starts from what you type for it, and its entries add to it from there. To put in what's already in an account (like the $3,000 already in your savings), tap it on Other Accounts (one of the tabs at the top) and type its balance. That doesn't add an entry, so it isn't counted as a gain. Money owed, like on a credit card, is a Negative balance.
    • Interest: turn on Add interest each month for an account and type its yearly rate (APR, like a credit card's). Once a month, on the day of the month you turned it on (or last typed its balance), its balance changes by a twelfth of that: a balance below $0 goes further below, and one above $0 grows.

    A Transfer for an account is still a transfer: it isn't a cost or gain, and only tracker rows set to count transfers count it. A Hidden entry for an account is a cost or gain like any Hidden entry, so the day's Gains and Costs show it, and the tracker counts it. Other Accounts (one of the tabs at the top) shows each account's balance, and at the top, the total of everything with In Bank.

    Lowest In Bank, Dave, And Upcoming

    • Lowest in bank after today is the lowest In Bank from today through the 28 days after the calendar.
    • Next to it, in the 28 days after the calendar (counting your recurring entries, connected spreadsheets' entries, and Upcoming entries, but not Hidden ones): Lowest is on the day In Bank is lowest in those 28 days, and how low it gets, and Day 28 is what In Bank will be on the 28th day. Amounts below $0 show in red, and when any of them is below $0, the whole Lowest card turns red.
    • Dave looks over your budget and lets you know what to keep in mind: days that go below $0, going below $0 in the 28 days after the calendar, uncommon recurring entries (ones that land every 3 months or less often) on the calendar, and Upcoming entries. Dave also has a new affirmation for you every day.
    • Dave's tips: the first time you open Home each day, Dave shows a tip for 10 minutes (his card glows, with a "Dave's Tip" label), and each visit after that shows the next one, until he's shown all of the day's tips. They're: on payday, about how much you tend to spend (besides your recurring bills) before the next paycheck; when the last 7 days' spending is well over a usual week's (both of these once you have 4 months of History); a big uncommon bill (or a one-time cost added for a later day) of $200 or more in the next 60 days, and how much to set aside from each paycheck until then; on Sundays, a reminder to check your In Bank against your bank and add what's missing (or a Catchup entry); once a week, when your Catchup entries add up to $100 or more in the last 30 days, a nudge to add purchases as they happen; and, right after a purchase that takes Lowest in bank after today below $0 (or further below), whether a refund is possible.
    • Upcoming lists entries dated after the calendar. Tap one to change, move, or delete it, like an entry on a zoomed day. Each one moves onto the calendar when its day shows up.

    Quick Entry

    The Quick Entry button (above the calendar, at the right, under Search) puts an icon on your phone's home screen that opens a New Entry form, with no login needed. The page it opens shows how to add it on your phone (iPhone, Samsung, Pixel, OnePlus, and more), and its Help shows how to open it from an iPhone's Lock Screen and check In Bank on an Apple Watch. Add it from your browser itself, not from a page opened inside another app. The icon can only add entries. Besides In Bank today and the lowest it gets (shown at its top), it never sees your budget, just your Other Accounts and their balances (so you can pick one) and your prisms. Its entries show up here the next time you open Prismal Budget.

    Prisms are entries you add often, saved on the Quick Entry page (Add Prism). Tapping one fills in New Entry with what it keeps (like a title, an amount, a type, or an account), and you fill in the rest. They're saved with your budget, so every icon has them. If your password changes, the icon stops working, and you can turn icons off anytime in Settings.

    Bring Over A Spreadsheet Budget

    For moving a Prismal Budget Google Sheet into this account. In Google Sheets, tap Share and set General access to Anyone with the link, then paste its link here. Its calendar, History, recurring entries, tracker, Upcoming, Change Logs (Form Entries), and paycheck calculator come over, replacing what this budget has.
