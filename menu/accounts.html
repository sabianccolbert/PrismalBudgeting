Equity Help

Equity keeps track of your money outside your budget's account, like savings, cash, investments, or a credit card. Balance is what's in each account today. In 6 Months is what it will have six months from today, counting what's still coming: entries on your calendar and in Upcoming, and the ones your recurring entries and connected spreadsheets will add. At the top, the total adds them all up with In Bank (your budget's own account), today and in 6 months.

Kinds Of Accounts

  • New Account (in Edit) starts by asking which kind it is: Car, Mortgage, Credit Card, Debit, or Other. Other has every box. The rest show tips on what each one is and how to fill it in, use its own words, show only the boxes it needs, and set up its interest the way that kind works. You can change an account's kind later from its window.
  • Got a Debt Calculator gem for it? Fill in from a gem, at the top, puts in what the gem keeps.
  • Once it's made, Add Recurring Entry? can set up its payment for you, with the amount you typed, on its next due day.
  • Car, Mortgage, and Credit Card are money owed: a balance paid past $0.00 earns no interest.

Moving Money In And Out

  • When you add a Hidden or Transfer entry (on the calendar, in Recurring Entries, or with the Quick Entry icon), pick the account under Equity Account (before the amount): tap its box for the list, where each account has its balance today under its name. The entry keeps its own title, and the account shows after it, like "Payday → Savings".
  • ⭕️ Transfer moves money between your budget's account and this one, so In Bank changes and the account gets the opposite amount. Moving $100 into savings is a $100 Transfer cost: In Bank goes down $100, and Savings goes up $100.
  • A ⭕️ Transfer can also come From another Equity account instead of In Bank (the box under Equity Account, In Bank to start): moving $100 from Savings into an HSA takes $100 out of Savings, puts it in the HSA, and leaves In Bank as it is. It shows like "Move ← Savings → HSA", and each account lists it in its entries.
  • ✖️ Hidden happens in the account itself, so In Bank stays the same and the account changes by the same amount. Putting $50 in cash into savings is a $50 Hidden gain (Savings goes up $50); paying $30 straight out of savings is a $30 Hidden cost (Savings goes down $30).
  • Only entries with the account picked under Equity Account count. An entry titled with an account's name, with no account picked, is an ordinary entry and doesn't change the account. Changing, moving, or deleting an entry for an account changes the account too.
  • % amounts: with an account picked, tap % next to the amount to make the entry a percent of the account's balance as its day starts, like paying 10% of a credit card's balance. It's worked out again whenever your budget changes (one % entry counts in the balance the next one starts from), and once its day passes it stays what it came to.
  • Entries for an account keep their type everywhere else: a ⭕️ Transfer is still a transfer (it isn't a cost or gain, and only a tracker row set to count transfers counts it), and a ✖️ Hidden one is a cost or gain like any Hidden entry (the day's Gains and Costs show it, and the tracker counts it).

Balances

  • Tap an account to type in what's in it today, like the $3,000 already in your savings. That doesn't add an entry, so your calendar, tracker, and Search stay the same. Money owed, like on a credit card, is a Negative balance: tap Negative before or after typing it.
  • From there, its entries add to it, so changing, moving, or deleting one changes the balance too. The panel also shows its latest entries and the ones coming up.
  • Tap an account, and under its name, balance, Max, interest, and escrow, Entries lists every entry made on it (on the calendar, in History, and in Upcoming), newest first, with what each put in or took out, in a box that scrolls (one with a note is underlined). Tap one to change it, like an entry on a zoomed day, and the list follows. Its balance follows too, unless you typed a new one (Save only sets the balance when you did).
  • Max (optional, right under the balance in the account's panel): the most it can hold, like a credit card's limit. Its balance then shows over it, like $500.00/$2000.00. A line sets the balance and Max apart from the interest under them, and another line the interest from the escrow.
  • If an account ever drifts from the real one, tap it and type in the real balance.

Interest

  • For a credit card, a loan, or savings that earn interest, tap the account, turn on Add interest, and type its yearly rate (its APR, like 24.99 or 6.125, decimals and all; for savings, its interest rate, not its APY, which already counts the compounding). The ? beside Interest says how to set it up for a credit card, a mortgage, a car or student loan, or savings.
  • Compounds is how often interest starts earning interest of its own: Daily (most credit cards and savings: each day earns a 365th of the rate on the balance and the interest so far), Monthly, Every 3 months, or Yearly (on its day of the month, that often: a twelfth of the rate for each month since), or Doesn't compound (only the balance earns it, never the interest added to it). Your statement or loan papers say which.
  • Added is how often it's added to the balance (every month, every 3 months, every 6 months, or every year), and On day the day of the month (a shorter month uses its last day), like a statement's closing day.
  • A balance below $0 goes further below, and one above $0 grows. Each time it's added shows in the account's entries as % Interest, and counts in its balance and In 6 Months.
  • Separate Interest (a switch under the interest's choices): off, interest is added to the balance, like a credit card's. On, it's a balance of its own, like a mortgage's: the panel shows Interest today right under the switch (type it in, or the balance; its ? says what it's for and how to use it), Equity shows it under the balance, only the balance earns interest, and a payment toward the account pays the interest first, then the balance. Everything that adds balances up (the totals, In 6 Months, Bob, Goals) counts the two together.
  • Typing a new balance (or interest balance), or changing the rate, any of its choices, or the escrow, starts the count again from that day, with the interest so far kept in the balance (or in the interest balance). The interest added next still counts from the last time it was added, the way a bank adds a month's interest at its end, so typing a balance partway through a month loses none of it, and neither does an account added partway through one. Turn the switch off to stop it. Interest set up before these choices keeps working as it did: monthly, on the day of the month it started.
  • To see when a balance owed is paid off, and what paying extra saves, open the Debt Calculator (in the Menu) and pick the account.

Escrow

  • For a mortgage (or another loan that collects for taxes and insurance), turn on Take out escrow, right above Entries, and type the escrow part of the monthly payment and the day the payment is due. Its ? says what escrow is.
  • Each month on that day, the escrow comes due. A payment toward the account (a Transfer or Hidden entry with it picked) pays the interest owed first (with Separate Interest), then the escrow due, and only what's left lowers the balance, so the balance follows just the principal. The escrow part leaves the account for the home's taxes and insurance, and shows in its entries as Escrow.
  • A payment short of the interest and escrow due pays what it can, in that order, and the escrow still due shows in the panel until a payment covers it. The amount usually changes once a year, after the lender's escrow review: change it here when it does.
  • A mortgage, all together: its rate, Compounds Monthly, added every month on the payment's due day, Separate Interest on, and its escrow.

Edit

  • Tap Edit to add an account (New Account), drag accounts by their ≡ bar, or check accounts and delete them. Every budget has Savings and Cash: they can be renamed, but not deleted (they show a 🔒 in Edit).
  • Savings is always there, so it can't be deleted (🔒), but you can rename it.
  • Tap an account to rename it. Its entries (and tracker aliases, connected spreadsheets and calendars set up with its name, and goals for it) get the new name too. If that would make two recurring entries the same (the same title, refraction type, and account), it says which, so you can change one first. Deleting an account leaves its entries on your calendar, but they stop changing an account.
