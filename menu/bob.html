Bob Help

Bob looks over your budget for you. His card on Home keeps to the basics (days below $0, uncommon recurring entries, Upcoming entries, and the day's affirmation), and tapping it opens this page, where he works out the rest. It's also in the Menu.

The Cards

  • Bob's Highlights, at the top, has a line from each card that's on. A line that needs a look is red-orange.
  • Data and Accounts are always on. Every other card has a switch at its right, and starts off. Turning one on or off is saved with your budget, so every device you use (and anyone you share it with) sees the same cards.
  • The little ? after each card's title says what the card is for.
  • Tapping a row with a ›, a button, or a stretch of the spectrum opens what's behind it in a window over the page.

Data

What Bob works everything out from: when your History starts, In Bank today and the lowest it gets, your Other Accounts today (each one's balance, Max, and interest), your goals, how many titles are on each wavelength, your recurring entries each month, your paydays (a payday is a Regular gain from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet), what you spent besides recurring bills in the last 7 days and in a usual week, what usually goes out between paychecks, your Catchup entries, big bills ahead, your purchases in the last 30 days, and the ones still to come before the lowest day. Tap a row with a › to see what's behind it. Payday and Spending Pace need 4 months of History.

Accounts

In Bank, each Other Account, and the Total, with what each holds now and what your budget says it will hold on the day in the box at the top of the third column (counting recurring entries, connected spreadsheets' entries, Upcoming, and interest). Pick any day from tomorrow to 10 years ahead; this device remembers it, and starts on 6 months ahead. When an account has interest, an Interest column shows what it gets (or, below $0, pays) from today through that day.

Goals

  • Tap New Goal to set one: what it's for (In Bank, the Total, or an Other Account), its amount (or a % of what that holds today, which stays that amount), and if you like, a title and a day to reach it by. A credit card's goal can be $0.00, for paid off.
  • Each goal shows what it's for now, how far along it is, and what Bob says: On track when your budget gets it there by itself; otherwise how much to set aside from each payday until its day (or, for In Bank and the Total, how much less to spend), counting a credit card's interest as it shrinks.
  • In danger means it's at risk: setting that much aside would take In Bank below $0, it's more than usually goes out between paychecks, no paycheck lands before its day, or its day passed. A goal with no day says when your budget gets it there, or what reaching it in a year would take.
  • Tap a goal to change or delete it.

Wavelength Planning

  • Your wavelengths are what your money is for. They start as Needs (like rent and groceries), Wants (like eating out), and Future (like savings, or paying down a card), but they're yours: tap Wavelengths to rename them, put them in order (the first is the longest wavelength, red, and the last the shortest, violet), take some out, or add more, from 1 to 10.
  • Once this card is on, every form that adds or changes an entry (New Entry, an entry's options, Recurring Entries, and the Quick Entry icon) asks at the bottom for its Wavelength, with a button for each of yours and None.
  • It's kept for the entry's title, so every entry with that title shares it (on every day, past and future). Turning the card off hides the question but keeps what each title has. Taking a wavelength out leaves its titles on none.
  • The spectrum adds up what each wavelength cost between the From and To days (it starts on the last 30 days), as one bar with a wave along it, long at the left and shorter toward the right, like light. Each wavelength's stretch is as wide as its share. Each title counts by what its entries come to together, so a refund takes off what it cost. Point at a stretch to see its amount, or tap it (or its name under the bar) to see its titles, biggest first, with each one's share.
  • What isn't on a wavelength yet shows under the spectrum: tap it to put those titles on one right there.

Bob's Tips

  • Payday: about how much usually goes out between paychecks besides your recurring bills, from your last 4 months, and on a payday, a heads-up to spend carefully.
  • Spending Pace: the last 7 days' spending besides recurring bills, next to a usual week. When it's $50 and 25% over, Bob says so.
  • Big Bills: bills of $200 or more in the next 60 days that don't come every month (like a yearly bill, or a one-time cost added for a later day), and how much to set aside from each paycheck until the next one. Never a bill that comes every month or more often, like rent.
  • Weekly Check: on Sundays, a reminder to compare In Bank with your bank, add what's missing, or tap that day's In Bank on Home and type what your bank says for a Catchup entry.
  • Catchup Entries: your Catchup entries from the last 30 days added up. At $100 or more, purchases are slipping by, so try adding each one as it happens.
  • Refunds: when Lowest in bank after today goes below $0, the purchases from the last 30 days (or still to come by that day) that a refund, or skipping one, would cover it with.
