Bob Help

Bob looks over your budget for you. His card on Home keeps to the basics (days below $0, uncommon recurring entries, Upcoming entries, and the day's affirmation), and tapping it opens this page, where he works out the rest. It's also in the Menu.

The Cards

  • Bob's Highlights, at the top, has a line from each card that's on. A line that needs a look is red-orange.
  • Bob's Cards has a row for each of his cards (Goals, Wavelength Planning, his tips, and Business, for a budget with Business Mode on), with what it says right now and whether it's On or Off. Tap a row to open that card in a window: its switch is there, it starts off, and turning one on or off is saved with your budget, so every device you use (and anyone you share it with) sees the same cards. What a card opens goes over its window, and closing that brings the card back.
  • Under it, the Spectrum Graph and Data's four cards fold open and shut: tap a title. A shut one shows its first figure by its title, and this browser remembers which are open. On a wide screen, Bob's Cards is at the left and these at the right.
  • The little ? after each card's title says what the card is for.
  • Warnings: a card that's on has a Warnings switch beside its own, at the card's top right in its window. With it on, when that card needs a look (its line in Bob's Highlights is red-orange), Bob says so on his card on Home, with See it › to open the card here, and his card moves up over the calendar. Okay there puts those warnings away, and his card back down, for the rest of the day, on that device (each device gets them once); one that says something new shows again.
  • In Bank And Equity has a list for what Lowest counts: In Bank (Default), or one of your Equity accounts (the same choice as on Home).
  • Tapping a row with a ›, a button, or a stretch of the spectrum opens what's behind it in a window over the page.

Spectrum Graph

One graph to compare anything on this page: In Bank, the Total, your Equity accounts, tracker rows (a whole row, or one of its aliases in the second list), each wavelength's costs week by week, and Bob's own figures, like purchases added up or what's spent each week. Pick a line and tap Add (up to 12); the × beside a line takes it off. It starts on In Bank, and the lines are kept with your budget, so every device shows the same ones.

Data

What Bob works everything out from, in three cards: In Bank And Equity (In Bank today with the lowest it gets, and your Equity accounts today, each one's balance, Max, and interest), Paydays And Spending (your paydays, where a payday is a Regular gain from a recurring entry or a connected spreadsheet, with what usually goes out between them; what you spent besides recurring bills in the last 7 days and in a usual week; your Purchases, the last 30 days' with the ones still to come before the lowest day and your Catchup entries, in one window; and when your History starts), and Bills And The Tracker (the tracker, with what your recurring entries come to each month, and big bills ahead). Money going out shows below $0, in red. Tap a row with a › to see what's behind it. Payday and Spending Pace need 4 months of History. Your goals' graph is under Goals (Goals Over Time), and each wavelength's week by week is under Wavelength Planning.

Tracker

The Tracker row (in Bills And The Tracker) opens the tracker: what each row came to from From through To (the last 3 months to start; pick any other days, and a day after today counts what's planned).

  • Each row counts the entries its aliases point to. An alias is an entry title, set to count All of its amounts, only its Costs, or only its Gains. If more than one row has the same alias, each of those rows counts it.
  • Tap a row to see what each of its aliases came to, with a line graph of the row's Total and each alias, added up day by day. Change Row there changes its title and aliases, or deletes it.
  • New Row adds a row. Edit moves rows (hold a row's ≡ and drag it), adds sections (+ Section), and deletes the rows you check.
  • The search box shows only the rows with what you type in their title or aliases, like "food" or "gas".
  • Rows leave out ⭕️ Transfers, since most transfers aren't real costs or gains. Turn on a row's "Count ⭕️ Transfers in this row" switch (in Change Row) for one that should count them (its title then starts with ⭕️). ✖️ Hidden entries always count.
  • Costs, Gains, Transfers, Interest, and Undefined fill in by themselves, so they can't be changed or deleted (the ? beside each says what it counts). Undefined lists the titles no row counts yet: add one as an alias to a row to move it out.
  • To see the entries behind a row, search for its title with the Tracker button in Home's Search.
  • Recurring Entries Each Month (at the bottom of the Tracker's window) lists each recurring entry, how often it lands, and what it comes to in a month, with the bills and gains added up.

Line Graphs

A row that can have one has a line graph at the bottom of its window. Start date is the first day it draws, and Show through starts on today; pick a later day to see what your budget expects, drawn dashed. Point at it (or tap it) to read a day, and See the numbers shows the same figures as a table.

Tap a name under it to change how that line is drawn, one step at a time: Separate (its own line), Red, Blue, then Hidden. Every line set to Red is added together into one thicker red line, and every line set to Blue into one blue line, so you can see a few as one, like Coffee, Coffees, and Drinks. All, after the ? at the start of the list, sets every line the same way at once; it turns grey while the lines are set differently (tap it then to set them all back to Separate).

Equity

The Equity row (in In Bank And Equity) opens In Bank, each Equity account, and the Total, with what each holds now (and the most it can hold), and what your budget says it will hold on the day in the box at the top of the third column (counting recurring entries, connected spreadsheets' and calendars' entries, Upcoming, and interest). Pick any day from tomorrow to 10 years ahead; this device remembers it, and starts on 6 months ahead. When an account has interest, an Interest column shows what it gets (or, below $0, pays) from today through that day, and a line under the account says how it's worked out. A car loan, mortgage, or credit card below $0 says the day it's paid off with everything planned, and the graph runs through that day. When a recurring payment into it keeps going after that (taking money from In Bank and putting the account above $0), a note says so, with what its last payment needs when that's less than the others, and End It Then sets that payment's End Date to the payoff day.

Goals

  • Tap New Goal to set one: what it's for (In Bank, the Total, or an Equity account), its amount (or a % of what that holds today, which stays that amount), and if you like, a title and a day to reach it by. A credit card's goal can be $0.00, for paid off.
  • Each goal shows what it's for now, how far along it is, and what Bob says: On track when your budget gets it there by itself; otherwise how much to set aside from each payday until its day (or, for In Bank and the Total, how much less to spend), counting a credit card's interest as it shrinks. It's helpful to put those payments in as a recurring entry, so your budget counts them.
  • In danger means it's at risk: setting that much aside would take In Bank below $0, it's more than usually goes out between paychecks, no paycheck lands before its day, or its day passed. A goal with no day says when your budget gets it there, or what reaching it in a year would take.
  • How You'll Get There (at the bottom of a goal's window): fill in any two of how often (every so many days, weeks, or months), how much each time, and By, and Bob works out the third (in grey slanted letters). With only one, how often starts as every month (and with only how often, By starts a year from now). Filling in a third clears the one you filled in first. Payments start tomorrow. Once one is worked out, Add Recurring (for a goal for an Equity account) opens a new recurring Transfer with all of it filled in: the title, the account, the amount, how often, the first day, and the last.
  • A goal can follow an Accrual Calculator gem (pick it in Gem, or tap Add To Goals in the calculator). A gem with In Bank or an Equity account sets what the goal is for and its amount, and when the gem changes, so does the goal. The goal also says when the gem's payments get it there.
  • Tap a goal to change or delete it.

Wavelength Planning

  • Your wavelengths are what your money is for. They start as Needs (like rent and groceries), Wants (like eating out), and Future (like savings, or paying down a card), but they're yours: tap Add Or Change Wavelengths to add your own, rename them, put them in order (the first is the longest wavelength, red, and the last the shortest, violet), take some out, or add more, from 1 to 10.
  • Once this card is on, every form that adds or changes an entry (New Entry, an entry's options, Recurring Entries, and the Quick Budget icon) asks at the bottom for its Wavelength, with a button for each of yours and Undefined (always there).
  • It's that entry's own (a recurring entry's is every day it lands on). Turn on Per Title under it (its ? explains it) to put the title on it instead: every entry with that title, on every day, past and future, except one given a wavelength of its own. Turning the card off hides the question but keeps what each entry and title has. Taking a wavelength out leaves its entries and titles on Undefined.
  • Goals: in Add Or Change Wavelengths, each wavelength (and Undefined) can have a goal: an amount (like 300) or a percent of your Regular gains (like 20%), per a number of days, weeks, or months. The card shows how much of each is used, and once one is 85% used, it needs a look (and with Warnings on, Bob says so on Home).
  • The spectrum adds up what each wavelength cost between the From and To days (it starts on the last 30 days), as one bar with a wave along it, long at the left and shorter toward the right, like light. Each wavelength's stretch is as wide as its share. Each title counts by what its entries come to together, so a refund takes off what it cost. Point at a stretch to see its amount, or tap it (or its name under the bar) to see its titles, biggest first, with each one's share.
  • What's Undefined shows under the spectrum: tap it for a list of those titles, with a button for each wavelength under each one, to put the title on one from there (Per Title).

Bob's Tips

  • Payday: about how much usually goes out between paychecks besides your recurring bills, from your last 4 months, and on a payday, a heads-up to spend carefully.
  • Spending Pace: the last 7 days' spending besides recurring bills, next to a usual week. When it's $50 and 25% over, Bob says so.
  • Big Bills: bills of $200 or more in the next 60 days that don't come every month (like a yearly bill, or a one-time cost added for a later day), and how much to set aside from each paycheck until the next one. Never a bill that comes every month or more often, like rent.
  • Weekly Check: on Sundays, a reminder to compare In Bank with your bank, add what's missing, or tap that day's In Bank on Home and type what your bank says for a Catchup entry.
  • Catchup Entries: your Catchup entries from the last 30 days added up. At $100 or more, purchases are slipping by, so try adding each one as it happens.
  • Refunds: when Lowest in bank after today goes below $0, the purchases from the last 30 days (or still to come by that day) that a refund, or skipping one, would cover it with.
