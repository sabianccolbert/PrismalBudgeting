Accrual Calculator Help

The Accrual Calculator works out how a balance changes with the same payment each time and its interest. Pick what it is at the top: Owed (a loan or a credit card) shows when it's paid off, how much interest it costs on the way, and what paying more each time saves. Saved (a savings account, or In Bank) shows what it grows to with deposits and interest, and when it reaches a goal amount.

The graph at the top shows the balance after each payment, as what you've typed comes to, with a second line for what paying extra does.

Its results change as you type, and what you typed is kept (a moment after you stop), so it's there the next time you open it, on any device. They're estimates: a lender's own figures can differ (its fees, how it counts days, and rounding).

The Balance

  • Account fills in what In Bank or one of your Equity accounts holds today and its interest, and picks Owed (below $0) or Saved. Pick None to type your own.
  • Goal amount (Saved) is what it should reach. Leave it blank to see what it grows to in 1 and 5 years.
  • Interest rate is the APR, a yearly percent. Leave it blank for none.
  • Compounds is how often interest starts earning interest of its own: daily (most credit cards), monthly, every 3 months, yearly, or not at all (only the balance earns it). Interest added and On day are when it's added to what's owed, like a statement's day.

Payments (Or Deposits)

  • Payment (or Deposit), every week, every 2 weeks, or every month, starting on the first one's day. The last one is only what's left.
  • Extra each payment (or deposit) shows what putting that much more in each time does: how much sooner it's done, and for what's owed, the interest saved.
  • Pay it off by (or Reach it by, with a goal amount) works out the payment it takes to be done by that day (up to 50 years ahead).
  • Each Payment (or Each Deposit) lists every one with the interest since the one before and the balance after it.

Add To Goals

Add To Goals, under the results, opens a new goal on Bob's page that follows these boxes as a gem: the gem you put in, or a new one it saves first (it asks for a name). The goal's day is your Pay it off by (or Reach it by) day. When the gem changes, so does the goal.

Gems

  • Gems are balances you look at often, like a car loan, a credit card's payoff, or savings for a trip, saved over the calculator so you don't have to type them each time. A gem keeps the boxes that are filled in when it's saved, and whether it's owed or saved (never the days). With In Bank or an Equity account picked, it keeps that, and the balance (and an account's interest) come from it each time.
  • After you tap a gem, change any box and tap Save Changes To it to keep the new boxes in that gem. A goal following it changes too.
  • Add Gem makes the calculator glow: fill in only what it should keep (what's already filled in counts), give it a name if you like, then tap Save Gem. Tap a gem to put what it keeps back in.
  • Edit Gems drags gems into order by their ≡, or deletes them with ×. They're saved with your budget, so every device has the same ones.

With In Bank Or An Equity Account

  • Its own pace comes from your budget: its entries, the ones still to come (like a recurring payment, Upcoming, or a connected spreadsheet's or calendar's), and an account's own interest. It says when it's back to $0 (Owed) or reaches the goal amount (Saved), looking up to 10 years ahead, and what the extra each time would change.
  • Its Entries (an Equity account's) lists what's coming up (on the calendar, in Upcoming, and from your recurring entries for the next year, with its interest) and what came before. Tap one to change it, like on a zoomed day.
