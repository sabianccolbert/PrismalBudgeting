Privacy Policy

Effective Date: November 24, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Sabian Colbert (“we”, “us”, “our”) collects, uses, and protects your information when you use the Prismal Planning (formerly Prismal Budget) website and services (the “Site”). If you do not agree with this Policy, please do not use the Site.

Prismal Planning is intended for people 18 and older. You must be 18 or older to create an account (see Section 7).

1) What We Collect

A) Information You Provide directly

When you create an account, we collect the username, password, email address, and birth date you provide, and we record when you agreed to this Privacy Policy and the Terms of Use. We ask your birth date only to make sure you're 18 or older; if you typed it wrong, you can fix it in Settings. We store your account details and your budget and finance data, for each budget you keep (such as your entries and the notes you add to them, recurring payments, goals, the wavelengths you name for what your money is for, which one each entry or title is on, and the goal you set for each, what you keep in the calculators, the pay settings of any calendar you connect, the order of your Calendar Fractals and the Holidays fractal's color, and, in a budget with Business Mode on, its owners' names and which business category each title is in, history, and change logs, which keep what each change did so it can be undone) in our Cloudflare SQL database. We never store your password itself, only a protected one-way form of it (see Section 5). You can sign in with your username or your email address (or another email of yours you add and confirm, see Section 1E). We use your email address to send you account emails, such as email verification links, password reset links, and security notices. When you create your account or change your email, we email you a link to verify that the address is yours, and we record when it's verified. Connecting a spreadsheet or a calendar requires a verified email (see Section 1E). To keep automated sign-ups out, the Create Account form uses Cloudflare Turnstile, which looks at information from your browser and device (not your budget or what you type) to check that you're a person; Cloudflare handles it under its own privacy policy, and we only receive whether the check passed.

B) Passkeys (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello)

Passkey sign-in is optional. If you turn it on, your device (or the password manager it uses, such as iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager, or Windows Hello) makes a passkey for the Site, and we store the passkey's public key and ID, the name you give it, the site address it was made on, when it was added and last used, whether your device reports it as synced, and a sign-in counter. We also store a random ID for your account that your devices keep with its passkeys, and whether you chose to turn passkey sign-in on or off (so we only ask you once). The private part of a passkey never leaves your device or password manager. Your face, fingerprint, or device PIN is checked only by your device: we never receive, see, or store any biometric data, only a yes from your device that the check passed. Your device or password manager keeps the passkey along with your username (so it can show which account it's for), and those companies handle it under their own privacy policies. Your browser also remembers the ID of the passkey it last signed in with (in its own storage, even after you sign out), so the login page can ask your device for that passkey right away; nothing else about you is kept with it.

C) Reminders, Notifications & Home Screen Icons

If you turn on reminders, we store your reminder time, your time zone, which kinds of reminders you want, which of your budgets they're about, which days that budget needs a reminder, and for each entry you set a reminder on, its title and day (so the reminder can name it). If you turn on push notifications, we store the push address your browser gives us for that device (provided by your browser's push service, such as Apple, Google, or Mozilla). If you turn on a calendar link, we store the private code in that link. If you set up a Quick Budget home screen icon, we store a protected form of its key and which budget it adds to, along with the entries you make with it until they're added to that budget (entries from an icon on another device wait on the budget's Home until someone who can open it adds or deletes them), any prisms (saved entries you add often) made on its page (kept with the budget, so everyone's icons for that budget have the same ones), and whether your icons show balances (a choice in Settings). If you make a Snapshot icon (a home screen icon that opens the Snapshot page with no login), we store a protected form of its key, which budget it shows, and when it was made and last used. Anyone who has the icon, or its link, can see that budget without signing in: its Snapshot, and what the Snapshot is worked out from (its entries, recurring entries, Upcoming entries, History, tracker, Equity accounts, goals, calculators, and its connected spreadsheets' and calendars' settings, like a calendar's pay), but not its Change Logs, its notes, your calendar, the links of its connected spreadsheets and calendars, or anyone's username. It can't change anything in the budget, so only make one on a device you keep locked, and never send its link to anyone. Each Quick Budget, Quick Calendar, and Snapshot icon can have a look you pick for it (its colors and its picture, with a small copy of any image you upload for it), which we store with that icon's key, and anyone with the icon can change it there.

D) Analytics Data

We use Google Analytics to understand how the Site is used. It may collect general device and browser information, approximate location (derived from IP address), and site usage events. Google Analytics never sees your budget, and the page addresses we send it leave out everything after the page's name, such as the private code in a password reset link. It doesn't load at all if your browser sends a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal, or if you turn analytics off for your device in Settings. Our infrastructure provider, Cloudflare, also counts page visits for us (Cloudflare Web Analytics); it doesn't use cookies or follow you across other websites. We don't show ads, and we don't use advertising services.

E) Connected Spreadsheets & Calendars

Connecting a spreadsheet is optional, and requires your email address to be verified. If you connect one on the Budgets, Calendars & Spreadsheets page, we store its link, the name you give it, the names of its tabs, which cells each part of an entry comes from (or what you typed in instead), and the entries it makes (their dates, titles, amounts, refraction types, and Equity accounts), along with any problems found reading it, like a cell that isn't a date. Each time Home or the Budgets, Calendars & Spreadsheets page reads your spreadsheets, our server downloads each one from where it's kept (Google, Microsoft, or Dropbox) and passes the file straight to your browser, which reads the cells you picked. We don't keep the file, or any other part of the spreadsheet. The host receives that request from our server, not from your device, so it doesn't get your IP address from it.

You share a spreadsheet with us in one of two ways. Anyone with the link: anyone who has the link can see the whole spreadsheet, so we suggest a separate spreadsheet with only the cells Prismal Planning needs. Shared with admin@prismalplanning.org (Google Sheets and Google Drive): our Google account can view the spreadsheet, and we read it only for the Prismal Planning account whose verified email, or another email it confirmed under Other Emails, matches the Google account that owns it or shared it, never for anyone else, and only the way described above. If you add an email under Other Emails in Settings (under Email), we store it and when it was confirmed, and email it a link to confirm it's yours, with your username so you can tell which account asked. Once it's confirmed, you can also sign in to your account with it (with your password), and use it for Forgot Password, which emails the reset link to it. Removing it there, or deleting your account, deletes it. You can remove our access at any time in the spreadsheet's Share settings. Deleting a connection on the Budgets, Calendars & Spreadsheets page deletes everything we stored about it, except the entries already on days that passed, which stay part of your budget.

Connecting a calendar of your work shifts is optional too, and also requires a verified email address. If you connect one, we store its link (anyone who has a calendar's link can see its events, so we keep it with your budget like the rest of your data, and Download My Data includes it), the name you give it, its paychecks' title, refraction type, and Equity account, the pay settings you enter (such as your pay rates, paydays, deductions, and tax settings, including what you enter from your Form W-4, like your filing status and amounts), and what each paycheck came to (its pay period, hours, pay, deductions, taxes, and take home), along with the entries they make and any problems found reading it. Each time Home or the Budgets, Calendars & Spreadsheets page reads your calendars, our server downloads each one from its link (from the calendar's provider, such as Apple, Google, Microsoft, or a work scheduling app) and passes it straight to your browser, which works out your pay from its events. We don't keep the calendar or its events; your browser keeps the shifts it found only while the page is open, to show them. The provider receives that request from our server, not from your device, so it doesn't get your IP address from it. Deleting a calendar on the Budgets, Calendars & Spreadsheets page deletes everything we stored about it, except the paychecks already on days that passed, which stay part of your budget.

F) Budgets You Keep And Share

You can keep more than one budget, and share any budget you own with other Prismal Planning accounts. For each budget, we store its name, which account owns it, and when it was made, and we remember which budget you opened last. To share one, you type the other account's username or email address (its own, or another email it confirmed under Other Emails): we tell you whether an account has it, and we store that you invited that account, what you typed, when it joined, and whether it can change the budget (its Can Edit switch: on unless you turn it off, and you can change it at any time in the budget's Share window or in Settings' Shared Users). Until it joins, the invitation shows in that account's budget list with the budget's name and your username, and nothing else of the budget. Once it joins, it can see everything in that budget (including the links and settings of the spreadsheets and calendars connected to it, such as a calendar's pay and tax settings) and, with Can Edit on, change everything in it, just as you can; with Can Edit off, it can only look. It sees your username and the usernames of everyone else who joined; you see its username. We don't show anyone your email address, your other budgets, or anything else of your account. Only a budget's owner can share it, rename it, delete it, turn its Business Mode on or off, or give it to someone who joined it. Giving a budget away makes that account its owner, with everything in it (including its Calendar Fractals), and the old owner stays in it like anyone it's shared with; an account's first budget always stays its own. Change Logs in a shared budget record which username made each change.

What anyone adds to a shared budget becomes part of that budget, which belongs to its owner (including the Change Logs rows with their username, and the budget's Calendar Fractals with their entries): it stays there when they leave or the owner stops sharing it, and it's deleted when the owner deletes the budget or their account. Leaving a budget, or the owner stopping sharing it with you, ends your access to it and stops your Quick Budget and Snapshot icons for it. Only share a budget with people you trust with everything in it.

G) Sign-Ins & Security Records

Each time you sign in, we keep a record of that sign-in (when it started and when it ends) so that signing out ends it on our side too. Each one is deleted when you sign out, when it ends after 24 hours, or when you change your password or sign out everywhere. To protect accounts and the Site from abuse, such as password guessing and spam, we also briefly keep counts of certain actions, like failed sign-ins. When a count is for a network, we store a scrambled form of the IP address it came from, never the address itself, and each count is deleted within about an hour.

H) Your Calendar

If you use the calendar, we store your Calendar Fractals (each one's name and color) and your calendar entries: each one's title, which Calendar Fractal it's in, its days and times (or that it's all day), the time zone of the device that saved it (so its alerts and calendar links use the right time, and a device in another time zone shows it at the right time there), its travel time, its location as you type it, how it repeats (with any days left out, and its last day), its alerts, and its note. Each Calendar Fractal is your own or a budget's (its Per Budget switch). Your own belong to your account: they show in every budget you open, and nobody else sees them unless you share one (see Sharing a Calendar Fractal, below). A budget's belong to that budget, like the rest of it (see Section 1F): everyone who joined the budget sees them and their entries (including locations and notes), can change or delete them when their Can Edit is on, and gets their entries' alerts; they're kept under the budget's owner, stay when the person who made them leaves, and are deleted with the budget. We also store which Calendar Fractal new entries start in for you (your default). A linked Calendar Fractal shows a calendar from somewhere else (its subscription link, or one of your connected calendars), and it's kept with the budget it's linked in, so the people you share that budget with see its entries and its link. Our server reads it the way it reads a connected calendar (see Section 1E), and your browser keeps what it read for up to 15 minutes. The Holidays fractal's days are worked out in your browser; we only store its color, with the budget. An entry's alerts are push notifications with its title, when it is, and its location, sent to the devices where you (and anyone who joined it or its Calendar Fractal, see below) turned on push notifications.

Sharing an entry: to share one, you type the other account's username or email address, or pick someone you share a budget with: we tell you whether an account has it, and we store that you invited that account, what you typed, and when it joined. Until it joins, the invitation shows in that account's Calendar Fractals list with the entry's title, its day and time, and your username. Once it joins, it sees the whole entry (including its location and note) and every change you make to it, and gets its alerts; you see its username. It can't change the entry, and it can take it off its calendar at any time. When you change just one day of a repeating entry you shared, that day stays shared with the same accounts. Stopping sharing an entry, or deleting it, takes it off their calendars.

Sharing a Calendar Fractal: you can share a whole Calendar Fractal of your own the same way (its Share window): we store that you invited that account, what you typed, when it joined, and whether it can change the fractal's entries (its Can Edit switch: off unless you turn it on). Until it joins, the invitation shows in that account's Calendar Fractals list with the fractal's name and your username. Once it joins, the fractal is on its calendar with all of its entries (including their locations and notes) and every change to them, and it gets their alerts; with Can Edit on, it can also add, change, and delete the fractal's entries, which stay yours like the rest of the fractal. You see its username, and it sees yours. It can leave at any time, and you can change its Can Edit or stop sharing the fractal with it in the fractal's Share window or in Settings' Shared Users; deleting the fractal takes it off their calendars.

Calendar links: if you make a calendar link in Settings (for every Calendar Fractal, or for one), we store the private code in it. Anyone who has a calendar link can see the entries it covers, with their titles, times, locations, and notes (the link for every Calendar Fractal has your own, those of every budget you can open, and the entries shared with you that you joined; a link stops showing a budget's Calendar Fractals once you can't open that budget), so only give it to calendar apps you use. Making a new link, or turning it off, stops the old one from working. A Calendar Fractal can also be one of your connected calendars (see Section 1E): then only your account (and, for a budget's Calendar Fractal, the people in that budget) can read it, and only through the Site.

Recipes, the Pantry, and meals: if you use the Recipes tab, we store, with the budget, its Pantry (each food's name, amount, unit, expiration day, and whether it's checked on the shopping list), its recipes (each one's title, foods with their amounts and units, and notes), the list of foods you use (each one's name, the unit you used it with last, and the other names you give it), and its meal entries (each one's title, day, how it repeats, foods, and note, or the recipe it follows), the Meals fractal's color, and the last day whose meals came out of the Pantry. They belong to the budget like the rest of it (see Section 1F): everyone who joined the budget sees them and can change them, and they're deleted with the budget.

Quick Calendar: if you set up a Quick Calendar home screen icon, we store a protected form of its key, when it was made and last used, which budget it was made in, and the entries and meals you add with it until they join your calendar (entries and meals from an icon on another device wait on Home until you add or delete them). The icon can see your calendar's entries (your own Calendar Fractals', those of the budget it was made in while you can open it, and the entries and Calendar Fractals shared with you that you joined), the calendars that budget links from elsewhere (our server reads them for the icon the way it reads them for you, and their links never reach the icon) and its Holidays, and that budget's meal entries, recipes, and list of foods (to fill a meal in from), and add new entries and meals, without signing in; it never sees the budget's money, its Pantry, or who your entries are shared with. Anyone holding a device with the icon can open it, so only make one on a device you keep locked.

2) Cookies & Browser Storage

Cookies: The Site itself doesn't set cookies. When Google Analytics is on, it sets its own cookies for this Site (named _ga and _ga_ followed by an ID) to count visits and tell a new visit from a returning one; turning analytics off in Settings, or a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal, keeps it from loading. Cloudflare may set a short-lived security cookie if it needs to check that a visit comes from a person, including the check on the Create Account form. We don't use advertising cookies. You can block or delete cookies in your browser's settings, and the Site works without them.

Storage in your browser: The Site keeps your sign-in (a signed token that stops working within 24 hours) and a few conveniences, like your Undo history, the budget you have open and the page to come back to after signing in again (both only in the open tab), when a budget first opens, whether to show the Getting Started card, what a linked Calendar Fractal last read (for up to 15 minutes), a heads-up about a limit for the page you go to next (only in the open tab), which of the cards on Bob's page you left open, which of the Recipes tab's cards you left open, and which Equity account Home's In Bank shows in each budget, and, in a home screen icon's app, the page it was left on and when (for 15 minutes), in your browser's storage on your device. Signing out removes them, except your device's own choices (whether analytics is off, the date format you picked, whether you turned the Site's music or sound effects off, the theme you picked (and a Custom theme's colors and choices), your Reduced Motion switches, the kind of phone you picked for the help's steps, the day you picked for Bob's Equity window, what Home's calendar shows (your budget, your calendar, or both), which Calendar Fractals are checked, whether a zoomed day lists its calendar entries, whether Home's calendar is in List Mode, whether Home should ask about notifications, the time format you picked, how far Lowest looks ahead on this device, the days each Wavelength Planning card adds up on this device (for each budget), the From and To each tracker card shows on this device (for each budget), which of Bob's warnings you okayed today on this device (for each budget, in a scrambled form), and which Change Logs rows this browser has seen, for each account and budget, in a scrambled form, so Home can count the changes made on other devices, and your Recipes settings: which of the Recipes tab's things glow, and whether meals come out of the Pantry on this device and since when), the ID of the passkey it last signed in with (see Section 1B), and a scrambled form of the Quick Budget and Quick Calendar icons it made or used (so their entries are added when you sign in there).

When you pick a new password, our server checks it against Have I Been Pwned's list of passwords exposed in data breaches. It sends only a small, scrambled piece of information that hundreds of different passwords share, so the service never receives your password, your username, your email address, or your IP address.

5) Security & Data Retention

We have reasonable administrative, technical, and organizational security measures in place to protect your account and your information from unauthorized access, loss, misuse, and alteration, and we review them as the Site changes. Your financial data and account details are stored with our infrastructure provider (Cloudflare) and sent over encrypted connections. Your password is never stored in plain text, and we keep only the public part of a passkey, which can't be used to sign in. Account emails tell you when your password or email changes or a passkey is added, and in Settings you can sign out every device at once. Changing your password in Settings signs out your other devices and stops your Quick Budget, Quick Calendar, and Snapshot icons; resetting it with an emailed link signs you out on every device, stops your Quick Budget, Quick Calendar, and Snapshot icons, and turns passkey sign-in off until you turn it back on in Settings. To keep these protections effective, we don't publish the details of how they work. No method of transmission or storage is 100% secure, so we can't guarantee absolute security. We retain your data as long as your account is active or as needed for legitimate business purposes. When you delete your account, we delete it and all of its data from our database right away, including the budgets you own, for everyone you shared them with (what you added to budgets other accounts shared with you stays in those budgets, which are theirs), and your calendar (your own Calendar Fractals, and those of the budgets you own; the entries you shared leave everyone's calendars), though copies may remain in our database provider's backups for up to 30 days.

6) Your Choices

• Account Data: You may update or delete your budget data on the Site. Your Change Logs keep what each of your recent changes did, including an entry or a note you deleted, so the change can be undone, until newer changes take its place. In Settings, you can change your email address (and verify it), add or remove other emails of yours, change your password, fix your birth date if you typed it wrong, or delete your account and all of its data at any time. Under Budgets (on the Budgets, Calendars & Spreadsheets page), you can stop sharing a budget of yours with anyone, delete a budget of yours (other than your first), or leave a budget someone shared with you.
• Calendar: On Home, you can change or delete your calendar entries, stop sharing one with anyone, or take an entry shared with you off your calendar. In a Calendar Fractal's Share window, or in Settings' Shared Users, you can stop sharing a fractal (or a budget) with someone, or change their Can Edit, and you can leave a fractal shared with you. On the Budgets, Calendars & Spreadsheets page, you can rename or recolor a Calendar Fractal, make it the budget's or your own (Per Budget), or delete it with its entries. In Settings, you can turn a calendar link off or make a new one, and turn off your Quick Calendar icons (which deletes the entries still waiting from them).
• Connected Spreadsheets & Calendars: On the Budgets, Calendars & Spreadsheets page (in the menu), you can turn each connected spreadsheet or calendar off or delete it at any time. To stop us from reading a spreadsheet at all, turn off its link sharing, or remove admin@prismalplanning.org, in the spreadsheet's Share settings; for a calendar, stop sharing it (or make a new link) in your calendar app.
• Download Your Data: In Settings, Download My Data gives you a copy of everything stored for your account (your budgets and who they're shared with, your calendar and who its entries and Calendar Fractals are shared with, settings, and account details) as a JSON file, at any time. Budgets other accounts shared with you belong to them, so the file only names them. Passwords, keys, and private link codes are left out. Upload A Backup, next to it, brings a budget back from such a file into the budget you have open: the file is read on your device, and what it brings back is saved like any other change to your budget.
• Analytics: In Settings, you can turn Google Analytics off (or back on) for your device. Browsers that send a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal never load it.
• Signed-In Devices: In Settings, you can sign out every device at once, turn off your Quick Budget icons (or keep them from showing any balances), turn off your Quick Calendar icons, or turn off your Snapshot icons.
• Passkeys: In Settings, you can turn passkey sign-in on or off at any time, add passkeys, or remove them. Removing a passkey deletes it from our database (you can also delete it from your device or password manager), and deleting your account deletes all of its passkeys.
• Reminders: You can turn push notifications and the reminders' calendar link on or off in Settings.
• Cookies: You can block or delete cookies in your browser's settings (see Section 2).
• Quick Budget: A Quick Budget icon can only add entries to your budget, and change or delete the ones it added until they join your budget. It can't see your budget, only those entries, what's in your bank account today and the lowest it's expected to get (with that day), your Equity accounts with their balances and each one's Max (so you can pick one), whether your budget asks each entry's wavelength, and your wavelengths' names (not which titles are on which), and the budget's prisms (saved entries you add often, made on its page or on another Quick Budget icon's page for that budget), which it can also change or delete. With Show balances on Quick Budget icons turned off in Settings, it gets no balances at all, just your Equity accounts' names. To stop all of your Quick Budget icons from working, turn them off in Settings or change your password.
• Snapshot: A Snapshot icon can only show the budget it was made in. To stop all of your Snapshot icons from working, turn them off in Settings, change your password, or sign out every device; deleting a budget, or leaving one shared with you, stops its icons too.
